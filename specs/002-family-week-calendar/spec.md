# Feature Specification: Family Week Calendar

**Feature Branch**: `002-family-week-calendar`
**Created**: 2026-09-02
**Status**: Draft
**Input**: Phase 2 of the `/family` Skylight Calendar clone — the Week view. A seven-day time grid on the wall tablet, timed and all-day events owned by Profiles and Labels, simple repeats with per-occurrence edits, drag to move and resize, and the same week reduced to a three-day slice on a phone. Phase 1 (household access, punch-in PINs, the shell, Profiles & Labels, tokens, PWA) is shipped and is the foundation this stacks on.

**Authoritative sources**: `docs/research/skylight/00-master-map.md` (§1 scope, §3 design system, §4.2 events, §5.1 calendar, §7 cross-cutting, §11 divergence ledger, §12 unknowns) and the eight source-tagged dossiers beside it, principally `01-calendar-tab-and-events.md`, `06-api-and-data-model.md`, `07-visual-design-system.md`, `08-ux-behaviors-and-reviews.md`. The operator's Phase 2 scoping decisions were settled on **2026-09-02** and are final for this specification.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-09-02

- Q: What timezone is the household in? → A: **America/Chicago** (US Central). Recorded in FR-284/Assumption 41; the seed writes it.
- Q: Where does the hour grid's vertical scroll sit when the calendar opens or idles? → A: **It follows the now line** — current time held about a third from the top while untouched; a manual scroll pauses following until the next day rollover or a Today tap. FR-290/Assumption 42.
- Q: The form is described as clock times on a date, but an event may cross midnight — how is that entered? → A: **The end has its own date**, defaulting to the start's; validation refuses end ≤ start as instants. FR-222/FR-226/Assumption 43.
- Q: On the phone, where may a paging swipe start, given any movement on a block begins a drag? → A: **Blocks always drag**; paging swipes start on empty grid, the hour ruler or the day-header band, and the arrows and Today always page. FR-279/Assumption 44.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

Example household used throughout, so the scenarios can be run by hand: profiles **Ana** (parent), **Ben** (parent) and **Cleo** (child), plus a Label **Bin day**. Week starts on Sunday.

### User Story 1 — The family reads its week on the wall (Priority: P1)

The tablet sits on the kitchen counter with nobody touching it. It shows this week as a grid of day columns over an hour ruler: Cleo's swim lesson as a block in her colour on Wednesday afternoon, the camping trip as one bar across Friday to Sunday in the band above the hours, an orange line across today's column at the current time, today's date in an orange circle. A minute later the line has moved. At midnight the day marker moves on its own, and on Saturday night the whole grid rolls into next week — nobody has touched it.

**Why this priority**: Reading is what the wall calendar does all day, and it is the only part of the feature a person uses without identifying themselves. Everything else in Phase 2 writes into this grid, so the grid must be correct before anything may change it. A mis-drawn event or a week that goes stale overnight is a wrong calendar, which is worse than no calendar.

**Independent Test**: Seed one week with a timed event, a multi-day all-day event, an overlapping pair, a multi-profile event, a label-only event and an event with no categories. Load `/family/calendar` on a tablet-landscape viewport with nobody punched in. Check each block's placement against its stored times by hand. Leave it open across a minute boundary and across midnight.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an event "Swim lesson" for Cleo on Wednesday 2026-09-16, 16:30–17:15, **When** the calendar opens on that week, **Then** a block titled "Swim lesson" sits in Wednesday's column with its top edge at 16:30 and its bottom edge at 17:15, filled with Cleo's colour.
2. **Given** an all-day event "Camping trip" from Friday 2026-09-18 to Sunday 2026-09-20, **When** the week renders, **Then** it appears once as a single bar in the band above the hour grid, spanning Friday, Saturday and Sunday — not as three separate pills.
3. **Given** the current time is 14:07 on Thursday, **When** the grid renders, **Then** a 2-pixel orange bar crosses Thursday's column only at 14:07, with a dot at the hour-gutter edge, drawn over the top of any event block it crosses.
4. **Given** the calendar is open and untouched, **When** one minute passes, **Then** the current-time bar has moved down by one minute's worth of grid, with no reload and no interaction.
5. **Given** the calendar is open at 23:59 on Saturday, **When** the clock passes midnight, **Then** the grid shows the new week, the orange circle has moved to the new day, and the current-time bar is at the top of the new day's column — with no reload.
6. **Given** "Dentist" 09:00–10:00 for Ana and "Standup" 09:30–09:45 for Ben on the same Tuesday, **When** they render, **Then** both are visible side by side inside Tuesday's column and each can be tapped separately; neither is hidden behind the other.
7. **Given** an event assigned to both Ana and Ben, **When** it renders, **Then** the block carries both of their colours in the striped treatment and its title remains readable.
8. **Given** an event assigned to no profile and no label, **When** it renders, **Then** it appears with a neutral white fill and a thin border, is readable, and can be tapped.
9. **Given** an event assigned only to the Label "Bin day", **When** it renders, **Then** it uses that label's colour in exactly the same way a profile-coloured event does.
10. **Given** nobody has punched in, **When** a person walks past and reads the week, **Then** the entire week is visible and no PIN is requested at any point.
11. **Given** today is Wednesday and the household's week starts on Sunday, **When** the week renders, **Then** the leftmost column is Sunday and Wednesday carries the today marker.
12. **Given** Saturday and Sunday columns and an event that finished two hours ago, **When** the week renders, **Then** the weekend columns are shaded and the finished event is dimmed relative to upcoming ones.
13. **Given** twelve events on Thursday, **When** the column renders, **Then** all twelve are reachable by scrolling within that day and none is silently dropped.
14. **Given** five events all running 09:00–10:00 on the same Tuesday, **When** the column renders at tablet-landscape width, **Then** three are drawn side by side and the remaining two are reachable through a "+2 more" control in that time band; none is drawn so narrow as to be unreadable and none is unreachable.
15. **Given** the calendar untouched at 20:00, **When** it is glanced at, **Then** the hour grid is scrolled so the now line sits about a third from the top — the evening hours, not the morning — and **Given** someone scrolls to the morning and walks away, **Then** the grid stays where they left it until midnight or a Today tap, when it resumes following.

### User Story 2 — Anyone who punches in can keep the week true (Priority: P2)

Ana taps the create control. The app asks "Who's here?", she taps her face and types her PIN, and a form opens: title, times, who it's for, whether it repeats. She adds "Piano" every Tuesday at 17:00 for Cleo, ending in December. Two weeks later term shifts, so she opens one Tuesday's occurrence, changes the time and picks "This and future events". Later still the class is cancelled for one week only, so she opens that occurrence, deletes it, confirms, and it is gone — that Tuesday only, and for good.

**Why this priority**: A calendar the household cannot correct stops being trusted within a week. This is the whole write surface — creation, the repeat model, the three scopes, deletion — and it is P2 only because it writes into the grid Story 1 draws.

**Independent Test**: Seed a household with three profiles, a label and an empty week. Create a one-off timed event, an all-day event and a weekly repeat through the form. Edit and delete an occurrence at each of the three scopes and compare the occurrence set before and after. Attempt each write with nobody punched in and confirm it is refused, including when issued directly rather than through the interface.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** nobody is punched in, **When** someone activates the create control, **Then** the "Who's here?" prompt appears before any event is written, and dismissing it writes nothing.
2. **Given** Ana is punched in, **When** she creates "Piano", Tuesday 2026-09-15, 17:00–17:45, assigned to Cleo, **Then** the block appears in Tuesday's column in Cleo's colour, and the stored event records Ana — not Cleo, and not the device — as its creator.
3. **Given** the create form, **When** "All day" is switched on, **Then** the start and end *time* controls are replaced by start and end *dates*, and the saved event renders in the all-day band rather than the hour grid.
4. **Given** the create form with repeat set to "Every week" and Tuesday and Thursday both chosen, ending 2026-12-17, **When** it is saved, **Then** an occurrence appears on every Tuesday and Thursday from the start date through 2026-12-17 inclusive, and none appears on 2026-12-22.
5. **Given** repeat set to "Every month on the date" with a start of 2026-09-15, **When** it is saved, **Then** occurrences appear on 15 October, 15 November and 15 December.
6. **Given** the create form untouched, **When** it is saved, **Then** the repeat is "Never" and exactly one occurrence exists.
7. **Given** the weekly "Piano" series, **When** a punched-in person opens the occurrence of Tuesday 2026-10-06, changes the title to "Piano recital" and chooses **This event**, **Then** only 6 October shows the new title and every other Tuesday still reads "Piano".
8. **Given** the same series, **When** they change the time to 18:00 on the occurrence of 2026-10-06 and choose **This and future events**, **Then** 6 October and every later Tuesday read 18:00, and 15, 22 and 29 September still read 17:00.
9. **Given** the same series, **When** they change the assigned profile on any occurrence and choose **All events**, **Then** every occurrence, past and future, carries the new profile.
10. **Given** the same series, **When** they delete the occurrence of 2026-10-13 with **This event** and confirm, **Then** 13 October disappears and 6 and 20 October remain.
11. **Given** any delete, **When** it is requested, **Then** a confirmation is shown first; **and** once confirmed there is no undo control anywhere in the calendar.
12. **Given** a non-repeating event, **When** it is edited or deleted, **Then** no scope question is asked at all.
13. **Given** Cleo is punched in, **When** she creates an event for herself, **Then** it succeeds — event writes are open to any punched-in profile.
14. **Given** the create form with an empty title, **When** save is attempted, **Then** it is refused with a message against the title field, the other entries are preserved, and nothing is stored.
15. **Given** a timed event whose end is set before its start, **When** save is attempted, **Then** it is refused and nothing is stored.
16. **Given** the household's timezone is `America/Chicago` and an event created on a device set to `Europe/London`, **When** the week is read on either device, **Then** both show that event at the same household time; **and** a weekly repeat created that way keeps its household wall-clock time across the *household's* daylight-saving dates, not London's.
17. **Given** the location field, **When** an address is typed, **Then** it is stored and displayed as text only — no map, no forecast, no lookup.
18. **Given** the weekly "Piano" series, **When** a punched-in person opens one occurrence and changes which Profiles and Labels it carries, **Then** the scope question offers only **This and future events** and **All events** — **This event** is not offered, so one occurrence can never end up a different colour from the rest of its segment.
19. **Given** the weekly "Piano" series and a per-occurrence time change already saved on 2026-10-06, **When** they delete the occurrence of 2026-10-06 with **This and future events** and confirm, **Then** 6 October and every later Tuesday are gone along with that per-occurrence change, and 15, 22 and 29 September still read 17:00.

### User Story 3 — Move it with a finger (Priority: P3)

Swim moves from Wednesday to Thursday. Ben presses the block, drags it across into Thursday's column at 17:00 and lets go. Because it is a repeating event, the app asks whether this is just this week, this week onwards, or every week. He taps "This event", the punch-in sheet appears, he types his PIN, and the block settles on Thursday — that week only. Later he catches the bottom edge of Saturday's dentist appointment and pulls it down half an hour.

**Why this priority**: Dragging is the fastest correction on a touch screen and the affordance the household will use most, but every change it makes can already be made through the edit form from Story 2 — so Story 2 must land first. It is also the phase's only invented gesture, which makes it the one most worth shipping last and testing hardest.

**Independent Test**: Seed a week holding a one-off timed block, an all-day pill and one weekly repeat. Move a block within a day, move one across days, resize by each edge, drag a timed block into the all-day band and an all-day pill back into the grid. Drag one occurrence of the repeat and confirm the scope prompt appears before the punch-in sheet. Release a drag on the top bar and confirm nothing is written.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** "Grocery run" on Saturday 10:00–11:00 and nobody punched in, **When** someone presses the block and drags it to Saturday 15:00 and releases, **Then** the punch-in prompt appears; **and** only after a correct PIN does the block settle at 15:00–16:00, keeping its one-hour length.
2. **Given** that punch-in prompt, **When** it is dismissed or the PIN is wrong, **Then** the block returns to 10:00–11:00 and nothing is stored.
3. **Given** "Swim lesson" on Wednesday 16:30–17:15, **When** it is dragged into Friday's column at the same height and dropped, **Then** it reads Friday 16:30–17:15 — the day changed, the duration did not.
4. **Given** a drag released at a position reading 09:07, **When** it commits, **Then** the event starts at 09:00; released at 09:23 it starts at 09:30 — every drop lands on a 15-minute boundary.
5. **Given** "Dentist" 09:00–10:00, **When** its bottom edge is dragged down to 10:30, **Then** it reads 09:00–10:30; **and** when its top edge is dragged up to 08:30 it reads 08:30–10:30.
6. **Given** "Dentist" 09:00–10:00, **When** its bottom edge is dragged above 09:15, **Then** the event stops at 09:15 — the minimum length is one 15-minute step and the block never inverts.
7. **Given** a timed block, **When** it is dragged up into the all-day band and dropped on Thursday, **Then** it becomes an all-day event on Thursday and its clock times are discarded.
8. **Given** an all-day pill, **When** it is dragged into the grid and dropped at Thursday 13:00, **Then** it becomes a timed event on Thursday 13:00–14:00.
9. **Given** one occurrence of the weekly "Piano" series and nobody punched in, **When** it is dragged and dropped, **Then** the scope question appears first — the same three options in the same wording as an edit — and the punch-in sheet only after a scope is chosen; nothing is written before both are answered.
10. **Given** that scope question, **When** "This event" is chosen, **Then** only the dragged occurrence moves and the series rule is unchanged, so the following week is back at its usual time.
11. **Given** a drag in progress, **When** the block is released over the top bar, the navigation rail, or anywhere that is not a slot in the grid or the all-day band, **Then** the block returns to where it started, nothing is written, and no punch-in is requested.

### User Story 4 — The same week in a pocket (Priority: P4)

Ana is at work. She opens `/family` on her phone and sees three days of the same week, today among them. She swipes left to look at the weekend, right to come back. She opens the filter sheet, hides Ben and hides the "Bin day" label, and is left with Cleo's afternoon. When she gets home the tablet still shows everything.

**Why this priority**: The wall tablet is the product; the phone is a read from elsewhere and an occasional correction. It reuses the same grid and the same writes, so it can arrive last, and it must not be allowed to compromise the tablet layout.

**Independent Test**: Seed a week with events on two profiles and on a label, in a household whose week starts on Sunday. Load at 390×844 and confirm three columns and no horizontal page scroll; load at 1180×820 landscape and confirm seven. Swipe in each direction and confirm which days arrive and that every slice stays inside one Sunday-to-Saturday week. Hide a profile and a label, reload, and confirm the hidden set survived on that device and did not change the tablet.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a 390×844 phone viewport and a household week starting Sunday, **When** the calendar opens on the week containing today, **Then** three day columns are shown, they are three consecutive days of that one week, today is one of them, and the page itself does not scroll sideways.
2. **Given** a 1180×820 tablet in landscape, **When** the calendar opens, **Then** seven day columns are shown — the whole anchored week in one slice.
3. **Given** the phone showing Sunday, Monday and Tuesday of a Sunday-start week, **When** the person swipes left, **Then** Wednesday, Thursday and Friday of the same week are shown — swiping left moves later.
4. **Given** the phone showing Wednesday, Thursday and Friday, **When** the person swipes right, **Then** Sunday, Monday and Tuesday of the same week are shown — swiping right moves earlier.
5. **Given** the phone showing Thursday, Friday and Saturday — the last slice of a Sunday-start week, pulled back to end on Saturday — **When** the person swipes left, **Then** Sunday, Monday and Tuesday of the *following* week are shown; no slice ever straddles two weeks.
6. **Given** the phone showing a week three weeks ahead, **When** the person taps Today, **Then** the slice of the current week that contains today is shown.
7. **Given** the filter sheet, **When** Cleo is hidden, **Then** every event assigned only to Cleo disappears from the grid; **and** an event assigned to both Cleo and Ana remains, because Ana is still visible.
8. **Given** the filter sheet, **When** the label "Bin day" is hidden, **Then** its events disappear — Labels carry the same show/hide toggle Profiles do.
9. **Given** several hidden categories, **When** the person taps show-all, **Then** every event returns.
10. **Given** a hidden profile on the phone, **When** the phone is reloaded, **Then** that profile is still hidden on the phone and is still shown on the tablet; **and** the event it was hidden from is still stored, unchanged, and reappears when the profile is shown again.
11. **Given** the phone, **When** an event is created or dragged there, **Then** it behaves exactly as on the tablet, and every control it needs is at least 44×44 points.

### Edge Cases

- **An event crosses midnight** — "Party" Friday 22:00 to Saturday 01:00 draws as one event with a segment at the bottom of Friday and a segment at the top of Saturday, each labelled. Editing or dragging either segment edits the one event.
- **A multi-day event crosses the edge of the displayed window** — the bar is clipped at the edge of the slice on show, keeps its title on the visible portion, and continues on the neighbouring slice or week rather than being drawn twice or dropped.
- **Three or more events overlap in one column** — they share the column's width side by side up to the cap in FR-285: three where the day column is at least 180 points wide, two below that. Every further overlapping event in that time band collapses into a tappable "+n more" that lists them, so nothing is hidden without a way to reach it. Scrolling the day column does not enter into it — scrolling is the answer to a day holding too many events (FR-216), not to one time band holding too many at once.
- **Several all-day events on one day** — the band grows or scrolls; no all-day event is unreachable.
- **Spring-forward gap** — a daily event at 02:30, on the date the household's clock jumps 02:00 → 03:00, renders exactly once, at the first valid time on that date. It is neither skipped nor duplicated.
- **Fall-back repeat** — a daily event at 01:30, on the date the household's clock repeats 01:00–02:00, renders exactly once, at the first of the two instants. An event that *runs through* the repeated hour occupies the extra hour of grid, which is the true wall-clock elapsed time.
- **A category on an event is deleted** — the event survives. It loses that colour, and if it had no other category it renders neutrally. The delete confirmation says how many events will be affected.
- **An exception whose series is deleted** — deleting the series with "All events" removes its per-occurrence edits and skips with it. No occurrence outlives its event, and a previously skipped date does not reappear as a ghost.
- **A delete at "this and future"** — the series ends immediately before the chosen occurrence. Every occurrence on or after that date goes, and so does every per-occurrence edit or skip dated on or after it; earlier occurrences and their per-occurrence edits are untouched. On the first occurrence it deletes the whole series (FR-286).
- **"This and future" on the first occurrence** — treated as "All events": the series changes in place, no split happens, and no empty leading segment is created.
- **"All events" after an earlier split** — a series split by a previous "this and future" change is two series from then on, and "All events" reaches only the segment the chosen occurrence belongs to. This is stated so the household is not surprised by it.
- **A drag dropped on an invalid target** — nothing changes, nothing is written, no punch-in is requested.
- **A drag whose punch-in is never satisfied** — same outcome: the block returns and nothing is written. An idle punch-out that expires mid-drag means the PIN is asked for again at the drop.
- **The device is offline when a change is committed** — the change is refused with a clear message (FR-288). Nothing is shown as saved that is not saved, and no write is queued optimistically.
- **Two people change the same event at once** — the later write wins, and the other device shows the newer version within seconds. Nothing is merged and no conflict dialog appears.
- **A device edits an event another device has just deleted** — the save is refused with a message saying the event no longer exists, and the form closes rather than recreating it (FR-288).
- **The view rolls over at midnight while a form is open** — the open form keeps the date the person chose; only the grid behind it advances.
- **A 15-minute event** — drawn at the minimum height of FR-218 (one line of title type plus padding, never below the 44-point touch floor), which may be taller than its true duration. Its stored times are unchanged and its details show the true times.
- **An occurrence dragged into the following week** — it is still shown, in its new place, after a reload from either week.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Week grid and rendering**

- **FR-201**: The system MUST present the calendar as one column per day over an hour-ruled time grid. `[V](36625171368987 — Week view is a seven-day grid)`; the number of columns actually rendered flexes with the viewport per FR-277 and FR-278 `[OURS 2026-09-02 #7]`.
- **FR-202**: The system MUST ship exactly one calendar view — Week — and MUST NOT offer a switcher to Day, Month or Schedule. `[OURS 2026-09-02 #1]`
- **FR-203**: The system MUST start the week on the day held in the household's existing start-of-week preference, defaulting to Sunday when none is set. The setting is `[V](36835449004315 — "Start Week On": Sunday / Monday)` and is already stored `[P1]`; the default is `[OURS 2026-09-02 #16]`.
- **FR-204**: The system MUST position and size a timed event block from its start and end times alone, measured against the hour ruler. `[V-photo]` (blocks are placed purely by time).
- **FR-205**: The system MUST render overlapping timed events side by side within their day column, up to the cap in FR-285, so every one of them is visible and separately selectable. `[V-photo]` — the photographed grid shows overlapping blocks shrinking in width and sitting side by side rather than stacking — corroborated for **Schedule** by `[V](360033104791 — "displays event lengths and overlaps")`.
- **FR-285**: The system MUST cap how many events are drawn side by side in one time band — **three** where the day column is at least 180 points wide, **two** below that — and MUST collapse every further overlapping event in that band into a tappable **"+n more"** control that lists them, so no event is hidden without a way to reach it. `[?]` — no source shows a three-way split, and the sources contain no non-reference layout at all; resolved `[OURS 2026-09-02]` (Assumption 36).
- **FR-206**: The system MUST render all-day and multi-day events as a single bar in a band above the hour grid, spanning every day the event covers, rather than repeating the event in each day. One spanning bar rather than a per-day repeat is `[V](36625171368987)` **for Schedule and Month**; the Week view's treatment of a multi-day or all-day event — a banner row versus a repeat in each cell — is `[?]` in every document (`01` open questions #12 and #13) and is resolved the same way here `[OURS 2026-09-02]` (Assumption 40). That there is a band above the hours at all is `[V-photo]` — an all-day pill is photographed inside the day-header band — and `[?]` in every document.
- **FR-207**: The system MUST keep every all-day event on a day reachable, growing or scrolling the band when more exist than its default height shows. `[?]` — no source shows a second all-day event; resolved `[OURS 2026-09-02 #15]`.
- **FR-208**: The system MUST show a current-time indicator: a 2-pixel orange bar (`#F66951`) across today's column only, with a ~14-pixel dot at the hour-gutter edge, drawn above event blocks; and MUST reposition it at least once a minute with no reload and no interaction. Existence and colour `[V](36625171368987 — "The current time in the calendar is displayed as an orange bar")`; geometry `[V-photo]`/`[ESTIMATED]`; refresh cadence `[OURS 2026-09-02 #12]`.
- **FR-209**: The system MUST mark today's date in the day header with a filled orange circle carrying the date numeral. `[V](36625171368987 — "The current date in the calendar is displayed in an orange dot")`; the ~44-point size is `[ESTIMATED]`.
- **FR-210**: The system MUST roll an open view over live at midnight — today's marker, the current-time indicator, and, at a week boundary, the displayed week — without a reload or an interaction. The displayed week MUST advance only while the week being displayed is the current one, so a person who has navigated to another week is not pulled away from it. `[?]` — no source describes midnight behaviour; resolved `[OURS 2026-09-02 #12]`.
- **FR-211**: The system MUST colour every event from the categories assigned to it, using the identical mechanism for Labels and Profiles. `[V](44740240234139 — "anything connected to a Profile or Label … will automatically show in that color")`
- **FR-212**: The system MUST render an event carrying more than one category with all of those colours in a striped treatment, with the title on a solid segment so it stays legible. That multi-colour rendering exists is `[V](36835449004315 — "Color Code Multi-Profile Events")`; the 45°, ~40-point stripe geometry is `[V-photo]`/`[ESTIMATED]` only — no Skylight document ever says "diagonal" — and it is always on in this phase `[OURS 2026-09-02 #16, #17]`.
- **FR-213**: The system MUST allow an event with no categories and MUST render it neutrally — a light fill with a thin border. The rendering is `[V-photo]`; the permission is `[?]` in every document and is granted `[OURS 2026-09-02 #9]`.
- **FR-214**: The system MUST choose event text colour for at least 4.5:1 contrast against the block's own fill, taking the fill from the leftmost solid segment on a striped block. `[OURS 2026-09-02]` — the reference's photographs contain only pastel fills, while nine of the twenty sanctioned colours are dark enough to fail a fixed dark ink.
- **FR-215**: The system MUST shade weekend columns and MUST dim past events. Both toggles are `[V](36835449004315)`; their visual values are `[?]`, and holding both permanently on in this phase is `[OURS 2026-09-02 #16]`.
- **FR-216**: The system MUST keep every event a day holds reachable, scrolling the day column when more exist than fit; no event may be dropped or hidden without a way to reach it. Overflow by scrolling is `[V](48026687853083 — "swipe up or down in the day to see more events")`; the documented "up to four full events per day" is treated as a consequence of the reference device's fixed geometry, not a rule `[OURS 2026-09-02 #15]`.
- **FR-217**: The system MUST draw an event that crosses midnight as one event with a segment in each day column it touches, and MUST treat an edit or drag begun from any segment as acting on that single event. `[?]` — resolved `[OURS 2026-09-02]`.

Eight places where the research sources disagree with each other. Each is recorded with both readings and the resolution, rather than averaged away or silently picked.

1. **View-switcher position.** One article places the switcher top-left `[V](360059869651)`; another implies top-right `[V](360033104791)`, and the photograph reads it as a top-right pill `[V-photo]`. **Resolution**: moot — this phase ships one view and therefore no switcher (FR-202). The previous-week / Today / next-week cluster joins the existing top-bar pills on the right, matching the photograph. `[OURS 2026-09-02 #1]`
2. **Swipe direction.** One article says *"Swipe left to see previous dates, and swipe right to see future dates"* `[V](44738510847259)`; the Week-view article says *"swipe right to show past events and swipe left to show future events"* `[V](36625171368987)`. These are opposite and both are verbatim. **Resolution**: **swipe left = later, swipe right = earlier** — the content follows the finger, matching platform convention on both mobile platforms. FR-279. `[OURS 2026-09-02 #14]`
3. **Long-press to create.** Dossier 01 records tap-and-hold-on-blank-space as `[V](360033104791)`; dossier 08 searched the whole product and found *no* source describing a long-press gesture anywhere, grading creation as a two-step tap flow. Both dossiers are in this repo and disagree. **Resolution**: side with dossier 08 — no long-press, no tap-and-hold. Creation is the primary create control plus a tap on an empty slot (FR-254), and a drag starts on press-and-move with no timed hold (FR-253). An invented gesture is not built on a contested citation. `[OURS 2026-09-02 #13]`
4. **Reminder input.** One article documents a free 1–120-minute field `[V](36836043247131)`; another documents 10 / 30 / 60-minute presets plus a Custom option with a Minutes / Hours / Days picker `[V](45795554249371)`. 5. **Week-view column count.** One product photograph shows seven day columns `[V-photo]`; the measured photograph closes its width arithmetic on five columns of 337 points `[V-photo]`, and its view pill may not even read "Week". ### Key Entities

- **Event** — one thing on the calendar. It has a title, optional notes, an optional free-text place, and either a span of clock time on a date or a run of whole days. It remembers the timezone of the device that created it as provenance — the calendar itself always works in the household's timezone — and carries a reserved flag for a future countdown feature that nothing in this phase reads or writes. It belongs to exactly one household, and records which profile created it and which profile last changed it.
- **Repeat rule** — an event's optional recurrence: how often it comes round (every day, every week on chosen weekdays, every month on the date) and when it stops (never, or an end date). An event has at most one. Stored so Skylight-format recurrences survive — in the reference product's own rule format — even though the interface offers only four choices, so nothing is lost if the interface grows later.
- **Occurrence** — one dated appearance of an event. A non-repeating event has exactly one; a repeating event has one for each date its rule produces. Occurrences are worked out from the event and its rule, not stored one by one, so a series that never ends costs nothing to keep.
- **Occurrence exception** — a record that one occurrence differs from its series: either it is skipped, or some of its fields are overridden. Exactly four things may be overridden — its time, its title, its place and its notes (FR-239). The event's Profiles and Labels are never among them; they change at series scope only (FR-287). It is identified by the occurrence's original date and time, so it stays attached to the right occurrence when the series changes. Deleting the series deletes its exceptions, and truncating the series at "this and future" deletes those dated on or after the cut (FR-286).
- **Event–category link** — which Profiles and Labels an event belongs to, in the order their colours are drawn on the block. An event may link to none, one or several. Deleting a Profile or Label removes its links and leaves the events themselves untouched.
- **Household calendar preferences** — the household-level settings the week reads: the existing start-of-week preference, which this phase reads unchanged, and the household's timezone (FR-284), which this phase adds, seeds at setup, and exposes to nothing. This phase adds no new settings interface; the record is the extension point where a later phase may expose the timezone along with the weekend-shading, past-dimming and multi-profile-colouring toggles that are fixed for now.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-201**: A parent standing at the tablet can add a repeating weekly event — title, day, time, who it is for, and the repeat — in **under 30 seconds** from first touch, including punching in.
- **SC-202**: At tablet-landscape width, every event that fits its day column without scrolling is legible and its owning Profile or Label identifiable **without any interaction at all** — no tap, no scroll, no hover. This supersedes 001 SC-004, which Phase 1 had no events with which to test it.
- **SC-203**: The week the family sees never shows a stranger's data: a request for another household's events, repeat rules, occurrence exceptions or category links returns nothing, on every path the calendar reads. Verified by an explicit test per path.
- **SC-204**: A moved occurrence survives a reload and appears moved on a second device **within 5 seconds**, with no reload on the second device.
- **SC-205**: Nothing on the calendar changes without someone identifying themselves: every create, edit, delete and drag commit is refused when nobody is punched in, including when the request bypasses the interface entirely; and every stored event names the profile that made it.
- **SC-206**: Moving an event to another day and time takes **one gesture and at most two prompts** — the scope question when the block is one occurrence of a repeat, then the punch-in sheet when nobody is currently punched in, in that order — and the block is in its new place **within 1 second** of the last prompt being answered. A one-off block moved by someone already punched in takes the gesture and nothing else.
- **SC-207**: Each of the three scopes does exactly what it says, proved by six checks — edit and delete, at each of this-occurrence, this-and-future and all — each comparing the full occurrence set before and after, and each also checking what became of that series' per-occurrence edits.
- **SC-208**: An event at 09:00 every Tuesday still reads 09:00 on the wall on both sides of every daylight-saving change in the household's timezone, across a series spanning a full year, whatever timezone the device that created it was set to; and no occurrence in that year is missing or duplicated.
- **SC-209**: The week renders with no horizontal scrolling of the page and no overlapping controls at tablet-landscape, tablet-portrait and phone widths, and every control on it is at least 44×44 points at all three.
- **SC-210**: No event a day holds is unreachable: with 12 events on one day, all 12 can be reached by scrolling that day; and with five events overlapping the same hour, every one is reachable — those beyond the side-by-side cap through that band's "+n more" — and none is silently dropped.
- **SC-211**: The calendar left open on the wall is still correct the next morning: the today marker, the current-time line and the displayed week are those of the new day, with nobody having touched it overnight.
- **SC-212**: Deleting is never accidental and never reversible: every delete asks first, and an audit of the calendar finds no undo, restore or trash path anywhere.
- **SC-213**: A person who hides a profile or a label on the phone sees exactly the events that remain, that choice survives a reload of the phone, and the tablet's view is unchanged.
- **SC-214**: Deleting a profile never destroys the family's history: every event that profile was on is still on the calendar afterwards, rendered neutrally if it has no other category.
- **SC-215**: An occurrence edited for that occurrence only survives a reload, shows the same single-occurrence change on a second device, and leaves every other occurrence in its series untouched.

## Assumptions

Decisions the operator took on **2026-09-02**, or delegated and had recorded, wherever the research was inferred, unknown, or had no equivalent. Recorded here rather than asserted as fact, per constitution §VIII. The numbers are the reference used by the `[OURS 2026-09-02 #n]` tags above.

1. **Week is the only view.** No Schedule, Day or Month view ships in this phase, and no view switcher is built. All four views are `[V]`; shipping one is a scope decision, and it makes the whole switcher-position contradiction moot. `[OURS]`
2. **Repeats are deliberately simple.** Never / Every day / Every week on chosen weekdays / Every month on the date, with an optional end date and no count. The reference's full repeat enum is `[?]` — only daily, weekly and monthly are verified — so the interface offers what is verified plus weekday selection, while the *storage* keeps the reference product's own canonical rule format so a richer interface later throws nothing away. No every-N interval, no nth-weekday, no yearly. `[OURS]`
3. **Both edit and delete offer all three scopes.** Contradiction 7. `[OURS]` One bound was added while writing this specification, and the operator should see it: when the field being changed is the event's Profiles or Labels, "this occurrence" is not offered, because the occurrence-exception record holds no categories and per-occurrence reassignment is out of scope (Assumption 26, FR-287). Every other edit and every delete keeps all three. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
4. **Drag is full, and it is entirely our invention.** Move within a day, across days, between the all-day band and the timed grid; resize by either edge; 15-minute snap; punch-in demanded on drop rather than on grab, so a passer-by can pick a block up but only an identified person can commit it. The reference documents no drag on the calendar grid at all, so every one of these parameters is a decision with no source behind it. `[OURS]`
5. **Dragging one occurrence of a repeat always asks.** Same three options, same wording as an edit, never silent. A drag that silently changed a whole series would be the single most destructive gesture in the app. `[OURS]` The *order* of the two prompts — scope first, punch-in second, so a person chooses what they are changing before they identify themselves — is ours, added while writing this specification. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
6. **Countdowns are deferred.** No countdown interface ships. The event record reserves the flag now — the reference's countdown is a per-event boolean `[V]` — so the later phase needs no migration. `[OURS]`
7. **Phone shows a three-day slice of the same grid, and the column count flexes.** Seven columns at 1024 points and wider in landscape; three on a phone; as many whole columns as fit in between. The reference is photographed at both seven and five columns and has no non-reference layout of any kind, so the count is a viewport consequence rather than a product fact. `[OURS]`
8. **Invited emails are dropped.** Contradiction 6. `[OURS]`
9. **An event may have no categories at all,** and renders neutrally when it does. The reference photographs a white-outlined unassigned pill, but no document says whether a category is required on an event; the requirement is verified only for chores, rewards and nudges, and that does not carry across. `[OURS]`
10. **Labels join the existing filter sheet** with show/hide toggles, exactly as profiles already have. Whether the reference does this is `[?]`; a label the household cannot hide would be the one colour on the wall nobody can turn off. `[OURS]`
11. **No undo. Delete confirms first, then is final.** The reference's delete is verified as irreversible; the confirmation dialog is ours, so the irreversibility is never a surprise. `[OURS]`
12. **The now line is `#F66951`, 2 points thick, with the ~14-point today dot, on today's column only,** repositioned every minute through the existing clock, and the open view rolls over live at midnight exactly as the Phase 1 clock already does. The colour and the existence of the bar and the dot are verified; the geometry is photo-estimated and the refresh cadence and the midnight rollover have no source at all — no article describes what an open view does at midnight. `[OURS]` One qualifier is ours, added while writing this specification: the *week* advances at a week boundary only while the current week is the one on show, so a person who has navigated three weeks ahead is not yanked back by a midnight they did not notice. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
13. **No long-press or tap-and-hold gesture anywhere.** Creation is the primary create control plus a tap on an empty slot. Contradiction 3. This also means the reference's per-column "+ Add Event" button, which is verified, is not reproduced — one create path plus direct slot entry is enough, and a per-column button in a three-column phone slice earns nothing. `[OURS]`
14. **Swipe left goes later, swipe right goes earlier.** Contradiction 2. `[OURS]`
15. **The documented view capacities are reference geometry, not laws.** "Up to four full events per day" in Week, ten or sixteen in Day, three in Month are all verified — for a fixed 1920×1080 device. Our grid self-measures at whatever size it is given and overflows honestly — by scrolling the day column when a day holds too many events, and by the side-by-side cap and "+n more" of Assumption 36 when one time band does — rather than encoding a number that was true of somebody else's screen. `[OURS]`
16. **No new settings interface ships in this phase.** Behaviour is fixed: multi-profile colour-coding on, weekend shading on, past events dimmed, and the week starting on the household's existing start-of-week setting, defaulting to Sunday. Every one of these is a verified *toggle* whose *factory default* is `[?]` — the reference publishes recommended values, not defaults — so these are chosen values. A later phase may expose them; the household preferences record is the agreed extension point. `[OURS]`
17. **The multi-profile treatment is the photographed striped bar,** and its geometry stays photo-estimated. The stripes are visible in the product photographs at roughly 45° and roughly 40 points wide, but no Skylight document ever uses the word "diagonal" — the documents say only "multiple colors" and, third-hand, "striped bars". The treatment is asserted because a photograph shows it, never because a specification says so. `[OURS]`
18. **Events store the timezone they were created in as provenance; the calendar always works in the household's timezone.** The per-event timezone is verified in the reference's own record shape, so it is kept; no timezone picker ships, and the creating device's zone is what gets written. Nothing in this phase renders from it or expands a repeat with it — that is the household's timezone's job, Assumption 34. `[OURS]`
19. **No reminders in this phase.** Delivery belongs to the notification phase, and nothing is stored now that presumes either of the reference's two contradictory reminder interfaces. Contradiction 4. `[OURS]`
20. **Location is free text only** — no weather, no geocoding, no lookup. The reference's field is "Location & weather"; weather is excluded from this project entirely. `[OURS]`
21. **Merging a Label or converting one to a Profile belongs to a later phase.** Both are verified reference features and both are category operations rather than event operations, but merging re-points every event's category links, which is work this phase does not need. `[OURS]`
22. **No pinch-to-zoom.** The gesture is verified, but it belongs to Schedule view, which does not ship. `[OURS]`

Delegated calls made while writing this specification, recorded here on the same footing:

23. **Event writes are open to any punched-in profile, parent or member.** Phase 1 reserves parent-only for profiles, labels, PINs, roles and household settings; events are not on that list, and a household where a teenager cannot add their own football match would not be used. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
24. **Deleting a Profile or Label never deletes its events.** The events survive, unassigned, rendered neutrally, and the confirmation says how many are affected. The reference is silent; destroying a child's history because a profile was tidied up is not a defensible default. The count is an **amendment to a Phase 1 surface**: 001 FR-026's category-delete confirmation is shipped and warns without counting, and this phase changes that dialog. The plan must treat it as work, not as inheritance. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
25. **An event is shown when any one of its assigned categories is visible,** and an event with no categories is always shown. Hiding one parent should not hide the event they share with the other. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
26. **A single-occurrence edit stays in its series with only the differing fields overridden,** rather than detaching into a standalone event, and the fields it may override are exactly four: time, title, place and notes. The reference's behaviour here is explicitly unknown; keeping it attached means a later change to the series still reaches everything that occurrence did not override. An event's **Profiles and Labels are deliberately not on that list** — categories change at series scope only, which is the same decision as putting per-occurrence reassignment out of scope, and it keeps a repeat one colour along its length. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
27. **"This and future" on the first occurrence means "all",** on a delete as much as on an edit, and after a split, "all" means the segment the person is standing in. Both are consequences of splitting a series, neither is documented anywhere, and both are stated so the household meets them in the wording rather than in a surprise. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
28. **Daylight-saving rules are stated, not inherited.** Occurrences are generated in the household's timezone (Assumption 34); a time that does not exist there lands on the first valid time that date; a time that happens twice renders once, at the first instant. The reference's own recurring-event behaviour around timezone boundaries is a documented defect in its store reviews, so it is a test list rather than behaviour to copy. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
29. **An all-day event's end date is inclusive.** The one captured example is consistent with both an inclusive and an exclusive reading; inclusive is what a person means by "Friday to Sunday". `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
30. **A title is required; an end before a start is refused.** Neither validation is documented for events in any source. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
31. **Event text colour is derived from the block's own fill.** The reference's photographs contain only pastel fills, while nine of the twenty sanctioned palette colours are dark enough that a fixed dark ink fails contrast — a case the source material simply never contains. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
32. **The create-form field order is ours.** The field *set* is verified; the sources state explicitly that the on-screen order is not. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
33. **Birthdays are not rendered on the calendar in this phase.** Profiles already carry a birthday, and birthdays on the calendar are in the project's scope, but the mechanism — synthesised each year from the profile, or created as real repeating events — is in no source and was not decided. Nothing in this phase renders them, and nothing here forecloses either mechanism. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
34. **The household has one timezone, and it is stored.** Phase 1 stores no timezone anywhere and no source names one, yet the grid, the DST rules and SC-208 all lean on "the household's local time". So this phase adds a single IANA name to the household record, seeded when the household is set up. It is read-only in this phase — Assumption 16 ships no new settings interface — and it is what the grid renders in and what a repeat's occurrences are generated in. The event's own creating-device zone stays as provenance (Assumption 18): if it drove the expansion instead, a weekly 09:00 event created on a travelling phone would keep another country's wall clock and shift on that country's daylight-saving dates, which is exactly what SC-208 forbids. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
35. **The displayed window is always a slice of one anchored week,** never a free-running run of N days. The week is anchored on the household's start-of-week day; it is cut into slices as wide as the column count, tiled from the week's first day, with the last slice pulled back so it ends on the week's last day and still shows a full set of columns. A swipe moves one slice and, past the last, into the next week's first slice; the arrows move a whole week. This is what decision #7 — "a three-day slice, swipe to move through the week" — means once the column count can be three, five or seven, and it is what keeps "the displayed week" a meaningful phrase on a phone. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
36. **Overlapping events are capped side by side, and the remainder is reachable, not hidden.** Three abreast where the day column is at least 180 points wide, two below that; anything further collapses into a tappable "+n more" for that time band. The sources show no three-way split at all, and four events sharing a phone-width column would be about 30 points each — unreadable, and unhittable against the 44-point floor. Scrolling a day column cannot widen simultaneous events, so it is not the answer to this case. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
37. **A "this and future" delete truncates the series.** It ends immediately before the chosen occurrence, taking every per-occurrence edit and skip dated on or after it, and leaving everything earlier untouched. Nothing in any source describes a truncating delete, and SC-207 requires all six scope-and-operation combinations to be provable, so the behaviour has to be stated rather than left to the implementation. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
38. **A write that cannot complete is refused, never queued.** Offline, and the case where another device has already deleted the event, both end in a message and no stored change — no optimistic write, no local queue, nothing shown as saved that is not saved. The offline cache is out of scope this phase, so pretending otherwise would be a lie the household could act on. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
39. **Deletions reach other devices as bare invalidation signals.** Phase 1's live-update channel filters by household on the server, and a deletion notice carries only the deleted record's identifier — not its household — so a filtered subscription would never fire on a delete. Phase 1's prohibition on broadcasting whole deleted rows stands, because a deleted event's title is exactly the child's-schedule data the constitution protects. So the calendar's records are subscribed without that filter and every notice is treated as "something changed, re-read" — the payload carries no content either way, and the household is the only tenant on this project. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
40. **Week renders a multi-day event the way Schedule and Month do** — one bar spanning the days, in a band above the hours, rather than a repeat in each day cell. The one-bar rendering is verified for Schedule and Month only; whether Week does the same, and whether Week has an all-day band at all, are both explicitly unknown in every document. A photographed all-day pill sitting inside the day-header band is what makes the band credible, and one bar is what a person means by "the camping trip". `[OURS 2026-09-02]`

41. **The household's timezone is `America/Chicago`.** Clarified with the operator 2026-09-02. The value the seed writes into the household record (FR-284); every DST example in this specification (the March jump, the November repeat) is therefore US Central's. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
42. **The idle grid follows the now line.** The hour scroll holds the current time about a third from the top while untouched, so the wall reads correctly at a glance all day; a manual scroll pauses the following until the day rolls over or Today is tapped. A fixed morning start was rejected — at 20:00 it shows an empty morning — and compressing 24 hours into the viewport makes short events unreadable at wall distance. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`

43. **A timed event's end carries its own date.** The form shows start date+time and end date+time, the end's date defaulting to the start's, and validation compares instants — so a Friday 22:00 – Saturday 01:00 party is creatable and a same-day backwards range is refused. Clarified with the operator 2026-09-02; a times-only form either forbids midnight-crossers the edge cases require or silently wraps a typo into a 23-hour event. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`
44. **Blocks always drag; paging swipes start elsewhere.** Movement on an event block begins a drag on every device (FR-253); a paging swipe starts on empty grid, the hour ruler or the day-header band, and the arrows and Today always work. One rule everywhere, and cross-day drag survives on the phone; the header band guarantees a swipe surface even on a day solid with blocks. Clarified with the operator 2026-09-02. `[OURS 2026-09-02]`

Smaller calls are carried by the requirement that makes them rather than numbered here, and each states its own reasoning inline: FR-214 (ink derived from the fill), FR-217 (a midnight-crossing event is one event), FR-218 (the minimum block height), FR-227 (colour order), FR-238 (extending the reference's one-time-item rule from chores to events), FR-240 (a single-occurrence delete is recorded as a skip), FR-252 (script-driven drag animation must honour reduced motion itself), FR-255, FR-260, FR-262, FR-265, FR-267 (filtering never writes), FR-272, and FR-273 (the calendar's new records get their own store-level policies). All are `[OURS 2026-09-02]`.

## Dependencies

- Phase 1 (`001-family-foundation`) shipped and deployed: household sign-in and the membership allowlist, the punch-in actor model and PIN flow, Profiles and Labels with the sanctioned colour palette, the app shell with its filter sheet and create control, the design-token layer, live updates for the household's records, and the installed PWA.

- Phase 1's live-update channel and its household-scoping rules, which are mechanisms this phase extends rather than inherits: the calendar's own records need their own store-level policies (FR-273) and their own place on that channel (FR-276, Assumption 39), and the Phase 1 category-delete confirmation gains an affected-event count (FR-274, Assumption 24).
- The research dossiers and master map in `docs/research/skylight/`, which this specification treats as the product definition, and the operator's Phase 2 decisions of 2026-09-02 recorded above.

## Out of Scope

Deferred to a later phase: the Schedule, Day and Month views and any view switcher; pinch-to-zoom; countdowns and their preview bar; reminders and their delivery; undo or a trash for deleted events; merging a Label or converting one to a Profile; a settings interface for the calendar toggles and the household timezone (they are fixed this phase); tasks progress and meals shown on the calendar; per-occurrence reassignment of Profiles and Labels (FR-287); search; birthday rendering; and the offline cache.

Excluded from the project entirely: weather in any form, including the event's forecast and the ten-day strip; external calendar synchronisation and every field that exists to serve it; invited emails; all AI features, including the photo, voice and email input methods on the create form and automatic emoji; the photo screensaver and sleep mode; themed character modes; companion hardware; grocery-ordering integrations; multi-device linking; guest invitations; and subscription tiers.
