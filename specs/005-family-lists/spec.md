# Feature Specification: Family Lists

**Feature Branch**: `005-family-lists`
**Created**: 2026-09-05
**Status**: Draft
**Input**: Phase 5 of the `/family` Skylight Calendar clone — the Lists tab: a row of coloured list cards, each a shared list the household can add to, check off, clear, section and reorder from the wall tablet or a phone; two lists ready on day one; a list that stays off the wall until a parent is punched in. Phases 1 (household access, punch-in PINs, the shell, Profiles & Labels, tokens, PWA), 2 (the Week calendar), 3 (the Tasks tab) and 4 (the Rewards tab and the star economy, through migration 027) are shipped and deployed, and are the foundation this stacks on. The locked plan's `family-lists-meals` is built as two phases — this one, then `006-family-meals` — the way `family-tasks-rewards` became 003 and 004 (Assumption 1).

The operator's Phase 5 scoping decisions were delegated on **2026-09-05** ("any questions you have, research Skylight and answer them yourself") and are recorded here as final for this specification.

**The governing rule for this phase**, from the operator on 2026-09-04 and unchanged: *match the reference product wherever the research says what it does.* A behaviour tagged `[V]` or `[V-photo]` is adopted, not weighed and not improved. ## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

The same example household as Phases 2–4, so the specifications read as one product and the scenarios can be run by hand: profiles **Ana** (parent), **Ben** (parent) and **Cleo** (child), plus the Label **Bin day**. Household timezone `America/Chicago`.

### User Story 1 — The Lists tab and its lists (Priority: P1)

The family opens the Lists tab and sees their lists side by side as coloured cards — the Grocery List and the To-Do List that were there from the first day, and any they have added since. A card carries its name, how many things are still to do on it, and its items. Anyone punched in can add a list, rename one, change its colour or type, or delete one they no longer need.

**Why this priority**: Without lists there is nothing to put items on. The tab, the cards and list management are the surface every other story lives inside, and the two default lists make the tab useful before anyone has typed a word.

**Independent Test**: On a fresh household, open `/family/lists`: two cards, "Grocery List" and "To-Do List", each empty with a count of 0. Add a list "Packing List" in rose; rename it; change its type; delete it with the confirmation. Nothing else in the app changes.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a household that has just been set up, **When** anyone opens the Lists tab, **Then** two cards are shown in this order — "Grocery List" (a Grocery list, in Sprout green) and "To-Do List" (a To do list, in Cyan) — each with no items and a count badge of 0, and the shell's profile chip row is not shown above them.
2. **Given** Ana is punched in on the tablet, **When** she taps the `+` and enters "Packing List", type Other, colour Grapefruit, **Then** a third card appears at the right end of the row with that name and colour, and on Ben's phone within five seconds.
3. **Given** the "Packing List" card, **When** Cleo (punched in) taps its name and changes the colour to River, **Then** the card's panel, item rows and count badge all take the new colour's tints at once, on every device.
4. **Given** the "Packing List" card holds four items, **When** Ben opens its menu and chooses Delete, **Then** he is told the list and its four items will be removed and nothing else, and only after he confirms does the card disappear everywhere.
5. **Given** nobody is punched in, **When** someone taps the `+`, **Then** the punch-in keypad appears first, and the Add List form only after a PIN is accepted.
6. **Given** a household with five lists on a 1920×1080 wall display, **When** the tab opens, **Then** three whole cards are visible and a swipe brings the next card into view one at a time; the page never scrolls sideways.

### User Story 2 — Items: adding, checking, clearing (Priority: P2)

Standing at the fridge, Cleo types "🥚 Eggs" into the box at the top of the Grocery List and it appears on the list. At the shop, Ben ticks Eggs on his phone; the row's checkbox fills, the text greys and strikes through, and the wall shows the same within seconds. When the shopping is unpacked, one tap on Clear Completed removes every ticked item. A parent who does not want ticked items cluttering the wall switches them off for that device in the Filter.

**Why this priority**: Adding and ticking are what a list is for, and clearing is what keeps it usable. This story is the daily loop.

**Independent Test**: Add three items to the Grocery List, tick two on a second device, watch the first device follow within five seconds, un-tick one, clear completed, and confirm exactly the one ticked item is gone and the count badge reads 2.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the Grocery List card, **When** Cleo (punched in) types "Milk" into the "Add item" box and presses enter, **Then** "Milk" appears as the last item of the list with an empty square checkbox, the count badge rises by one, and the box is empty and focused for the next item.
2. **Given** "Milk" is unchecked, **When** Ben taps its checkbox on his phone, **Then** the checkbox fills, the text turns grey with a strikethrough, the item stays exactly where it was, the count badge falls by one, and the wall display shows the same within five seconds.
3. **Given** "Milk" is checked, **When** anyone punched in taps its checkbox again, **Then** it returns to unchecked everywhere and the count badge rises by one.
4. **Given** the Grocery List holds three checked and two unchecked items, **When** Ana chooses Clear Completed from the list menu, **Then** she is told three completed items will be removed, and on confirming exactly those three are gone on every device and the two unchecked items keep their order.
5. **Given** the wall display, **When** a parent turns "Completed items" off under Lists in the Filter sheet, **Then** checked items are hidden on that device only; the count badge is unchanged (it never counted them); every other device still shows them struck through.
6. **Given** an item "Kosher salt", **When** someone taps its text, **Then** a small sheet opens where the text can be corrected, the item moved to a section, or the item deleted outright — the way the phone app's "x" deletes an item without ticking it.
7. **Given** the tablet has lost its connection, **When** someone ticks an item, **Then** the tick is refused with a plain message and the checkbox stays empty; nothing is queued to happen later.

### User Story 3 — Sections and order (Priority: P3)

The Grocery List has grown to twenty items. Ana groups them: from the list menu she chooses Add section, names it "Dairy", and picks Milk, Yoghurt and Butter; the three now sit under a "Dairy" header with its own count and a chevron that folds it away. Later she long-presses "Bread" — space opens around it and a small pointer appears to its left — and drags it up under "Bakery". When the sections are no longer wanted, removing one leaves its items on the list, ungrouped.

**Why this priority**: Sections and reorder turn a long list into a shopping route. They are real, documented and released features of the reference, but a list works without them.

**Independent Test**: Create a section with two items, rename it, drag a third item into it, collapse and expand it, remove it and see all three items still present and ungrouped; on a second device the same order and grouping appear within five seconds.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a list with six ungrouped items, **When** Ana chooses Add section, types "Dairy", picks two items and confirms, **Then** the two items move under a "Dairy" header showing "2 items" and a chevron, below the four ungrouped items.
2. **Given** a "Dairy" section exists, **When** Ana adds a section and types "Dairy" again with one more item selected, **Then** no second "Dairy" is made; the item joins the existing section and its count reads 3.
3. **Given** the "Dairy" header, **When** someone taps its chevron, **Then** its items fold away on that device and the header stays with its count; tapping again unfolds them.
4. **Given** "Bread" is ungrouped, **When** Cleo presses and holds it, **Then** after a moment space opens around it and a small pointer appears to its left; **When** she drags it between "Milk" and "Yoghurt" and lets go, **Then** it lands exactly there, inside "Dairy", in one write, and the second device shows the same order and grouping within five seconds.
5. **Given** "Bread" is being dragged, **When** Cleo lets go outside the list, **Then** nothing changes and nothing is written.
6. **Given** a "Dairy" section with three items, **When** Ana removes the section, **Then** the three items stay on the list, ungrouped, in their order, and no item is lost.
7. **Given** a "Dairy" section, **When** Ana renames it "Fridge", **Then** every item that was under "Dairy" is now under "Fridge" and nothing else moves.

### User Story 4 — A list that stays off the wall (Priority: P4)

Ana is planning Cleo's birthday. She creates "Party" and marks it Parents only. On the wall display, where nobody is punched in, the card is simply not there. When Ana punches in on the tablet the card appears; when her punch-in lapses it goes again. On her own phone, punched in as a parent, it is always there. Cleo, punched in as a member, never sees it.

**Why this priority**: It is the reference's documented reason for "Hide on Device" — gift and party lists — and it is the one place this phase departs from the reference, so it must be specified precisely. Everything else works without it.

**Independent Test**: Mark a list Parents only; check it is absent with nobody punched in and with Cleo punched in, present within a second of Ana punching in, and absent again after punch-out; a member cannot reach its items by any path shown on screen.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** Ana (parent) is punched in, **When** she edits "Party" and turns on Parents only, **Then** the card stays on her screen and vanishes from every screen where no parent is punched in, within five seconds.
2. **Given** the wall display with nobody punched in, **When** Ana punches in, **Then** the "Party" card appears in its place in the row; **When** her punch-in lapses or she punches out, **Then** it disappears and any sheet open on one of its items closes.
3. **Given** Cleo (member) is punched in on her tablet, **When** she opens the Lists tab, **Then** "Party" is not shown and the count of cards excludes it.
4. **Given** "Party" is Parents only, **When** Ana turns Parents only off, **Then** the card appears on every device within five seconds.

### Edge Cases

- **Two devices tick the same item in the same second.** Both writes carry the same intent; both succeed; the item is checked once, and neither device shows an error. One ticks and the other un-ticks in the same second: the later write wins and both devices settle on it within five seconds.
- **Clear Completed races an un-tick.** Ana clears completed on the tablet while Ben un-ticks "Milk" on his phone. Whichever write the store applies first decides: if the un-tick landed first, "Milk" survives; if the clear landed first, "Milk" is gone and Ben's un-tick is refused as no longer here. Nothing is half-done.
- **A list is deleted while another device is adding to it.** The add is refused with the shipped "no longer here" message and nothing is written; the card disappears from the second device within five seconds.
- **The item text is only spaces, or is longer than the limit.** It is refused before it is sent, against the box, and nothing is written.
- **A section name collides only by case or surrounding spaces** ("dairy " vs "Dairy"). Names are trimmed and compared case-insensitively; the items join the existing section under its existing spelling.
- **The last item leaves a section** (moved, deleted or cleared). The section header disappears with it; a section exists only through its items.
- **Every list is deleted.** The tab shows an empty state — "No lists yet" with the `+` still available — and never an error.
- **All lists are Parents only and nobody is punched in.** The tab shows the same empty state; it does not reveal that hidden lists exist.
- **A parent's punch-in lapses mid-drag on a Parents only list.** The drag is cancelled, nothing is written, and the card leaves the row.
- **A dropped item lands on its own position.** No write is made.
- **The phone is narrower than a card.** One card fills the width, cards page one at a time by swipe, and a press-and-hold on an item is a drag while a plain horizontal swipe is a page — the two gestures never fight.
- **Storage is refused on the device** (private mode, quota). The Completed switch and the collapsed sections still work for the session and are simply not remembered; the sheet says so, as the Tasks filters already do.
- **A Profile who created lists and ticked items is deleted.** Every list and item stays exactly as it is; only the record of who made or ticked it is cleared. Nothing in the delete confirmation needs to mention lists.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**The Lists tab**

- **FR-501**: The system MUST replace the Phase 1 placeholder behind the shipped Lists navigation tab with this tab, keeping the tab's place, label and list icon. `[P1]` FR-029 fulfilled for Lists.
- **FR-502**: The system MUST present the household's lists as a single horizontal row of cards, one card per list, in the household's stored order, and MUST show as many whole cards as fit the available width — the fit measured against the width a card needs, never a breakpoint — with the remaining cards reached by swiping one card at a time. The row is verified `[V](37275069922971 — "a horizontally scrollable row of list cards/columns; swipe left/right to see additional lists")`, `[V](36625171368987 — swipe on Lists reveals more)`; the photographed screen shows ≈3.4 cards of ~495 units `[V-photo]` `[ESTIMATED]`; the whole-card fit and one-at-a-time paging are the shipped board chassis `[P3]` FR-394, applied to cards that are never wrapped into a second row `[OURS 2026-09-05 #11]`.
- **FR-503**: Each card MUST show, top to bottom: a header with the list's name in the serif face and a round count badge at the right; the list's items as rows; and a footer row drawn as a section header in its placeholder state reading "Add section". The header, rows and footer are `[V-photo]` (dossier 07 §6.5, `gallery/07`, `pdp/08`); the metrics — card ~495 wide, radius ~28, gap ~38; header ~100; rows ~76 high, radius ~14, pitch ~114; checkbox a rounded square ~63 with radius ~10; count badge a circle ~53; footer ~110; title ~46, item text ~25 — are `[ESTIMATED]` and live in the token layer.
- **FR-504**: Each card MUST be tinted from the list's one stored colour in three steps — the panel very light, each item row mid, the count badge saturated — derived the way every profile tint is derived, never hand-picked. `[V-photo]` (dossier 07 §1.7 sampled ramps); `[V](00-master-map §5.4)`; derivation rule `[P1]`.
- **FR-505**: The count badge MUST show the number of unchecked items on the list, and MUST NOT change when checked items are hidden by the device's Completed switch. The badge is `[V-photo]` ("Grocery List (5)"); what it counts is `[?]`, resolved `[OURS 2026-09-05 #10]`.
- **FR-506**: The system MUST NOT render the shell's profile chip row on the Lists tab. The photographed Lists screen goes from the top bar straight into the cards `[V-photo]` (dossier 07 §6.5); lists are not per-Profile, so the chips would count nothing `[OURS 2026-09-05 #12]`. This changes the shipped tab definition `[P1]` FR-032, as Phase 3's FR-314 did for Tasks.
- **FR-507**: The shipped create control (the `+`) MUST open the Add List form on this tab. `[V](37275069922971 — "The '+'/Add control opens an 'Add List' dialog")`; the reference's photo and voice capture inside that dialog are Sidekick and excluded (Out of Scope). `[P1]` FR-034 registered for Lists `[OURS 2026-09-05 #12]`.

**Lists**

- **FR-509**: A list MUST have a name (1–120 characters, trimmed), a type, and a colour chosen from the household's 20-colour palette. Name, type and colour are the three fields the reference requires and edits `[V](37275069922971 — "change Title, List Type, or Color")`, `[V](pyskylight — "Label can't be blank; Kind can't be blank; Color can't be blank")`; the palette is the shipped domain `[P1]`; the length bound is Phase 2's title bound `[P2]`.
- **FR-510**: The type MUST be one of **To do**, **Grocery** and **Other**, offered in that order, and MUST have no effect on the list's behaviour in this phase beyond being shown when the list is edited — a Grocery list is the kind a later phase may push recipe ingredients onto. Three types `[V](37275069922971 — "'To do' (task-based lists), 'Grocery' (grocery lists; default-named 'Grocery List'), and 'Other' (custom/general lists)")`; the reference's grocery-only buttons are excluded (Out of Scope); Contradiction 1 `[OURS 2026-09-05 #2]`.
- **FR-511**: Any punched-in Profile MUST be able to create a list, and to change an existing list's name, type or colour by tapping its name or from its menu. The edit paths are `[V](37275069922971 — "tap the list name or use the list's menu")`; who may do it is `[?]`, resolved `[OURS 2026-09-05 #4]`.
- **FR-512**: Any punched-in Profile MUST be able to delete a list from its menu after a confirmation that states how many items go with it, and the deletion MUST remove the list and its items everywhere. Deleting a list is in no source `[?]`, and is provided because a family cannot live with lists it cannot remove `[OURS 2026-09-05 #14]`; the confirmation is constitution §VI.
- **FR-513**: A newly set-up household MUST have two lists ready: **"Grocery List"**, type Grocery, colour Sprout (`#B6E085`), then **"To-Do List"**, type To do, colour Cyan (`#A8D4D3`). They MUST be ordinary lists — renamable, recolourable, deletable — and MUST NOT be re-created once a household has any list. Two defaults `[V](37275069922971)`; their live names and colours `[V](skylight-api — "Grocery List", "To-Do List"; #B6E085 Sprout, #A8D4D3 Cyan)`; Contradiction 2 `[OURS 2026-09-05 #3]`.
- **FR-514**: A list MUST carry a **Parents only** switch, off by default. While it is on, the list MUST be shown on a device only while a parent is punched in on that device, MUST leave the row — closing any sheet open on it — the moment that punch-in ends, and MUST be absent for a punched-in member as it is for nobody. This is the reference's per-list "Hide on Device" `[V](47603555960475 — "hides a given list from appearing on the household's Calendar device(s)… keeping gift-shopping or birthday-party-planning lists private")`, `[V](skylight-api — hide_on_device)`, mapped onto this project's identity model `[OURS 2026-09-05 #5]`.
- **FR-515**: A list MUST belong to the household and never to a Profile or Label, and MUST record which Profile created it and which last changed it. `[V](skylight-api — no category on the list resource)`; attribution `[P1]` FR-016.

**Items**

- **FR-516**: Every card MUST carry an "Add item" box at the top of its items; typing a name and confirming MUST add the item to the end of the list's ungrouped items, unchecked, and leave the box empty and focused for the next. Text-only items `[V](skylight-api — a list item's only content is `label`)`; emoji and "x5" inline `[V-photo]` (`gallery/07`); no emoji is ever assigned for the person — the master map's scope excludes auto-emoji and AI sectioning as AI `[V](00-master-map §1)`; the limit `[?]` `[OURS 2026-09-05 #8]`.
- **FR-518**: Every item row MUST show its text at the left and a rounded **square** checkbox at the right; tapping the checkbox MUST check the item, and tapping it again MUST uncheck it. Checkbox and tap `[V](37275069922971 — "each item has a checkbox; tapping it checks the item off")`; square on the device `[V-photo]` (Tasks use circles, Lists squares — dossier 07 §3); un-checking `[?]` `[OURS 2026-09-05 #6]`; Contradiction 3.
- **FR-519**: A checked item MUST stay exactly where it is and be drawn with a filled checkbox, grey text and a strikethrough, until it is cleared or unchecked; checking MUST never delete or move it. `[V](37275069922971 — "filled checkbox, in gray text, and with strikethrough")`, `[V](47603555960475)`, `[V](51482785426075 — items "persist (grayed out, struck through) until manually cleared")`.
- **FR-520**: The shipped Filter sheet MUST gain a **Lists** section with one per-device switch, **Completed items**, on by default; off, checked items are hidden on that device only, sections still show their counts of unchecked items, and nothing is written. `[V](37275069922971 — "A Filter control toggles whether completed items are shown/hidden")`; per-device and default `[OURS 2026-09-05 #6]`; the sheet `[P1]`/`[P3]` FR-383 extended `[OURS 2026-09-05]`; Contradiction 4.
- **FR-521**: Each list's menu MUST offer **Clear Completed**, which after a confirmation stating the number of completed items MUST remove every checked item on that list and no other, in one write. The action and its scope are `[V](47603555960475)`, `[V](51482785426075 — "Clear completed list items in one step")`; the confirmation is constitution §VI `[OURS 2026-09-05 #7]`.
- **FR-522**: Tapping an item's text MUST open a small sheet where any punched-in Profile can correct the text, move the item to a section (an existing one or a new name), or delete it outright, with the sheet's refusal shown where the tap happened. Outright deletion `[V](360041476692 — "tap an 'x' button to delete an item outright")`; editing the text and the sheet itself `[?]` `[OURS 2026-09-05 #8]`.
- **FR-523**: Any punched-in Profile MUST be able to reorder items within a list by pressing and holding an item — after which space opens around it and a small pointer in the accent colour appears to its left — and dragging it to a new position; letting go MUST place it exactly there in one write, and letting go outside the list MUST change nothing. `[V](37275069922971 — "long-press an item — space appears around it with a small orange pointer to the left — then drag to the new location")`, `[V](51482785426075 — "Reorder list items with drag and drop")`; the press-and-hold machine is `[P3]` FR-379 applied to items `[OURS 2026-09-05 #13]`.
- **FR-524**: An item's order MUST be one stored position among its list's items, written once per drop, so a device that reads the list draws it in the same order as the device that dropped. `[V](skylight-api — list_item.position)`; the fractional index is `[P1]` FR-022's mechanism `[OURS 2026-09-05 #13]`.
- **FR-525**: Every item MUST record which Profile added it, and, while checked, which Profile checked it and when. `[P1]` FR-016 `[OURS 2026-09-05 #4]`.
- **FR-526**: Checking, unchecking, adding, editing, moving, deleting and clearing MUST produce no celebration, sound or animation beyond the row's own change under reduced motion rules; the reference documents no celebration for lists `[V-absence]` (dossiers 07 §7, 08) `[OURS 2026-09-05 #15]`.

**Sections**

- **FR-527**: A list's items MAY be grouped into sections; a section MUST be nothing more than a name carried by its items, so a section exists exactly while at least one item carries it and disappears with its last item. `[V](skylight-api — section is a string on the item; there is no section resource)`, `[V](44739335665051 — "At least one item must exist… an empty section can't be created standalone")`.
- **FR-528**: The list's menu MUST offer **Add section**, which asks for a name (1–60 characters, trimmed) and a choice of one or more of the list's items, and on confirming MUST put those items under that name. The flow `[V](44739335665051 — "open the list's menu, select 'Add Section,' type the section name… At least one item must exist")`; the bulk choice is the same article's "select multiple items, then use a 'Move' button to relocate them into an existing or new section" `[V]`; the length bound `[?]` `[OURS 2026-09-05 #16]`.
- **FR-529**: If the name given matches an existing section on that list — compared trimmed and case-insensitively — the chosen items MUST join the existing section under its existing spelling, and no second section MUST be made. `[V](44739335665051 — "If the chosen section name already exists, the selected items move into that existing section instead of creating a duplicate")`; the comparison rule `[OURS 2026-09-05 #9]`.

### Key Entities

- **List** — one shared list of the household: a name, a type (To do, Grocery or Other), one colour from the palette, whether it is Parents only, its place in the row, and which Profile created it and last changed it. It belongs to the household, never to a Profile or Label. Its count is not stored; it is the number of its unchecked items.
- **List item** — one line on a list: its text, whether it is checked (and by whom and when, while it is), the name of the section it sits under or none, its position among the list's items, and which Profile added it. It belongs to exactly one list and goes with it.
- **Section** — not a record: a name that some of a list's items share. It appears while at least one item carries it and is gone when none does. Its fold state is the device's, not the household's.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-501**: On a fresh household the Lists tab shows exactly two cards, "Grocery List" then "To-Do List", in the specified colours and types, and re-running the household set-up creates no third card.
- **SC-502**: An item added on one device appears on a second device **within 5 seconds** with no reload, as the last ungrouped item of the same list.
- **SC-503**: A check or uncheck on one device is drawn on a second device — filled, grey and struck through, or plain — **within 5 seconds**, with the item in the same place and both count badges agreeing.
- **SC-504**: Two devices checking the same item within the same second both succeed and neither shows an error; two devices checking and unchecking it within the same second settle on one state on both **within 5 seconds**.
- **SC-505**: Clear Completed on a list with N checked and M unchecked items removes exactly N items in **one** write, leaves the M in their order, and both devices agree **within 5 seconds**.
- **SC-506**: A press-and-hold lifts an item **within 500 ms** of holding still; a drop lands the item exactly where it was released — including into or out of a section — writes **once**, and the second device shows the same order and grouping **within 5 seconds**. A drop on the item's own position writes nothing.
- **SC-507**: A Parents only list is absent from a device with nobody punched in and from a device with a member punched in; it appears **within 1 second** of a parent punching in on that device and leaves **within 1 second** of the punch-in ending; a member's write to it is refused at the server.
- **SC-508**: Every write on this tab from a device that is offline is refused with a message **within 5 seconds**, stores nothing, and never appears later; every write with nobody punched in opens the keypad first and stores nothing until a PIN is accepted.
- **SC-509**: An anonymous session reading either of this phase's tables gets a refusal, not an empty result; an authenticated session from another household reads **zero** rows on every path; an authenticated session of this household cannot insert, update or delete a list or item directly — every write goes through an action that re-checks the punch-in.
- **SC-510**: At 1920×1080 three whole cards are visible; at 1180×820 two; at 820×1180 and 390×844 one, filling the width; at every width the page never scrolls sideways, a horizontal swipe pages exactly one card, and every control's touch target is at least 44×44 CSS px.
- **SC-511**: Every list metric named in FR-503 is a single token in the shipped token layer, each tagged `[ESTIMATED]`, read back by a unit test; no card, row, checkbox or badge size is written in a component.
- **SC-512**: Every checkbox, card, badge, menu and section chevron has an accessible name that a screen reader announces with its state; the whole tab is operable by keyboard, including reorder.
- **SC-513**: The pure logic of this phase — grouping items into ordered sections, the count rule, the section-name match, the position of a drop, what the Completed switch hides — is covered by unit tests that fail before the behaviour and pass after; the two tables' policies, grants and the actions' refusals are covered by the policies suite against the local stack.
- **SC-514**: After this phase is live, the Week calendar, the Tasks board and the Rewards tab still receive another device's changes **within 5 seconds** — the shared live channel is not taken down by the new tables.
- **SC-515**: The Phase 1 placeholder is gone from the Lists tab, the profile chip row is absent on it, and the `+` reads "Add List"; the Tasks and Rewards tabs are unchanged to the pixel.

## Assumptions

Decisions taken on **2026-09-05** under the operator's delegation ("any questions you have, research Skylight and answer them yourself"), wherever the research was inferred, unknown, contradictory, or had no equivalent in the reference product. Recorded here rather than asserted as fact, per constitution §VIII. The numbers are the reference used by the `[OURS 2026-09-05 #n]` tags above.

1. **Phase 5 is Lists only; Meals is Phase 6; the rest moves to Phase 7.** The locked plan's `family-lists-meals` is built as two phases for the reasons `family-tasks-rewards` was: each is reviewable and deployable alone, and Meals depends on Lists (its ingredients land on a Grocery list). The reference itself keeps them apart — lists are free, "Make and share lists" `[V](36009559376795)`, while meal planning and recipes are behind Calendar Plus `[V](36009559376795)`, `[V](44739809442587)`. Here there are no tiers: the operator's household is a personal planner with every feature present, so the gating is recorded only to explain the cut. Every earlier spec's "Phase 5" — reminders, Web Push, the home screen, search, the offline cache, the service worker — now means **Phase 7**, `family-notifications`. `[OURS]`
2. **Three list types, and the type does nothing yet.** The device article names To do, Grocery and Other `[V]`; the API's live capture has exactly two kinds, `shopping` and `to_do` `[V]` (Contradiction 1). The three are offered because they are what the person at the wall is shown. In this phase the type only labels the list — the reference's Grocery-only buttons are Organize (AI aisle sorting) and Order (Instacart), both excluded — and Phase 6 may treat Grocery lists as the candidates for a recipe's ingredients. The reference's `default_grocery_list` flag is **not** reserved: its only purpose is the hard-wired destination the divergence ledger's row 7 already rejects, and Phase 6 can add a preferred-list marker if its picker wants one. `[OURS]`
3. **The two default lists are "Grocery List" and "To-Do List".** The device article says "Shopping" and "To-Dos" `[V]`; the live API has "Grocery List" and "To-Do List" `[V]`; the phone screenshot says "Grocery list" `[V-photo]`; every recipe article says "Add to Grocery List" `[V]` (Contradiction 2). The live labels win: they are what the product stores today, and "Grocery List" is the name the rest of the product talks about. Their colours are the live defaults, Sprout and Cyan `[V]`. They are ordinary lists, made once for a new household and never re-created, the way the Task Box's templates are seeded once. `[OURS]`
4. **Every list write is open to every punched-in Profile, and every write needs a punch-in.** Phase 1 reserved parent-only for profiles, labels, PINs, roles and settings; Phase 2 opened events to any punched-in Profile on the strength of that list; Phases 3 and 4 made creation parent-only because tasks and rewards carry stars. Lists carry nothing: the reference lets anyone at the device add, tick and reorder `[V]`, its Parental Lock covers events and tasks and never lists `[V](35089525796251)`, `[V](36824456433051)`, and a child adding to the grocery list is the feature. Ticking still needs a punch-in because every change here records its actor (Phase 1 FR-016) and the shipped model is "viewing is free, acting is attributed"; the operator's open question about *when* the punch-in is asked (recorded in Phase 4's plan) stands and is not re-decided here. `[OURS]`
5. **"Hide on Device" becomes Parents only.** The reference hides a list from its Calendar device so that gift and party lists stay a surprise `[V]`. It can only say "the device" because its device has no idea who is standing at it — the master map's divergence row 1 makes the same point about ticking. This project knows: a punch-in names a Profile and a role. So a hidden list is shown wherever a parent is punched in and nowhere else — on the wall it appears while Ana is punched in and leaves when her punch-in lapses, on her phone it is always there, and Cleo never sees it. Rejected: a per-device "show this list" switch (anyone at the wall could flip it, defeating the surprise); a "this device is the wall" flag (a second identity to manage, and nothing in the shipped shell is per-device but display preferences). Within the household this is a display rule under the shipped read model, not a security boundary — the data stays inside the household as every other row does (FR-539). `[OURS]`
6. **Checked items stay in place, and the Completed switch is per device, in the Filter sheet, on by default.** The device shows checked items greyed and struck through until cleared `[V]`, and hides them through the top-bar Filter `[V]`; the phone has a "Show Checked-off Items" control at the bottom `[V]` (Contradiction 4). This app is the device, so the switch joins the shipped Filter sheet, beside the Tasks switches, and defaults to shown as "Completed tasks" does. Un-checking is in no article and is provided because a mis-tap must be reversible `[?]`. `[OURS]`
7. **Clear Completed confirms first.** The reference clears in one step `[V]`. Constitution §VI requires a destructive action to confirm and say what will be lost; deleting an event, a task or a reward already confirms. The confirmation names the count ("Clear 3 completed items?") and the action itself remains one write. `[OURS]`
8. **An item is text, up to 200 characters, edited and deleted from a small sheet.** No source gives an item anything but its text `[V]`; the photographs show emoji and quantities typed into that text `[V-photo]`, and the mobile app has an "x" to delete `[V]`. Editing the text is undocumented `[?]` and provided because a typo on the wall must be fixable without deleting and retyping. The sheet follows the shipped details-sheet pattern; the "x" is not reproduced on a 76-unit row that already carries a 63-unit checkbox. The limit is ours; the reference's is unknown. `[OURS]`
9. **Sections are names on items; ungrouped first; ordered by first item; removal keeps the items.** The API stores a section as a string on each item with no section resource `[V]`, and the article says a section cannot exist empty `[V]`, so the spec keeps that shape rather than inventing a table. What no source says: where ungrouped items sit (first — they are the list as it was before anyone sectioned it), how sections are ordered (by their first item, so dragging orders sections as it orders items), how a name matches (trimmed, case-insensitive — "dairy " is "Dairy"), what removing a section does to its items (nothing — constitution §VI), and how folding is remembered (per device). New items land ungrouped, at the end. `[OURS]`
10. **The count badge counts unchecked items.** The badge is photographed `[V-photo]`; what it counts is not. Unchecked items are what a shopper needs to know; the phone's section headers count the same way. `[OURS]`
11. **Cards are one row on the shipped chassis; the partial-card peek is not reproduced.** The photographed device shows ≈3.4 cards with the fourth clipped `[V-photo]`, a free horizontal scroll. The shipped board chassis measures how many whole columns fit and pages by one on a swipe, and it is what every other board here does; a clipped card would be a second scrolling idiom for the same gesture. Cards never wrap into a second row — the reference's row is one row `[V]`, and two rows of 76-unit item rows would not read as lists. `[OURS]`
12. **No chip row; the `+` adds a list; items are added in the card.** The photographed Lists screen has no profile chip row `[V-photo]`, and lists are not per-Profile, so the chips would count nothing; the tab's shipped `showsChipRow` flag is turned off as Phase 3 did for Tasks. The `+` opens Add List `[V]`; items are added from the box at the top of every card `[V]`, so the create control is never ambiguous. `[OURS]`
13. **Reorder is the shipped press-and-hold machine, with the pointer.** The device's gesture is a long-press, space opening around the item, a small orange pointer to its left, then a drag `[V]`. Phase 3 shipped exactly this machine for routines (press-and-hold, lift, drag, keyboard alternative); it is applied to items, the pointer is drawn in the accent colour as described, and a drop across a section boundary carries the section change in the same write. One stored position per item, written once per drop, is Phase 1's fractional index. `[OURS]`
14. **Deleting a list confirms with its item count; Profile deletion touches no list.** Deleting a list is in no source `[?]`; a family needs it. Lists belong to the household, so a deleted Profile's lists and items stay and only the attribution is cleared, the way events survive (Phase 2). The thrice-amended Profile delete confirmation gains no fourth sentence: nothing is lost. `[OURS]`
15. **No celebration on a list.** Every documented celebration is for chores and rewards; nothing celebrates a ticked item or a finished list `[V-absence]`. Under the governing rule an absence is matched as an absence. Row changes still honour reduced motion. `[OURS]`
16. **Section names are up to 60 characters.** The reference's limit is unknown `[?]`; a header must fit a 495-unit card beside its count and chevron. `[OURS]`
17. **A list's place in the row is stored but not yet draggable.** The photographed row has an order that is not alphabetical `[V-photo]`; nothing says how it is set. New lists append; a later phase may add dragging a card, and needs no migration to do it. `[OURS]`

Smaller calls are carried by the requirement that makes them and each states its reasoning inline: FR-508 (the empty state never reveals hidden lists), FR-516 (the box refocuses), FR-529 (the match rule), FR-531 (fold state is the device's), FR-545 (nothing reserved for Meals).

## Contradictions in the research, and how each is resolved

Six places where the research sources disagree with each other. Each is recorded with both readings and the resolution, rather than averaged away or silently picked.

1. **Two list kinds or three?** The device article offers three List Types — To do, Grocery, Other `[V](37275069922971)`; the live API's `kind` has exactly two values, `shopping` and `to_do`, confirmed by live testing `[V](pyskylight)`. **Resolution**: the three the person is shown are offered; storage is this project's own; the type has no behaviour this phase, so nothing turns on the difference. `[OURS 2026-09-05 #2]`
2. **What are the default lists called?** "Shopping" and "To-Dos" `[V](37275069922971)`; "Grocery List" and "To-Do List" `[V](skylight-api)`; "Grocery list" `[V-photo]` (`shot05`) and "Grocery List" with "To-Do" `[V-photo]` (`gallery/07`); "Grocery List" as the name of the Grocery type's default and of every recipe-push destination `[V](37275069922971)`, `[V](42181628465435)`. Dossier 03 reads the first pair as one list under naming drift `[I]` and reconciles the To-Do names nowhere. **Resolution**: "Grocery List" and "To-Do List", the live labels. `[OURS 2026-09-05 #3]`
3. **Square or round checkboxes?** The device's list rows carry rounded squares `[V-photo]` (`gallery/07`, `pdp/08`; dossier 07: "Lists use a square; Tasks use a circle"); the phone's grocery list carries circles `[V-photo]` (`shot05`). **Resolution**: this app is the device; squares, at every size. `[OURS 2026-09-05 #6]`
4. **Where is the completed-items control?** The device: the top-bar Filter with a "Completed" toggle `[V](37275069922971)`; the phone: "Show Checked-off Items" at the bottom of the list `[V](360041476692)`; the phone's toolbar also shows a "Completed" button with an eye icon `[V-photo]` (`shot05`). **Resolution**: the shipped Filter sheet, as the device does; the phone's placement is not reproduced. `[OURS 2026-09-05 #6]`
5. **Where is a list's type changed?** The device article: tap the name or the menu to change Title, List Type or Color `[V](37275069922971)`; the multiple-grocery-lists article: a list is converted to Grocery "via the Mobile or Desktop App" `[V](47929314290203)`. **Resolution**: there is one app here; the type is edited wherever the list is edited. `[OURS 2026-09-05 #2]`
6. **Is there an "Add item" box on the card?** The device article says one "sits at the top of every list" `[V](37275069922971)`; the photographed cards start with their first item row and show no input `[V-photo]` (`gallery/07`, `pdp/08`), and the phone adds through its `+` `[V-photo]` (`shot05`). **Resolution**: the box, as documented — the photographs are marketing renders of filled lists, and a documented control outranks its absence from a still. `[OURS 2026-09-05 #12]`

Two further disagreements are between the research and this project rather than within the research, and are resolved in the Assumptions: the reference's one-step Clear Completed against constitution §VI (Assumption 7), and the reference's device-scoped "Hide on Device" against this project's identity model (Assumption 5). The master map's `[V]` on the "Add section" footer with a count and a chevron is a photograph reading (dossier 03 marks the affordance `[UNKNOWN]`; dossier 07 photographs "Add section (0) ⌃"); this spec calls it `[V-photo]` and gives the footer its behaviour in FR-503 and FR-528.
