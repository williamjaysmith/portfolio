## Clarifications

### Session 2026-09-04

- Q: Does Phase 3 carry the whole Tasks **and** Rewards surface? → A: **Tasks only.** Stars, the star ledger, reward cards, redeem/unredeem and the celebrations are a later phase. A task field that exists only to serve rewards — a task's star value — is **reserved** in storage now, the way Phase 2 reserved the countdown flag, so the rewards phase needs no migration; nothing in this phase reads, shows or edits it. FR-329, FR-375, Assumption 1.
- Q: What does the Tasks tab show? → A: **A column per Profile, showing one day** — the reference's own layout — with Previous / Today / Next. FR-301, FR-303, Assumption 2.
- Q: May anyone tick a task, as on the reference? → A: **No. A punch-in is required to resolve anything**, and the completion records the punched-in actor. A `member` may complete their own tasks and claim up-for-grabs; a `parent` may complete anything. FR-350, FR-351, Assumption 3.
- Q: One repeat mode or both? → A: **Both — Scheduled Date and Completed Date** — because the reference has both. FR-339 to FR-346, Assumption 4.
- Q: Late carry-forward and skip? → A: **Both ship**, per the reference. FR-356 to FR-364, Assumption 5.
- Q: How many profile columns on a phone? → A: **However many fit, measured — the calendar's own rule.** Not a fixed one. A narrow phone may still land on one, but the number is a consequence of the space rather than a constant to be wrong about, and the same measurement serves every width. FR-396, Assumption 25.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

The same example household as Phase 2, so the two specifications read as one product and the scenarios can be run by hand: profiles **Ana** (parent), **Ben** (parent) and **Cleo** (child), plus the Label **Bin day**. Household timezone `America/Chicago`; the week starts on Sunday.

### User Story 1 — The family sees today and ticks it off (Priority: P1)

The tablet sits on the kitchen counter showing today. Three columns, one per person, each with their morning routine at the top and their chores below. Cleo comes down, taps her face, types her PIN, and taps the circle beside "Brush teeth" — the card darkens into her colour and the ring at the top of her column fills a little further. She taps "Make bed" too. Ben, already punched in from earlier, ticks "Take out trash" in his own column. Nobody has typed anything or opened a form.

**Why this priority**: Reading today and ticking it off is what the Tasks tab is for, all day, every day. Everything else in this phase puts tasks *into* this board, so the board and its completion path must be right before anything may fill it. A board that shows the wrong day, the wrong person's tasks, or lets the wrong person tick them is worse than no board.

**Independent Test**: Seed one day with, per profile, a morning routine in two slots, an all-day chore and an anytime chore. Load `/family/tasks` on a tablet-landscape viewport with nobody punched in and read every column, its ring and its count against the seed by hand. Punch in as Cleo and resolve one of hers; attempt one of Ben's. Punch in as Ana and resolve one of Cleo's.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** Ana, Ben and Cleo are all shown on the Tasks tab, **When** the tab opens, **Then** there is one column per profile, each headed by that profile's avatar and name, each holding only that profile's tasks for the displayed day.
2. **Given** Cleo has a routine "Brush teeth" in Morning and Evening and a chore "Feed the cat" due today, **When** her column renders at 08:00, **Then** the Morning occurrence of "Brush teeth" and "Feed the cat" are both visible, and the Evening occurrence is hidden until the Evening toggle is switched on.
3. **Given** nobody is punched in, **When** someone taps the circle on Cleo's "Brush teeth", **Then** the "Who's here?" prompt appears before anything is recorded, and dismissing it leaves the card incomplete.
4. **Given** Cleo is punched in, **When** she taps the circle on her own "Brush teeth", **Then** the circle becomes a checkmark, the whole card darkens from her colour at 40 % to her colour in full, and her column's completion ring and count both advance by one.
5. **Given** Cleo is punched in, **When** she taps the circle on Ben's "Take out trash", **Then** nothing is recorded, the card stays incomplete, and a message says the task is Ben's and only Ben or a parent may complete it.
6. **Given** Ana (a parent) is punched in, **When** she taps the circle on Cleo's "Make bed", **Then** it completes, and the stored resolution records **Ana** as the actor and **Cleo** as the profile credited.
7. **Given** a completed card, **When** its checkmark is tapped, **Then** it returns to incomplete, the card returns to the 40 % tint, and the column's ring and count go back by one.
8. **Given** any task card, **When** its body — not the circle — is tapped, **Then** a details view opens showing the title, emoji, description, who it is for, its schedule, and the actions that apply to it.
9. **Given** nobody has punched in, **When** a person walks past and reads the board, **Then** every column and every card is visible and no PIN is requested at any point.
10. **Given** the board is open and untouched at 23:59, **When** the clock passes midnight, **Then** the board shows the new day and every column's count is the new day's — with no reload and no interaction.
11. **Given** twenty tasks in Cleo's column, **When** it renders, **Then** all twenty are reachable by scrolling inside that column, the page itself never scrolls sideways, and every circle is at least 44×44 points.
12. **Given** an anytime chore "Sort the recycling" with no date and no time, **When** the board renders on any day, **Then** it appears in Cleo's Chores section, is never marked late, and stays there every day until it is completed.
13. **Given** Ben has ten occurrences on the displayed day and has completed three of them, **When** his column header renders, **Then** the ring is filled three tenths and the count reads three of ten.

### User Story 2 — A parent sets the household's chores and routines (Priority: P2)

Ana punches in and adds "Take out trash" for Ben, every Tuesday, due 18:00. She adds a routine for Cleo — "Brush teeth", morning and evening, every day, with Track Habit on. Then a chore she is tired of chasing: "Clean the bathroom", which should come round two weeks after it was last actually done, not two weeks after it was last scheduled — so she picks Completed Date, after 2 weeks. She saves "Homework" into the Task Box while she is at it, because it will be assigned again next term.

**Why this priority**: A board nobody can fill is empty. This is the whole write surface — the two task types, the four chore sub-types, both repeat modes, assignment, the Task Box — and it is P2 only because it writes into the board Story 1 draws.

**Independent Test**: Seed three profiles and an empty board. Through the form, create one timed chore, one all-day chore, one anytime chore, one scheduled-date repeat with an interval greater than one, one completed-date repeat, and one routine in two slots on three weekdays. Read the produced occurrences for the next month against each rule by hand. Convert a chore into a routine and back. Attempt each write as Cleo and confirm it is refused.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** Ana is punched in, **When** she creates "Take out trash" for Ben with a date and the time 18:00, **Then** it appears in Ben's column on that date as a timed chore, and the stored task records Ana as its creator.
2. **Given** the create form, **When** a date is set and the Time toggle is left off, **Then** the saved chore is an all-day chore — completable at any point that day and not tied to a clock time.
3. **Given** the create form, **When** neither a date nor a time is set, **Then** the saved chore is an anytime chore: no deadline, present on the board every day until completed, and never late.
4. **Given** the create form with an empty title, **When** save is attempted, **Then** it is refused with a message against the title, the other entries are preserved, and nothing is stored.
5. **Given** the create form, **When** no profile is selected and Up for Grabs is off, **Then** save is refused — every task needs at least one profile, or the Up for Grabs toggle instead.
6. **Given** the create form, **When** the assignment picker is opened, **Then** it lists Profiles only; the Label "Bin day" is not offered, because a Label cannot complete anything.
7. **Given** the type toggle set to Routine, **When** the form renders, **Then** the Date and Time fields are gone and are replaced by a repeat (every N days, or weekly on chosen weekdays) and a Morning / Afternoon / Evening selection, of which at least one must be chosen.
8. **Given** an existing chore, **When** it is edited and its type is switched to Routine, **Then** the form requires a repeat, **its weekdays where the repeat is weekly**, and at least one time of day before it will save, and afterwards the task behaves as a routine in every respect — an "every 2 days" routine with no weekdays saves, because weekdays belong to the weekly repeat (FR-333, FR-334, US2-7).
9. **Given** the repeat set to Scheduled Date, every 2 weeks, on Tuesday, starting Tuesday 2026-09-08, **When** it is saved, **Then** occurrences fall on 8 and 22 September and 6 October, and none falls on 15 September.
10. **Given** the repeat set to Scheduled Date with "Repeats until 2026-12-15", **When** the board is read past that date, **Then** no occurrence appears after 15 December.
11. **Given** "Clean the bathroom" set to Completed Date, after 2 weeks, **When** it is saved, **Then** exactly one occurrence exists — the first one — and no later occurrence exists anywhere until that one is completed.
12. **Given** that chore completed on Thursday 2026-09-10, **When** the board is read afterwards, **Then** exactly one new occurrence exists, due 2026-09-24, and it did not exist before the completion.
13. **Given** a routine "Brush teeth" every day, Morning and Evening, **When** the board renders on any day, **Then** that routine contributes exactly two occurrences to that day — one in each section — each separately completable.
14. **Given** Ana saves a new task with "Save to task box" ticked, **When** the Task Box is opened afterwards, **Then** a template with that title, emoji and type is listed there.
15. **Given** Cleo (a member) is punched in, **When** she attempts to create, edit or delete any task, **Then** it is refused — creating and changing tasks is parent-only — and the refusal holds when the request bypasses the interface.
16. **Given** the household timezone is `America/Chicago` and a daily chore due at 02:30, **When** the board is read on 2026-03-08, the date the clock jumps 02:00 → 03:00, **Then** the chore appears exactly once that day, at the first valid time; and on 2026-11-01, when 01:00–02:00 happens twice, a chore due 01:30 appears exactly once.

### User Story 3 — When life intervenes (Priority: P3)

Cleo is ill on Tuesday, so Ana opens her evening routine and taps Skip — it does not count against her, and her streak survives. Ben forgets the bins entirely; on Wednesday morning "Take out trash" is sitting on his column marked late, with the day it was due. He does it Thursday and ticks it then, and the record says Thursday. Meanwhile nobody has claimed "Empty the dishwasher", sitting in the Up for Grabs column at the left; Cleo taps it, picks her own face, and it moves into her column.

**Why this priority**: Late, skip and up-for-grabs are what makes the board survive a real week rather than a demonstration week. They are P3 because each one is a state on top of the tasks Story 2 creates and the completion path Story 1 provides.

**Independent Test**: Seed a repeating chore due two days ago and unresolved, a routine on a repeat, and an up-for-grabs chore. Skip and unskip an occurrence and compare the column's count and the streak before and after. Complete the late chore and check on which day it is recorded and how the previous day now reads. Claim the up-for-grabs chore from two devices at once.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** "Take out trash" was due Tuesday 18:00 and was not completed, **When** the board is read on Wednesday, **Then** it appears in Ben's column marked late, showing the date it was due, and it counts toward Wednesday's total.
2. **Given** that late chore, **When** Ben completes it on Thursday, **Then** the record says it was completed on **Thursday**, not on the Tuesday it was due; Thursday's count includes it; and navigating back to Tuesday shows that occurrence as completed late.
3. **Given** a late chore left unresolved, **When** the board is read on each of the intervening days, **Then** it appears on its own scheduled day and on today, and not repeated on every day in between.
4. **Given** an anytime chore, **When** any amount of time passes, **Then** it is never marked late — there is no deadline to miss.
5. **Given** Cleo's daily routine and Ana punched in, **When** she opens Tuesday's occurrence and taps Skip, **Then** it disappears from the column, is excluded from both the count and the ring, and Cleo's streak on that routine is unbroken.
6. **Given** that skipped occurrence and the Skipped filter switched on, **When** the board renders, **Then** the occurrence is visible and marked skipped, and tapping it offers **Unskip**, which returns it to unresolved.
7. **Given** a one-off chore that does not repeat, **When** its details are opened, **Then** no Skip action is offered — skip exists for routines and repeating chores only.
8. **Given** "Clean the bathroom" in Completed Date mode with a two-week delay, **When** its open occurrence is skipped on 2026-09-10, **Then** the next occurrence is scheduled for 2026-09-24 — a skip advances the cycle rather than ending the chore.
9. **Given** the up-for-grabs chore "Empty the dishwasher" and Cleo punched in, **When** she opens it, selects her own profile and completes it, **Then** it leaves the Up for Grabs column, appears completed in her column, and the resolution credits Cleo.
10. **Given** that completed up-for-grabs chore, **When** it is marked incomplete again, **Then** it returns to the Up for Grabs column and belongs to nobody.
11. **Given** two devices open on the same up-for-grabs chore, **When** both claim it within the same second, **Then** exactly one claim is recorded, the other is refused with a message naming who got there first, and both screens show the same result within five seconds.
12. **Given** an up-for-grabs chore, **When** anyone punched in skips it, **Then** that occurrence is skipped for the whole household, since it belongs to nobody until it is claimed.
13. **Given** Cleo is punched in and taps a card in the Up for Grabs column, **When** she selects Ben as the profile to credit, **Then** it is refused — a member may claim only for themselves; a parent may credit anyone.
14. **Given** the board is open and untouched at 23:59 with a chore due today still unresolved, **When** the clock passes midnight, **Then** that chore is on the new day's board marked late, showing the date it was due, with no reload and no interaction.

Ana wants only what is left: she opens the filter sheet and hides completed tasks. Later she hides Ben's column on her phone while she checks Cleo's homework. At the top of Cleo's column the ring says how far through the day she is. Beside "Brush teeth" a small badge counts eleven days in a row. When Ana needs to add homework again she opens the Task Box, finds it in the routines section, taps it, and only has to say who and when.

**Why this priority**: Filters, search, streaks and the Task Box each make the board faster or clearer, but none of them is the board — the columns and their counters belong to Story 1, because a column without its ring is not the board a family reads. These come last because each reads state the earlier stories create.

**Independent Test**: Seed a day with completed, late and skipped occurrences across three profiles, a routine with Track Habit on and a run of completions behind it, and the seeded Task Box. Toggle each of the five filters and check what appears, and that no column's ring or count moves while they are toggled. Type into the search box and check what is left. Add a task from a template. Break the streak by leaving a day unresolved and check the badge.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the filter sheet, **When** "Completed tasks" is switched off, **Then** completed cards disappear from every column and each column's count is unchanged, because the counters describe the day and not the filtered view.
2. **Given** the filter sheet, **When** "Late chores" is switched off, **Then** chores carried forward from earlier days disappear and chores due today remain.
3. **Given** the filter sheet, **When** "Up for Grabs" is switched off, **Then** the left-most column disappears and the remaining profile columns are unchanged.
4. **Given** the filter sheet on Ana's phone, **When** Ben is hidden, **Then** Ben's column disappears on that phone, is still there on the tablet, and is still there on the phone after Ben is shown again — nothing about Ben's tasks changed.
5. **Given** Ben's column reads three of ten and one of the seven still outstanding is skipped, **When** the header re-renders, **Then** the count reads three of nine — a skipped occurrence leaves the total.
6. **Given** a routine with Track Habit on, completed on each of the last eleven days, **When** its card renders, **Then** a badge with a lightning-bolt icon beside its name reads eleven.
7. **Given** that streak and a day on which the routine is skipped, **When** the next day's board renders, **Then** the streak still reads eleven — a skip protects it without advancing it.
8. **Given** that streak and a day that ends with the routine unresolved, **When** the next day's board renders, **Then** the badge has reset.
9. **Given** the Task Box on a household that has never edited it, **When** it opens, **Then** it lists exactly the seventeen seeded templates in a Chores section and a Routines section, with a search box that filters them by title.
10. **Given** the routine template "Homework", **When** Ana taps it, **Then** the create form opens pre-filled with that title, emoji and type, and with assignment and scheduling empty and still required.
11. **Given** a template, **When** it is edited, **Then** its title, emoji and type may be changed and no star value is shown or asked for.
12. **Given** a template, **When** it is deleted, **Then** a warning says the deletion cannot be undone, and tasks already created from it are untouched.
13. **Given** the board holding "Brush teeth" and "Take out trash", **When** "trash" is typed into the Tasks tab's search box, **Then** only "Take out trash" is left on the board, every column's ring and count is unchanged, and clearing the box restores every card.
14. **Given** a 390×844 phone, **When** the tab opens, **Then** one profile column fills the width, swiping left and right moves between profiles including the Up for Grabs column, and no control is smaller than 44×44 points.

### Edge Cases

- **A late chore completed days after it was due** — the resolution is recorded on the day it was ticked, not the day it was due `[V](36846381293979)`. The occurrence keeps its scheduled date as its identity, so the day it was due shows it completed-late and the day it was ticked counts it.
- **Completed-date mode when the completion is undone** — the occurrence scheduled by that completion is withdrawn and the original occurrence returns to unresolved. If that next occurrence has already itself been resolved, the undo is refused with a message saying so, because withdrawing it would destroy a real record.
- **A chore whose assignee is deleted** — the assignment goes with the profile, along with that profile's own resolutions of it. A task with another assignee survives, minus that person. A task left with nobody assigned is deleted with the profile: a chore becomes up-for-grabs only by an explicit choice, never by attrition. The delete confirmation says how many tasks are affected, alongside the event count Phase 2 already put there.
- **An up-for-grabs chore claimed by two people at once** — exactly one claim is recorded. The second is refused with a message naming the profile credited, and the card moves to that profile's column on both screens within seconds. Nothing is merged and there is no reserved-but-not-done state.
- **A routine crossing a time-of-day boundary** — an occurrence belongs to the slot it was generated for, not to the clock. A Morning occurrence is still a Morning occurrence at 22:00: it is still completable, it still counts toward the day, and it appears whenever the Morning section is shown. Nothing migrates between sections as the day passes.
- **A skipped occurrence of a repeating chore** — the skip is per occurrence and per assignee: skipping Cleo's Tuesday does not skip Ben's. It leaves the count and the ring, does not break a streak, is hidden unless the Skipped filter is on, and Unskip restores it exactly. Later occurrences are unaffected.
- **DST against a timed chore** — the household timezone rules of Phase 2 apply unchanged: a due time that does not exist on a date because the clock jumped forward lands on the first valid time that date; a due time that happens twice uses the first instant; the occurrence appears exactly once either way.
- **A task assigned to two people** — it is one task with two assignees, and each of them has their own occurrence, their own resolution and their own streak. Editing it edits it for both. Deleting it deletes it for both.
- **A chore whose repeat is missed several times in Scheduled Date mode** — the schedule does not shift `[V](36846381293979)`. Three missed Tuesdays are three late occurrences alongside the fourth Tuesday's fresh one, each separately completable.
- **A late chore that is never completed** — it stops carrying forward onto today 28 days after its scheduled date and remains visible only on its own day. Without a bound the board would accumulate a year of forgotten chores.
- **A profile hidden from the Tasks tab household-wide** — the column disappears for everyone and the profile is no longer offered when assigning a task; existing tasks are untouched and reappear with the column when it is switched back on.
- **The board rolls over at midnight while a form is open** — the open form keeps what the person entered; only the board behind it advances.
- **Two people resolve the same occurrence at once** — one resolution exists; the second write finds it already resolved and reports the state rather than creating a duplicate.
- **The device is offline when a tick is committed** — the change is refused with a clear message. Nothing is shown as complete that is not stored, and no write is queued.
- **A task deleted on another device while its details are open here** — the action is refused with a message saying the task no longer exists, and the details close rather than recreating it.
- **An edit that destroys an occurrence a resolution keys to** — changing a due date, a repeat rule, an interval or an end date, or converting a chore into a routine or back, can leave a stored resolution keyed to a date or a slot the task no longer produces. Nothing is deleted: the resolution is kept, and simply not surfaced where its occurrence no longer exists. A type conversion is permitted on a task that already carries resolutions, on the same terms — no completion history is ever destroyed by an edit.
- **A column with nothing in it** — the column still renders, with its header, its ring at zero of zero, and an empty state; a person with no tasks does not vanish from the board.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**The Tasks tab and its columns**

- **FR-301**: The system MUST present the Tasks tab as one column per Profile, each column holding that Profile's routines and chores for one displayed day. `[V](36846381293979 — "Skylight Calendar shows a list of Tasks with for each Profile in columns"; 45761249913499 — "The Tasks view displays the current tasks for each Profile by day")` `[OURS 2026-09-04 #2]` for shipping the day view alone.
- **FR-302**: The system MUST group a column's contents into four sections — **Morning**, **Afternoon**, **Evening** and **Chores** — with routines in the time-of-day sections they were given and every chore in the Chores section. `[V](36846381293979 — the four toggles show and hide exactly these groupings)`; that chores occupy a section of their own rather than a time of day follows from a chore having no time of day at all `[V](36846381293979 — "Chores do not need to happen at a particular time of the day")`.
- **FR-303**: The system MUST offer **Previous**, **Today** and **Next** controls that move the board one day at a time, and MUST open on today. `[V](45761249913499)` — all three of the reference's documented navigation schemes agree on this trio; the opening day is `[?]` and is decided `[OURS 2026-09-04 #2]`.
- **FR-304**: The system MUST head each column with that Profile's avatar, its name, and a completion progress indicator, and MUST fill the header panel itself with that Profile's colour at **20 %** — the third rung of the same tint ladder the task cards use at 40 % and 100 % (FR-349). `[V](36846381293979 — "icons at the top of each column" tracking the completion rate; the header carries the profile name)`; the avatar is `[V-photo]`, and so is the 20 % panel — the ladder *100 % completed card · 40 % incomplete card · 20 % column header panel* was solved numerically for alpha across four profiles and checks within ±3 per channel.
- **FR-305**: The system MUST express that indicator as **both** a circular progress ring around the profile's avatar and a plain completed-of-total count, where the total is every one of that Profile's occurrences on the displayed day — routines in each of their slots, chores due that day, undated anytime chores, chores carried forward late (FR-356), and any up-for-grabs occurrence that Profile has claimed, which joins that Profile's total and completed count at the moment of the claim and leaves the Up for Grabs count (FR-308) — **less any occurrence that is skipped**, and the completed number is those resolved complete. The ring is `[V-search](8204862253339 — "as a person marks a chore as complete, a circular progress bar will fill around the category")`; the count's pill form is `[V-photo]` and its literal string is `[?]`; that a skipped occurrence leaves the total is `[V](36846381293979 — "no longer counted in the total tasks or the circular completion bar")`; the rest of the denominator is `[?]` and is decided `[OURS 2026-09-04 #8]`. See Contradiction 3.
- **FR-306**: The system MUST provide four round toggles in each column header — **Morning**, **Afternoon**, **Evening**, **Chores** — that show and hide their sections at any time, and MUST select a time-of-day section automatically by the clock: **midnight–noon Morning, noon–18:00 Afternoon, 18:00–midnight Evening**. The active toggle MUST draw a **full-circumference** ring in the Profile's accent and an inactive one a partial ring faded to 40 %; the **Chores** toggle MUST always be drawn as a distinct **full** ring — that clause describes its ring's **geometry only**, because Chores is not a time of day and never draws the partial arc, and the same on/off treatment still applies to it: the Profile's accent when on, the same full ring faded to 40 % when off. A Chores toggle that is off MUST be visibly off, never identical to one that is on and readable only by its section's absence (FR-307). `[V](36846381293979, 35336169688475 — the same three windows in two sources; the toggles "show/hide those routines at any time", overriding the automatic selection)`; the ring states are `[V-photo]` (07 §4.10).

The field is `[V](36846381293979; release-log — "[Feature] Task Description", 2026-05-19; skylight-api — `description` on the chore resource)`; where it is displayed is `[?]` and is decided `[OURS 2026-09-04]`.
- **FR-322**: The system MUST require every task to be assigned to at least one Profile, unless it is marked Up for Grabs, which is assigned to nobody. `[V](36846381293979 — "All tasks need to be assigned to at least one Profile"; skylight-api — an up-for-grabs chore carries no category)`
- **FR-323**: The system MUST offer only Profiles as assignees, never Labels, and MUST refuse a task assigned to a Label at the data store as well as in the interface. That chores exist on non-profile categories in the reference is `[V]`+`[I]` (`skylight-api — a `linked_to_profile` filter implies the other kind exists`); refusing it here is `[OURS 2026-09-04 #13]` — a Label has no punch-in identity, so nobody could ever complete its tasks.
- **FR-324**: The system MUST hold a multi-assigned task as **one** task with several assignees, each of whom gets their own occurrences, their own resolutions and their own streak; an edit or a delete MUST reach every assignee. That a task may be "assigned to one or more people" is `[V](36846381293979)`; the reference achieves it by creating one chore row per profile `[V](skylight-api — `create_multiple` "creates one chore per profile in the array")`, which this phase deliberately does not copy `[OURS 2026-09-04 #11]`. See Contradiction 6.
- **FR-325**: The system MUST derive a chore's sub-type from its date and time fields rather than from a separate choice, producing exactly four behaviours: **Timed** (a date and a time), **All-day** (a date, no time), **Anytime** (neither), and **Late** (a Timed or All-day chore past its date and unresolved, arrived at automatically). `[V](32082293222043 — the four sub-types and their definitions; 00-master-map §4.3 — they fall out of the fields, not an enum)`
- **FR-326**: The system MUST treat a chore's due time as a wall-clock time in the household's timezone, and MUST apply the same daylight-saving rules the calendar already uses: a time that does not exist on a date lands on the first valid time that date; a time that happens twice uses the first instant; the occurrence appears exactly once either way. `[P2]` (002 FR-234 to FR-236, FR-284), applied to tasks `[OURS 2026-09-04]`.
- **FR-327**: The system MUST make an all-day chore completable at any point on its date and MUST NOT attach a clock time to it. `[V](32082293222043 — "completable any time that day")`
- **FR-328**: The system MUST give an anytime chore exactly one undated occurrence that stays on the board every day until it is completed, MUST count it toward the day's total, and MUST NEVER mark it late at any point, however long it goes unresolved — it has no deadline to miss. `[V](32082293222043 — no deadline, "still counts toward daily objectives"; only Timed and All-day chores go late)`
- **FR-329**: The system MUST reserve a star value in storage on every task **and on every Task Box template**, while presenting no star interface at all in this phase — nothing reads either field, shows it, edits it or totals it. The fields are `[V](skylight-api — `reward_points`, an integer or null, on the chore resource and again on `task_box_item`)`; the deferral is `[OURS 2026-09-04 #1]`, the same pattern as Phase 2's reserved countdown flag `[P2]`, and it exists so the rewards phase inherits both shapes without a migration.
- **FR-330**: The system MUST present the create and edit form in this order: title; emoji; description; assignment; task type; the type's own scheduling fields; Up for Grabs (chores only); Track Habit (routines only); "Save to task box". The field *set* is `[V](36846381293979 — the documented creation sequence)`; the order shown here follows that sequence and is otherwise `[OURS 2026-09-04]`.
- **FR-331**: The system MUST apply every edit of a task to the whole task, and MUST NOT offer per-occurrence overrides of a task's title, time, description or assignment. `[?]` — nothing in any source shows a single occurrence of a task being edited; the only per-occurrence acts the reference documents are complete, skip and delete-this-instance `[V](36846381293979)` — resolved `[OURS 2026-09-04 #19]`. This is a deliberate difference from Phase 2's events, which do carry per-occurrence overrides.
- **FR-332**: The system MUST retain every stored resolution whose occurrence an edit destroys — a changed due date, repeat rule, interval or **Repeats until** date, or a conversion between the two task types (FR-318), any of which can strand a resolution on a date or a slot the task no longer produces — surfacing such a resolution only where its occurrence still exists, and MUST NOT refuse an edit or a type conversion because the task already carries resolutions. `[?]` — no source describes what becomes of a completion whose occurrence an edit removes; resolved `[OURS 2026-09-04]`. A completion is a record of something a child actually did, and no change to a schedule may destroy it.

**Routines**

- **FR-333**: The system MUST give routines no date and no due time: their timing is a repeat, **its weekdays where that repeat is weekly**, and a set of times of day. The weekday set is a field of the *weekly* repeat and not a separate requirement of every routine — an "every N days" routine (FR-334) carries no weekdays and MUST still save, which is the reading FR-334, US2-7 and the validation schema all take. That a routine has none of the timing options a chore has, and that converting a task into a routine forces a repetition pattern, a day and a time of day, is `[V](35340626864027 — "because Routines do not have timing options available to Chores"; 36846381293979 — "Routines occur around the same time of day, which repeat on a daily basis and can be set for morning, afternoon, and evening")`. That this list is **exhaustive** — that weekday plus time of day is the whole of a routine's timing — is the inference `behaviour.md` grades `[I]`, adopted here as a decision `[OURS 2026-09-04 #26]`. See Contradiction 10.
- **FR-334**: The system MUST offer a routine a repeat of **every N days** or **weekly on chosen weekdays**, with more than one weekday allowed. `[V](36846381293979 — "Every day", "Every 2 days"; "Routines can be assigned to multiple days")`
- **FR-335**: The system MUST let a routine carry more than one time of day and MUST generate one separately completable occurrence per chosen time of day on each matching date. `[V](36846381293979 — "Routines can be assigned to multiple times of day"; skylight-api — `instance_time` is required on completion and "is exactly what distinguishes two occurrences of a chore that repeats morning and evening")`
- **FR-336**: The system MUST fix an occurrence to the time of day it was generated for, so it neither moves to another section nor expires as the clock passes that window. `[?]` — resolved `[OURS 2026-09-04]`; a morning routine done at 22:00 is still the morning routine.
- **FR-337**: The system MUST offer a **Track Habit** switch when creating or editing a routine, and only a routine, and MUST NOT offer habit tracking on a chore on any surface. `[V](36846381293979, 50672917447067 — the toggle is documented on Routines; it is never stated to exist on chores)`; that its absence on chores is a prohibition rather than an omission is `[?]` and is decided `[OURS 2026-09-04 #9]`.
- **FR-338**: The system MUST NOT let a routine be marked Up for Grabs, and MUST NOT carry a missed routine occurrence forward to another day. `[V](36846381293979 — "Only chores can be set as Up For Grabs"; 32082293222043 — only Timed and All-day chores carry forward)`

**The two repeat modes**

- **FR-339**: The system MUST offer chores a **Repeats** switch with two mutually exclusive modes — **Scheduled Date** and **Completed Date**. `[V](36846381293979 — "If you would like the chore to be automatically rescheduled, enable Repeats", with the two modes described separately; release-log — "Repeat a Chore After Completion", 2026-08-03)`
- **FR-340**: The system MUST offer Scheduled Date mode the fields **Every** [N] and a **unit of time** (day, week or month), a **position within the unit** (which weekday, or which day of the month), and an optional **Repeats until** end date. `[V](36846381293979)` Implementation note: the position is a **submitted** field only for the weekly unit (the weekdays); for the monthly unit the day of the month is shown **read-only, derived from the chosen date**, following the shipped events precedent — a monthly repeat starting on the 8th recurs on the 8th, and there is no separate control to disagree with the start date. `[OURS 2026-09-04]`
- **FR-341**: The system MUST make a Scheduled Date repeat absolute: an occurrence that is missed MUST NOT delay or shift the next one, and the missed occurrence MUST remain outstanding alongside it as a late chore. The rule is `[V](36846381293979 — "The chore will always repeat after the set interval. The new chore will not be delayed if the earlier scheduled chore was not completed")`; that the missed one becomes a late chore beside it is `[I]`, the composition of two verified facts, adopted `[OURS 2026-09-04]`.
- **FR-342**: The system MUST offer Completed Date mode the choice **After → Immediately** or **After → Custom** with a [N] and a unit of time, plus the same optional **Repeats until**. `[V](36846381293979)`
- **FR-343**: The system MUST make a Completed Date chore have **at most one open occurrence at a time**: the next occurrence MUST NOT exist until the current one is resolved, and when it is, the next one is due that resolution's date plus the configured delay — "Immediately" meaning the same day. An open Completed Date occurrence whose date has passed is late by the ordinary rule (FR-325), carries forward under FR-356, and holds the cursor where it is: no next occurrence appears while it is outstanding, however late it becomes. **It is the one occurrence exempt from FR-357's twenty-eight-day bound** — see Contradiction resolution 11: bounding it would put the cursor on no reachable screen and strand the chore for ever. `[V](36846381293979 — "rescheduled only after it has been completed")` for the rule; the delay counting from the resolution date, the interaction with carry-forward, and a skip advancing the cycle the same way (FR-362) are all `[?]` and are decided `[OURS 2026-09-04 #15]`.
- **FR-344**: The system MUST withdraw the occurrence a Completed Date completion scheduled when that completion is undone, returning the original occurrence to unresolved; and MUST refuse the undo, with a message, when the scheduled occurrence has itself already been resolved. `[?]` — resolved `[OURS 2026-09-04 #15]`.
- **FR-345**: The system MUST support repeat intervals greater than one — "every 2 days", "every 3 weeks", "every 2 months" — in both a routine's repeat and a chore's Scheduled Date repeat, for whole intervals from 1 to 99. `[V](36846381293979 — "Every [N]" with a unit of time; "Every 2 days" in the routine frequency picker)`; the upper bound is `[OURS 2026-09-04 #14]`. This widens the recurrence grammar Phase 2 shipped, which accepts an interval of one only — see Assumption 14 for the cost.
- **FR-346**: The system MUST offer an optional **Repeats until** end date in both chore repeat modes and on routines, and MUST NOT offer a count-of-occurrences limit. The end date on the two chore modes is `[V](36846381293979)`; no source shows one on a routine, so extending it there is `[OURS 2026-09-04]` — a routine that should stop at the end of term needs an end date exactly as a chore does, and refusing it would make the only way to end a routine deleting it. Declining a count follows Phase 2's shipped grammar `[P2]` `[OURS 2026-09-04]`.
- **FR-347**: The system MUST offer, when deleting an occurrence of a repeating task, the scopes the reference offers for that type: for a **repeating chore**, this occurrence / all future / all; for a **routine**, all future / all. It MUST implement "all future" as ending the task's repeat immediately before the chosen occurrence, keeping every earlier occurrence and its resolutions, and MUST record a "this occurrence" delete as a skipped occurrence (FR-364). The scopes and their asymmetry are `[V](36846381293979 — the two delete menus differ exactly this way; skylight-api — the scope enum is `this` / `this_and_future` / `all`)`; how each is carried out is `[?]` and is resolved `[OURS 2026-09-04 #20]`. A routine's single occurrence is removed with **Skip** instead (FR-359), which is why it needs no "this occurrence" scope, and because tasks carry no per-occurrence overrides (FR-331) truncating needs no series split of the kind Phase 2 required.

The card state change is `[V-photo]` (sampled across three profiles in the reference's own photography, and recorded in the master map); its duration and easing are `[?]` `[OURS 2026-09-04 #21]`; the reduced-motion rule is `[P1]` for declarative animation and `[OURS 2026-09-04]` for anything script-driven.

**Late and skip**

- **FR-356**: The system MUST carry a Timed or All-day chore that was not completed by its date forward automatically, keeping it outstanding and counting toward the day's total until it is completed, subject to the twenty-eight-day bound in FR-357. `[V](32082293222043 — "not completed by its due date automatically carries forward to subsequent days"; persists and keeps counting until marked complete)`
- **FR-357**: The system MUST show a carried-forward occurrence on **today's** board and on **its own scheduled day**, and MUST NOT repeat it on every day in between; and MUST stop carrying it forward onto today **28 days** after its scheduled date, after which it remains visible only on its own day. The bound is a **strict inequality measured in whole days from the scheduled date**: an occurrence scheduled 27 days ago is still carried onto today, one scheduled 28 days ago is not, and one scheduled 29 days ago is not — day 28 is the first day it has stopped. **One occurrence is exempt: the open occurrence of a Completed Date chore (FR-343), which is the cursor itself** — Contradiction resolution 11. `[?]` — the sources say only that it carries forward and persists; resolved `[OURS 2026-09-04 #7]`.
- **FR-358**: The system MUST mark a carried-forward occurrence as late and show the date it was due, using a treatment distinct from the destructive-action colour. - **FR-361**: The system MUST offer **Unskip** on a skipped occurrence, returning it to unresolved and back into the day's total, and MUST show skipped occurrences only when the **Skipped tasks** filter is on. `[V](36846381293979)`
- **FR-362**: The system MUST advance a Completed Date chore's cycle when its open occurrence is skipped, scheduling the next occurrence from the skip date by the configured delay. `[?]` — resolved `[OURS 2026-09-04 #15]`; without this a skip would end the chore forever.
- **FR-363**: The system MUST record a skip per occurrence and per assignee, so skipping one person's occurrence leaves every other assignee's untouched; and MUST treat a skip of an unclaimed up-for-grabs occurrence as household-wide, since it belongs to nobody. Per-occurrence is `[I]` (it must be, for a routine that recurs) and per-assignee follows from FR-324; the up-for-grabs case is `[?]`; both are decided `[OURS 2026-09-04 #9]`.
- **FR-364**: The system MUST record a "this occurrence" delete of a repeating task as a skipped occurrence, so that one record covers both acts and no occurrence can be both deleted and skipped. `[?]` — resolved `[OURS 2026-09-04 #9]`, following Phase 2's precedent of recording a single-occurrence delete as a skip `[P2]`.

- **FR-367**: The system MUST let a punched-in person claim an up-for-grabs occurrence by opening it, choosing the Profile to credit, and completing it — after which it leaves the Up for Grabs column and appears completed in that Profile's column. `[V](36846381293979 — tap the task → tap a profile from the popup → Complete; the task then appears in that profile's column)`; who may credit whom is FR-351.

Composed literally, both hold and the chore is destroyed by inattention: after day 28 the open occurrence is on no reachable screen, nothing can resolve it, and because that occurrence **is** the cursor no later one can ever be scheduled — the only ways back are twenty-nine taps of **Previous** or an edit. **Resolution**: the open occurrence of a Completed Date chore is **exempt** from FR-357's bound; every rule-mode chore keeps the bound exactly as FR-357 states it. The exemption is stated in FR-343 and in FR-357 themselves, not only here, because those are where an implementer or a reviewer reads the bound. `[OURS 2026-09-04 #15]`

### Key Entities

- **Task** — one chore or one routine; the same kind of thing, told apart by which it is. It has a title, an optional emoji, an optional description, the people it is for, and a schedule that depends on its type: a chore has an optional date and an optional time, a routine has a repeat, a set of weekdays and a set of times of day. A chore may also be marked up for grabs, in which case it is for nobody until someone claims it. Every task carries a reserved star value that nothing in this phase reads. It belongs to exactly one household and records which profile created it and which last changed it.
- **Assignment** — that a task is for a particular Profile, in the order that Profile's routines appear in a section. A task may be for several Profiles; each of them has their own occurrences and their own history of it. A task may never be assigned to a Label.
- **Occurrence** — one dated appearance of a task for one assignee: a chore's date, an anytime chore's single undated appearance, or one time-of-day slot on one date for a routine. Occurrences are worked out from the task, never stored one by one, so a routine that never ends costs nothing to keep. An occurrence's identity is its scheduled date and slot, which never changes even when it is resolved days later.
- **Resolution** — that an occurrence was settled: completed, or skipped. A completion records the day it was ticked, the moment, the Profile credited and the punched-in actor who did it. A skip records nothing but that it was skipped and by whom, and a skip of an unclaimed up-for-grabs occurrence credits nobody, because it belongs to nobody. An occurrence with no resolution is simply outstanding, which is why an endless routine stores nothing until somebody acts. A resolution survives every edit to the task it belongs to, even one that stops producing the occurrence it keys to; it is then kept and not surfaced, never deleted.
- **Repeat** — how a task comes round. A routine repeats on a rule. A chore either repeats on a rule (Scheduled Date: absolute, unaffected by whether the last one was done) or on a cursor (Completed Date: the next one is due a set time after the last one was settled, and does not exist before then). Either may stop on an end date. A task has at most one repeat.
- **Habit streak** — how many days in a row one assignee has completed one routine. Held per person per routine, advanced by a fully completed day, protected but not advanced by a skipped one, and reset by a day that ends outstanding.
- **Task Box template** — a reusable name, emoji and type, with a reserved star value nothing this phase reads or edits, and no schedule and no assignment, kept per household, seeded with the reference's seventeen and thereafter the household's own to edit and delete.
- **Task filter set** — which of completed, late, skipped, up-for-grabs and each Profile a *device* is currently showing. It changes nothing stored, it never leaves the device, and it is distinct from the household-wide switch that removes a Profile from the tab for everyone.

This also parks three open questions rather than pre-answering them: the star chip on the card face (Contradiction 4), the two conflicting star-value guidance bands (help centre "1, 2, 3, 5"; marketing "five to ten" and up to a hundred — both verified, neither a limit), and the redeem modal's copy (Contradiction 9). Nothing this phase stores presumes any of them. Two verified star-facing consequences of behaviour that *does* ship here are inherited rather than lost: that a skipped occurrence earns no stars `[V](36846381293979)`, which this phase cannot observe because it has no star surface (FR-360), and that a template's star value is editable on the same form as its title `[V](39074226341659)`, which is the fourth field FR-380 does not ship. `[OURS]`
2. **The tab is a column per Profile showing one day, and it is the only view.** This is the reference's own layout and the one thing its three conflicting navigation descriptions agree on. The Week and All views are separate, dated, real features of the reference, but nothing anywhere shows what either renders, and the master map's own scope line asking for a Tasks week view is not enough to design one from. Deferring them whole beats shipping a guess. **Task search does not go with them** — that equivalence is not in any source, and the two are separate dated releases; the Search control ships here, as Assumption 27 explains. Contradiction 2. `[OURS]`
3. **A punch-in is required to resolve anything, and a member may resolve only their own.** The reference has no gate at all — it attributes a completion by which card was tapped — and that is the single most-repeated complaint in its owner reviews. Ours records the actor as well as the credited Profile, so "Ana ticked Cleo's homework" is a fact the record keeps. A member may complete, un-complete, skip and unskip their own tasks and claim up-for-grabs for themselves; a parent may do any of it for anyone. A refused tap changes nothing, stores nothing, and says whose task it is. This is the first permission in the app whose answer depends on *which* record is being touched rather than only on the role — a change to the shipped Phase 1 permission rule, and work the plan must treat as such, not as inheritance. `[OURS]`
4. **Both repeat modes ship**, because the reference has both and they are genuinely different tools: Scheduled Date for a thing that must happen on a rhythm, Completed Date for a thing that should come round a while after it was last actually done. Shipping one would have quietly removed the reason a family uses the second. `[OURS]`
5. **Late carry-forward and skip both ship**, per the reference, with their documented consequences intact: a missed chore follows the family to today, and a skipped one costs nothing and protects the streak. `[OURS]`
6. **Creating and changing tasks is parent-only; resolving them is not.** The reference's own answer is a surface split — stars and task creation on the app, ticking on the wall device — which this project discarded in Phase 1, because the split exists only because their device has no idea who is standing at it. Translated to roles: parents create, edit, delete, manage templates, reorder columns and control who appears on the tab; anybody punched in resolves within the ownership rule and may reorder the routines in their own column. Chores are not reorderable by anyone, which is the reference's rule and not ours (Contradiction 1). `[OURS]`
7. **A late chore appears on its own day and on today, for twenty-eight days.** The sources say it "carries forward to subsequent days" and "persists until marked complete" and never say whether the intervening days show it. Smearing one chore across every day since it was due would make a week away from home unreadable; showing it only on today would lose the fact that it was Tuesday's. So: its own day, and today, and a bound — after twenty-eight days it stops following the family and stays on the day it belonged to. The bound is arbitrary and is stated so it can be argued with. Up-for-grabs chores carry forward on the same rule; nothing suggests they should not. `[OURS]`
8. **The column counters count the day, not the view.** The total is that Profile's occurrences on the displayed day — routines in each slot, chores due that day, undated anytime chores, and chores carried forward late — less skipped ones, which the reference explicitly removes from "the total tasks or the circular completion bar". The completed number is those resolved complete. Filters never change it: a person hiding completed cards is tidying their screen, not undoing the morning. This rule is also what the calendar's Tasks Progress display and the later home screen will read, so it is fixed here once for all three. `[OURS]`
9. **Skip, streaks and per-assignee state.** A skip is a resolution of one occurrence for one assignee, recorded beside completion — a knowing divergence, since the reference's own interface rejects "skipped" as a completion state and stores it somewhere no source names (Contradiction 5); the observable behaviour is copied exactly. Skipping an unclaimed up-for-grabs occurrence skips it for the household, because it belongs to nobody. A "delete this occurrence" is recorded as the same skip, so an occurrence can never be both. Streaks are per routine per assignee, advanced by a day whose every occurrence was completed, held flat by a skipped day, reset by a day that ended outstanding, recomputed when a resolution is undone, and offered on routines only — the toggle is documented nowhere else. Everything here beyond "skips do not break a streak" is unknown in the sources; the rules are chosen so a badge always describes stored records rather than a counter nobody can audit. `[OURS]`
10. **The four toggles are independent switches, and the clock takes them back.** Any combination may be on, including none; Chores composes with any time of day, because it is not a time of day. A manual change holds until the next automatic boundary — noon, six, midnight — and then the automatic selection re-asserts, so a wall tablet someone poked at breakfast is showing the evening's routines by evening. The reference says only that tapping overrides the automatic selection "at any time"; how long an override lasts is unknown. `[OURS]`
11. **One task, several assignees; one routine, several times of day.** The reference reaches both by making more records — one chore per profile, one record per time of day — which makes "edit this chore" ambiguous and lets the morning and evening halves of one routine drift apart. Here a task is one thing that several people each have their own occurrences and history of, and a routine is one thing with a set of slots. The cost, stated rather than hidden: a routine cannot be ordered differently in its Morning and Evening sections, which the reference can do. Contradictions 6 and 7. `[OURS]`
12. **"Show on Tasks tab" hides the column *and* withdraws the profile as an assignment target**, household-wide, without touching a single stored task; switching it back restores both. The support case behind the reference's per-profile filter is exactly this — a newly created profile invisible in task creation until enabled — and Phase 1 already shipped the switch for this phase to define. It is a different thing from the per-device filter, which hides a column on one screen and nothing else. `[OURS]`
13. **A task may never be assigned to a Label.** The reference's own filters imply chores exist on non-profile categories, but a Label has no PIN, no punch-in identity and no star balance, so nobody could ever legitimately complete a Label's task and there would be nobody to credit. Refused in the picker and at the data store, not merely discouraged. `[OURS]`
14. **The recurrence grammar is widened to intervals greater than one, and this is real work.** "Every 2 days", "Every N weeks" and "Every N months" are verified in both the routine picker and the chore repeat form, and Phase 2's shipped grammar accepts an interval of one and refuses everything else, backed by a database constraint and a byte-stable parse-and-emit test. Meeting the reference here means widening all three — the grammar, the constraint, the round-trip test — and teaching the expander modular arithmetic against the series anchor, which it does not do today. The alternative was to restrict this phase to interval one and quietly not match the product; the operator should see the cost rather than the trim. The interval is bounded at **1 to 99** — a whole number, at least one, at most two digits: no source states any bound, so the ceiling is ours, chosen as the widest a picker of this shape can plausibly express, and stated here because it is the number the database constraint and the round-trip test will be written against and so is the thing to argue with. Phase 2's invariant of one occurrence per series per local date survives, since a wider interval only makes occurrences rarer. `[OURS]`
15. **The Completed Date cursor: from the resolution, not the due date.** The next occurrence appears at the moment the current one is resolved, dated that resolution's date plus the delay ("Immediately" being zero). At most one occurrence is ever open. Undoing a completion withdraws the occurrence it scheduled and restores the original, unless that scheduled occurrence has itself been resolved, in which case the undo is refused rather than destroying a real record. A skip advances the cycle the same way a completion does — otherwise a single skip would end the chore forever, which nothing suggests is intended. None of this is documented; all of it is forced by the mode existing at all. `[OURS]`
16. **The Up for Grabs column has no profile, so it gets no profile treatment**: a neutral panel, its own name, a count of what is unclaimed, no avatar and no ring. Only its left-most position is verified; it has never been photographed. `[OURS]`
17. **The routine's own progress indicator and the streak badge exist; their geometry is ours.** Both features are verified — a per-routine progress bar and a lightning-bolt badge beside the name — and neither appears in any photograph. Sizes and placement are design decisions this specification does not fix beyond requiring they be legible at wall distance and inside the 44-point floor. `[OURS]`
18. **The profile chip row does not render on the Tasks tab.** The reference's Tasks layout goes from the top bar straight into columns, while the shipped shell renders the chip row on every tab. The columns are the profiles; a second row of them is noise. This changes a shipped Phase 1 component and is work, not inheritance. `[OURS]`
19. **Tasks have no per-occurrence overrides.** Nothing in any source shows one occurrence of a chore or routine being retitled or retimed; the only per-occurrence acts documented are complete, skip and delete-this-instance. So an edit is an edit of the task, for everyone it is assigned to, and the per-occurrence exception machinery Phase 2 built for events is deliberately not reused. This is a real difference between the two phases and is stated so nobody unifies them later. `[OURS]`
20. **The delete scopes match the reference's asymmetry.** A repeating chore offers this occurrence, all future, all; a routine offers all future and all. That asymmetry is verified, and it costs nothing here because a routine's single occurrence is removed with Skip, which is the same record a "this occurrence" delete writes anyway. "All future" ends the repeat immediately before the chosen occurrence and keeps everything earlier; because tasks carry no per-occurrence overrides (Assumption 19), it needs none of the series-splitting Phase 2 required. `[OURS]`
21. **The card darkening is the completion feedback.** The reference's documents describe no animation and no sound on ticking a single task; one owner review describes stars dancing across the screen, and the design dossier proposes a short cross-fade as an estimate. What is real is photographed: the card goes from the profile's colour at 40 % to the profile's colour in full. That is the feedback, with a short cross-fade that collapses under reduced motion, and no sound. Two further celebrations are deferred, and the reason matters because neither is star-facing and both hang off work *this* phase does: the **whole-list emoji rain** fires when every chore in one person's list is checked off `[V]` — a task-completion trigger, whose "every" will be read from FR-305's denominator rule, so a skipped occurrence is out of it and anytime and late chores are in — and the **"Amazing Week"** celebration fires on a full week of completed routine tasks `[V](50672917447067)`, hanging off the habit streaks FR-371 to FR-374 ship. Both are held back because celebrations are one body of work with the redemption confetti, not because either is keyed to stars, and both are named in Out of Scope so the phase that builds them inherits the evidence rather than losing the trail here. What either renders is `[?]` and is left to that phase. `[OURS]`
22. **The late state and the completed check need colours no source provides.** The reference has no late or overdue treatment in any image, and its completed check circle is described only as "deeper" with no value. So: late is marked with its own treatment and the original due date, deliberately not the delete colour, which would read as destructive; and the checkmark and card text are derived for contrast against the card's own fill at both tints, because several of the twenty sanctioned palette colours fail a fixed ink at full strength. Same rule Phase 2 set for event blocks. `[OURS]`
23. **Adding from the Task Box asks for what a template cannot hold.** A template is verified to hold a title, an emoji, a type and a star value — no date, no repeat, no assignment, no description — so choosing one opens the ordinary create form pre-filled with those and empty everywhere else, with assignment and scheduling still required. The seventeen seeded templates are copied verbatim from the reference, as the household's own copies, editable and deletable from the first day; they are seeded when a household is set up rather than hard-coded into the interface, so a second household starts identically. `[OURS]`
24. **Deleting a Profile takes their assignments and their own history, and nothing else.** A task with another assignee survives, minus that person. A task left with nobody assigned is deleted with the profile — a chore becomes up-for-grabs by an explicit choice, never by attrition, and a routine with no one to do it is a phantom. The confirmation states both counts, alongside the affected-event count Phase 2 added to the same dialog: this is the second amendment to that shipped Phase 1 surface, and the plan must treat it as work. It also makes one dialog carry two different promises, which is the first thing an operator reading it will notice and so must be said in its copy rather than discovered: Phase 2 guarantees that **no event is destroyed by deleting a category** (002 FR-274), while a task left with no assignee at all **is** deleted with the profile. The asymmetry is deliberate — an event with no category is still an event on the calendar; a task with nobody to do it is nothing — and it is the reason the two counts in that dialog cannot be worded as one. `[OURS]`
25. **The column count is a fit consequence, and the phone shows one.** The reference's ~400-point column is a photographed measurement whose own arithmetic leaves a fifth column half visible, which is exactly consistent with its verified swipe-for-more-profiles. So the board measures itself and stretches, as the calendar already does: four columns at the reference width and on a landscape tablet, three on a portrait tablet — wrapping onto a second row, which is what reproduces the reference's photographed two-by-two — and as many as fit on a phone, which on a narrow one is a single full-width column, with a swipe moving between the rest and Up for Grabs first.

Clarified with the operator on 2026-09-04: the count is **measured at every width**, never a fixed number per device class. They had just corrected the calendar for behaving as though there were only two layouts when it in fact fits three to seven columns, and the same reasoning applies here — a 430-point phone that can show two people readably should show two. The calendar's three-column *floor* still does not transfer, because a Tasks column is a person and one person is a coherent view where one day is not. `[OURS]`
26. **A routine's timing is a weekday and a time of day, and the exhaustiveness of that is a decision.** What the article verifies is narrower than the rule the spec needs: 35340626864027 says only that *"Routines do not have timing options available to Chores"*, and that converting a task into a routine forces a repetition pattern, a day and a time of day. The briefs then disagree about what grade that supports — `behaviour.md` marks the positive claim `[I]`, `data.md` marks the same sentence `[V]` (Contradiction 10). Resolved in favour of the lower grade and the article's own words: the absence of the chore timing fields is fact; that weekday plus time of day is the *whole* of a routine's timing is adopted as a decision, because a routine carrying a due time would simply be a chore and the conversion note says as much. `[OURS]`
27. **Task search ships, as a filter over the board rather than a view.** The reference has a Search control, it is a dated release of its own, and it is verified to find tasks by name and description — so under the governing rule it is adopted, not deferred. Carrying it out with the Week and All views would mean treating a search result list as the All view under another name; no source supports that equivalence, the two are separate dated features two months apart, and a search box changes no layout, so it is not deferred with them. What no source shows is what its results *render* as, and that is the only part decided here: the board itself, filtered in place as the five filters already filter it, with the column counters unmoved. If the reference turns out to open a separate result list, that is a later change of surface and not of behaviour. It also gives the optional description (FR-321) a second reason to exist: it is one of the two fields the reference's search matches. `[OURS]`

Smaller calls are carried by the requirement that makes them rather than numbered here, and each states its own reasoning inline: FR-302 (chores get a section of their own), FR-316 (an empty column still renders), FR-319 (a title is required), FR-321 (the description shows in the details view, not on the card), FR-330 (the form's field order), FR-336 (an occurrence never leaves its time of day), FR-341 (a missed scheduled occurrence stands beside the next one), FR-351 (the refusal wording), FR-370 (one claim wins), FR-381 (deleting a template leaves its tasks), FR-384 (filters never move the counters), FR-393 (a write that cannot complete is refused, never queued), FR-394 (a column scrolls, the page does not), FR-311 (the order chores appear in), FR-346 (a routine may carry an end date too), and FR-332 (an edit never destroys a resolution). All are `[OURS 2026-09-04]`.

- Phase 2 (`002-family-week-calendar`) shipped and deployed: the household timezone and the discipline of working every date in it; the recurrence grammar, its database constraint and its round-trip test, which this phase widens (Assumption 14); the window-expansion approach that produces occurrences without storing them; the fractional ordering helper that column and routine reordering reuse; the measured-geometry pattern the column fit rule reuses; the server-action contract — read under the household's own policies, write through an action that re-checks the punch-in — and its refusal posture.
- Three shipped surfaces this phase **changes** rather than inherits: the shell's profile chip row, which no longer renders on this tab (FR-314); the Profile delete confirmation, which gains task counts **and a second, opposite promise** — Phase 2 guarantees no event is destroyed by deleting a category (002 FR-274), while a task left with no assignee is deleted, so the same dialog must now say both (FR-391, Assumption 24); and the permission rule, which gains its first decision that depends on the record being touched (Assumption 3).
- The research dossiers and master map in `docs/research/skylight/`, which this specification treats as the product definition, and the operator's Phase 3 decisions of 2026-09-04 recorded above.

## Out of Scope

Deferred to a later phase of this project: **everything star-facing** — star values in the interface, star chips, column star totals, balances, the star ledger, manual give-and-take of stars, editing a Task Box template's reserved star value (the reference's fourth editable template field `[V](39074226341659)`, which FR-380 does not ship), the whole Rewards tab, reward cards and their progress, redeem and unredeem, and the redeem modal; the **celebrations**, all three of them, held back together as one body of work rather than because each is star-facing — the **redemption confetti**, which does hang off the stars above, the **whole-list emoji rain**, which does not (its trigger is completing every chore in one person's list `[V]`, the task-completion path this phase builds, and its "every" will be read from FR-305's denominator rule), and the **"Amazing Week" celebration** a full week of completed routine tasks earns `[V](50672917447067)`, which hangs off the habit streaks this phase ships (Assumption 21); **task reminders and notifications** of any kind, which belong to Phase 5 — this phase leaves behind a task record from which each occurrence's due instant can be computed, and a completion record carrying its actor and its task, and delivers nothing; the **Tasks Progress** display on the calendar, which will read this phase's completed-of-total rule; the **home screen** and its task pane, also Phase 5; the **Week** and **All** views of tasks — but not the task **search**, which ships here (FR-386, Assumption 27); per-occurrence edits of a task's own fields (Assumption 19); an undo or a trash for deleted tasks or templates; and an offline cache.

Excluded from the project entirely: weather in any form; external calendar synchronisation and every field that exists to serve it; all AI features, including photo, voice and email input and automatic emoji; the photo screensaver and sleep mode; themed character modes; companion hardware, including the routine timer the reference offers for it; grocery-ordering integrations; multi-device linking; guest invitations; and subscription tiers, of which this project has none — everything the reference gates behind its paid tier is simply present here.
