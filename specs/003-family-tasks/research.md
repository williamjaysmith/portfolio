## The four lane briefs, and which one decides each question

Phase 0 ran as four parallel lanes — recurrence, completed-date, schema, client — each producing its own brief, plus the distilled `data.md` Part B they were all written against. Two lanes reached the same question from different ends and disagreed. One document decides each question and the loser says who won, exactly as 002 recorded its two superseded drafts. **The four lane briefs are not checked into `specs/003-family-tasks/`**, so every citation of one carries its file name — `r-schema.md` D303/D306/D312, `r-client.md` R332, `r-completed-date.md` — and those identifiers are *that brief's* numbering, never this document's R301–R326 series:

- **The schema lane's `tasks.next_due_date`** — a Completed Date chore's open occurrence stored as a column on the task row — is **superseded by R309**. The completed-date lane found the column demonstrably wrong under FR-324 (one column on `tasks`, two independent cycles when a chore has two assignees, so Ben completing on Thursday moves Ana's due date) and answered the schema lane's one real objection — that the deciding row lies outside the board's window — with a published view rather than a stored copy.
- **The schema lane's (`r-schema.md`) locked single-claim function D306 and its two cursor-carrying RPCs D312** are **narrowed by R310** and then **cut entirely by `data-model.md`**: with R308's key the unique index arbitrates the claim and the `cycle_prev` foreign key carries FR-344's refusal, which left the two functions holding only the streak checkpoint — and Phase 1 design judged that checkpoint's half-state (a stale badge FR-374's recompute already heals) to be Phase 1's documented non-atomic-action posture rather than Phase 2's data-loss bar. **No function is on this phase's write path.** See the superseded banner on R310; `data-model.md` decides.
- **The client lane's `r-client.md` R332** ("Completed Date is a cursor the client reads") is restated in R309's terms: the client still never computes a *next* date from history it might have stale, but it does derive the open occurrence from the published chain tail, through the same pure function the action validates with.
- **The client lane's contract #1** — "the store must make the double claim impossible, because `(task, category, date, slot)` admits two claimants crediting two different profiles" — is **discharged by R308**, structurally, rather than by the lock it asked for.

And the distilled `data.md` Part B is input, not gospel. Superseded in full: its ten-migration rewards scope (FR-375 — R313), `task_completions` as a name and `completed_on`/`completed_at` as its date pair (R308), the `repeat_mode` enum (R306), `carry_forward_from` (R316), `next_due_date` (R309), the `recurrence_set` array and `BYHOUR` slot encoding (R301, R306), `sort_order` on templates (R312), the `0..500` star bound (R313), and the `familyKeys.taskDay` cache key (R314). ## R301 — Widen `INTERVAL` in place: one grammar, one parser, one emitter, one expander

**Decision**: `lib/family/recurrence/grammar.ts` accepts and emits a whole `INTERVAL` of **1–99**, unpadded, regex `^([1-9]|[1-9][0-9])$`. Nothing else about R201's closed grammar moves: `INTERVAL` is still **always written**, still in slot 2 immediately after `FREQ`, and the canonical field order stays `FREQ;INTERVAL;UNTIL;WKST;BYDAY|BYMONTHDAY` — no `RRULE:` prefix, no `COUNT`, no `DTSTART`, `WKST` weekly-only, `BYDAY`/`BYMONTHDAY` always explicit, `UNTIL` inclusive by household-local date comparison, one rule per series, server-emitted only. Two deltas beyond the number: `interval` becomes a **required** field on `RecurrenceRule` (not optional-defaulting-to-1), and **`WKST` becomes required on `FREQ=WEEKLY` with `INTERVAL > 1`** — optional and inert at 1 (R303 says why). The emitter refuses an interval it could not parse back, mirroring the shipped `formatByMonthDay`/`formatByDay` posture. Tasks reuse this grammar for two of their four repeat shapes and get their own structured client choice: `TaskRepeatChoice` is a new union in `lib/family/types.ts`, and `RepeatChoice` and `repeatChoiceSchema` — the calendar's contract — are **not touched**.

**Rationale**: FR-345 and Assumption 14 make "every 2 days", "every 3 weeks" and "every 2 months" `[V]` behaviour in both the routine picker and the chore repeat form, so under the phase's governing rule they are adopted, not weighed. The grammar is the wrong place to fork: R202 chose a hand-rolled subset precisely because the recurrence arithmetic is ~30 lines and the risk is zone math, and widening one field keeps that trade while keeping one predicate that events, chores and routines all agree on. The change is small because Phase 2 built it to be — `INTERVAL` was already written on every rule rather than omitted at 1 (R201 called that "self-describing"), so widening it moves no bytes, and `expandSeries` already computes the anchor R303 needs `[code: recurrence/expand.ts:78]`. Requiring `interval` rather than defaulting it is the same instinct as pinning the events schema against it: a widening should be *visible* at every site that could exploit it, and a required field makes `tsc --noEmit` enumerate every producer the moment the type lands — the compiler doing the audit, rather than a silently-defaulted field a future task action forgets to set. It also keeps `parseRule(emitRule(r))` deep-equal to `r`, which the shipped round-trip test asserts with `toEqual` `[code: __tests__/unit/grammar.test.ts]`.

**Alternatives rejected**: **Restrict Phase 3 to interval 1 and not match the product** — the cheapest option, and the one Assumption 14 exists to price; rejected by the governing rule, and the cost turns out to be ~40 lines of grammar and expander plus a test block, smaller than the spec feared. **A second grammar for tasks** (`tasks_rrule` with its own parser) — two parsers over one string format is the exact drift Phase 2 designed against, it doubles the DST test surface for zero benefit, and it would be a worse trap here because both tables would share a column type and a CHECK while disagreeing about what `INTERVAL=2` means. **`interval?: number` defaulting to 1** — keeps the ~6 event construction sites in `actions/events.ts` compiling untouched, which is the saving that hides the bug. **Defaulting `WKST` to `MO` (RFC 5545) or to `SU` (this household)** — RFC's default contradicts the household's week, and the household's would need `expandSeries` to take a new argument or read settings from inside a pure module, which R202 kept it free of. **Copying the reference's `recurrence_set` array of rule strings** — R201 already rejected `text[]` for events ("fidelity to an array that never held a second element in any observed row"), and the one thing that array was observed carrying is multiple `BYHOUR` values, which R306's `times_of_day` column replaces.

## R302 — The widening is live-data-safe structurally, not by care taken

**Decision**: Every rule string Phase 2 has ever written survives parse → emit **byte-for-byte**, and every shipped series expands to **exactly** the same occurrences. This is asserted by two tests rather than by inspection: an **exhaustive interval-1 equivalence** unit test (the shipped three-line predicate inlined as `legacyMatches`, then `matchesOn(rule, day, anchorDay) === legacyMatches(rule, day)` for every rule shape × every day in 2025-01-01…2027-12-31 across three anchors — ~44 000 evaluations, milliseconds) and a **stored-corpus round-trip** in the policies project (`select distinct rrule from family.events`, asserting parse succeeds, emit is byte-identical, and `interval === 1` on every row), run against an **untouched schema** before migration 022 lands. Say what each reaches, because they do not reach the same rows: the sweep is algebraic, and the corpus test runs in the `policies` project, which `vitest.config.ts` binds to the **local** 553xx stack `[code]` — it reads the seeded fixtures after `db reset`, never a hosted row. The family's live rules are covered by a third check, the operator's read-only query before 022 is written (quickstart §4.2, data-model §022), which must return zero rows.

**Rationale**: Three properties make it structural. `interval: 1` formats as `"1"` in a slot that did not move, did not change delimiter and did not become conditional, so an interval-1 rule emits a character-identical string — the FR-233 reference capture `FREQ=WEEKLY;INTERVAL=1;UNTIL=20260106T235959Z;WKST=SU;BYDAY=MO,TU` is a member of that set, preserved with no special case. `x % 1 === 0` for every integer, so all three of R303's new predicates collapse at `interval === 1` to precisely today's three — algebraically the same function, not "equivalent in the cases we tested", which is why the proof is an equivalence sweep and not a sample. And the new mandatory-`WKST` rule applies only above interval 1, so it cannot invalidate a stored rule, every one of which is interval 1. Phase 2's licensing invariant survives too and is strengthened rather than threatened: R204's date-keyed exceptions and 012's date-keyed uniqueness rest on "at most one occurrence per series per local date", and a wider interval makes matching dates **rarer**, never denser. The one place a new anchor is minted is `family.split_event_series` (015), which re-emits the head's rule through `emitRule({...parseRule(rrule), until})` and re-anchors the tail; the object spread carries `interval` through, and the split is always taken at a real occurrence, so the tail sits on the same parity lattice — a genuine property to test in the policies tier rather than assume.

**Alternatives rejected**: Proving it by sampling a handful of rules — the whole reachable domain is ~44 000 cheap evaluations, so sampling would be a choice to know less for no saving. Migrating stored rules to a new canonical form "while we are in there" — nothing needs migrating, and rewriting live rows to prove a change is safe is the opposite of proving it.

## R303 — The expander becomes anchor-relative; epoch-day arithmetic inherits Phase 2's DST immunity

**Decision**: `dateMatches(rule, day)` becomes `dateMatches(rule, day, anchorDay)` — DAILY matches when `(day − anchorDay) % interval === 0`; WEEKLY when the weekday is in `BYDAY` **and** the week parity from `WKST` matches; MONTHLY when the day-of-month equals `BYMONTHDAY` **and** `monthsBetween(anchorDay, day) % interval === 0`. Two helpers are **hoisted into `lib/family/recurrence/plain-date.ts`**, the existing shared epoch-day vocabulary: `weekStartDay(day, startWeekOn)` and `monthsBetween(from, to)`. `lib/family/calendar/dates.ts`'s `weekStartOf` is **refactored onto** `weekStartDay` — its body is already that expression `[code: calendar/dates.ts:55]` — so the change **removes** a duplication rather than adding one, which matters because `npm run fallow:dupes` measures against `main` and the alternative (copying the expression into `recurrence/`) would be a new dupe in a module the baseline says is clean.

**Rationale**: Four things fall out. **`WKST` stops being decoration** — RFC 5545 counts weeks from the week containing the anchor, where a week begins at `WKST`, so "every 2 weeks on Tue and Thu" anchored on a Sunday produces different dates under `WKST=SU` and `WKST=MO`; at interval 1 the choice is invisible, which is exactly how the shipped `dateMatches` got away with never reading it `[code]`. Making it required above interval 1 (R301) turns a silent wrong answer into a parse error at the only moment it could matter, and means `expandSeries` needs no new household argument. **No `floorMod` helper is needed**: `expandSeries` clamps `firstDay = max(rangeStart, anchor.day)` so the walk never goes below the anchor, and where the predicate is exported for the tasks walk, JavaScript's sign-preserving `%` yields `-0` for a negative dividend and `-0 === 0`, so `x % n === 0` is already correct before the anchor. **The arithmetic is DST-immune by construction** — epoch days are UTC-midnight counters (`plain-date.ts`'s own header: "UTC has no transitions, so stepping by whole days can never cross a DST boundary"), the parity is integer arithmetic on those counters, and the only zone-aware step is `nominalTimes`, untouched; SC-313's full-year every-2-days sweep across both `America/Chicago` transitions therefore inherits Phase 2's correctness, and the test ships as a regression guard rather than as a new risk. **Complexity stays inside budget**: `dateMatches` goes from cyclomatic 3 to 6, cognitive ~4, well under `maxCyclomatic: 20` / `maxCognitive: 15` — but it is branchy and pure, the canonical CRAP shape the gate punishes without coverage, so R325 covers every branch and both sides of `interval === 1`.

**Known consequence, recorded rather than fixed**: `BYMONTHDAY=31` with `INTERVAL=3` anchored 2026-01-31 matches Jan / Apr / Jul / Oct, and April has no 31st, so the visible gap is **six months** instead of the one interval 1 produces. This is existing behaviour (a date that does not exist never matches), not a regression, and it belongs in the form copy so nobody reports it as a bug. Clamping to month-end is refused in the alternatives below.

**Alternatives rejected**: **Clamping `BYMONTHDAY=31` to the month end** — invents a semantic no source verifies, makes a stored rule no longer readable as a plain per-date predicate, and would change shipped behaviour for existing monthly events, which is the one thing R302 promises not to do. (The opposite answer is correct for the Completed Date cursor and R309 says so: a rule may legitimately be silent in a month, a cursor must always land somewhere or the chore is lost.) **Duplicating `weekStartDay` into `recurrence/`** — a new duplication in a clean module, to avoid an import the fallow boundary already permits (`family-calendar-core` → `family-recurrence` is an allowed edge and `plain-date.ts` is inside `family-recurrence`; the reverse would be both forbidden and a cycle). **Passing the household's start-of-week into `expandSeries`** — see R301's `WKST` alternative; it would put settings inside a pure module.

## R304 — `ruleDatesIn`: one rule walk, two consumers

**Decision**: `lib/family/recurrence/expand.ts` grows one exported, date-only primitive —

```ts
ruleDatesIn(rule: RecurrenceRule, anchorDate: string, range: LocalDateRange): string[]
```

— the household-local dates a rule matches given the series anchor, and **`expandSeries` is refactored onto it**. Events keep their times; tasks take the dates. The tasks expander (R315) drives its own loop over `ruleDatesIn` and never calls `expandSeries`.

**`ruleDatesIn` floors its walk at `max(range.start, anchorDate)`, and that is part of the primitive's contract, not an accident of its first caller.** The floor is the guard `expandSeries` holds today at `lib/family/recurrence/expand.ts:78` (`firstDay = Math.max(epochDayOf(range.start), anchor.day)`) `[code]`; stated here because the anchor-relative predicate R303 introduces is `(day − anchorDay) % interval === 0`, JavaScript `%` keeps the **dividend's** sign, and every negative offset an exact multiple of the interval therefore matches (`-2 % 2 === -0`). At interval 1 that is invisible — every negative offset yields `-0 === 0`, which is exactly why R302's ~44 000-evaluation equivalence sweep is green and proves nothing about it — but at interval ≥ 2 an unfloored walk renders an "every 2 days" task on days **before its own start date**, and FR-303's Previous navigation makes `displayedDate < startsOn` reachable. Both consumers inherit the floor from the primitive rather than each re-deriving it, and the tasks generators (R315) assert it directly.

**Rationale**: The cheapest-looking alternative — call `expandSeries` with a fabricated `EventTimes` — would invent a start instant for a routine that has no clock time at all, dragging gap/fold policy into a thing with no time to shift and re-importing precisely the lie that storing `due_time` as a `time` rather than a `timestamptz` exists to avoid (R306). Extracting the date walk instead is nearly free, because R303 already requires `dateMatches` to learn the anchor: the refactor turns work that had to happen anyway into the primitive both phases stand on. One walk means the FR-345 widening is proved once for both phases, the `UNTIL`-inclusivity rule has one implementation, and Phase 2's "one occurrence per series per local date" invariant is a property of one function. The refactor must move **no** existing test: `expandSeries`'s shipped tables are the regression proof that the extraction changed nothing.

**Alternatives rejected**: A second expander in `lib/family/tasks/` duplicating the rule walk — the drift trap Phase 2 refused twice (015's own comment, and "how the week is read"), and it would need its own DST tables to stay honest. Calling `expandSeries` with synthetic times — above. Expanding server-side in SQL — a second rrule implementation in PL/pgSQL, rejected in R206 and rejected again here for the same reason. Storing occurrence rows — forbidden by the spec's own Key Entities.

## R305 — The rrule CHECK: identical on both tables, and sequenced last

**Decision**: `family.tasks.rrule` carries a **tightened** grammar CHECK from birth in 016 — `rrule ~ '^FREQ=(DAILY|WEEKLY|MONTHLY);INTERVAL=([1-9]|[1-9][0-9])(;|$)' and rrule !~ '(^|;)COUNT='` — and `family.events.rrule` is tightened to the **same text** in migration **022**, which is deliberately the **last** file of the phase. Both tables then carry one backstop for one grammar; `grep 'INTERVAL=(\[1-9\]'` finds both if a later phase widens it again.

**Rationale**: A correction to Assumption 14's premise, read out of the code: the shipped constraint is `rrule ~ '^FREQ=' and rrule !~ '(^|;)COUNT='` `[code: 010_events.sql:29]` and **it never mentioned `INTERVAL`** — the database accepts `INTERVAL=2` today. The pin was entirely in TypeScript (parser, emitter, type, expander), so **no migration is forced** and the widening touches three artefacts, not the four the spec priced. That is why 022 is sequenced last rather than first: land the grammar and the expander, prove byte-stability against a database nobody has touched (R302's corpus test is stronger evidence that way), and only then tighten. It still ships, because Assumption 14 promises the operator a constraint written against 1–99, because `family.tasks.rrule` needs *some* CHECK at creation and two different backstops for one grammar is drift by construction, and because a bounded regex is the last line of defence if a future action ever bypasses `emitRule`. The regex is strictly tighter than what shipped, so `ADD CONSTRAINT` validates every existing row and all of them pass; at a household's series count the ACCESS EXCLUSIVE lock is sub-millisecond and no `NOT VALID` dance is needed. The `(;|$)` tail is what rejects `INTERVAL=100` and `INTERVAL=01`: POSIX leftmost-longest tries `10` then `1` and neither is followed by `;` or end-of-string, and `[1-9]` cannot match a leading `0`.

**One knowing narrowing of R201, recorded**: R201 declined a `FREQ` whitelist so storage could accept a richer imported Skylight rule (FR-233). Three FREQ values are the whole set the emitter can produce and the parser will accept, so a stored value outside them is unreadable by definition and the whitelist costs nothing that was ever reachable. Named here so nobody reads it as an oversight.

**Alternatives rejected**: **A full-grammar regex in the CHECK** — duplicating a 200-line parser in a constraint nobody can unit-test at the same granularity, and the two drift on the first widening, which is this document. **Skipping 022 entirely** (the database already accepts `INTERVAL=N`) — tempting, and it is why the file is sequenced last rather than first; rejected because the tasks table needs a constraint anyway and two backstops for one grammar is the drift being avoided. **Tightening events *first*** — it would make R302's corpus test read a database this phase had already altered.

## R306 — One `family.tasks` table; the repeat mode is which fields are populated

**Decision**: One row per definition (FR-317), with `routine boolean` the sole discriminator. A chore's schedule is `starts_on date` + `due_time time`; a routine's slots are `times_of_day family.time_of_day[]`. The four chore sub-types are **derived, never stored** (FR-325): date + time → Timed, date only → All-day, neither → Anytime, and **Late is a position, not a value** — it is never stored at all. **No `repeat_mode` enum ships**: rule mode ⇔ `rrule is not null`, cursor mode ⇔ `renew_after_amount is not null`, and `check (num_nonnulls(rrule, renew_after_amount) <= 1)` makes FR-339's mutual exclusivity structural. `renew_until date` is the cursor mode's end date (FR-346); a rule-mode task's end date lives inside the rule's own `UNTIL`, where R201's sole emitter writes it. `times_of_day` is constrained to **the seven legal non-empty sets** on a routine and to the empty array on a chore. Six further CHECKs make each type's illegal columns unpopulatable: up-for-grabs is a chore only, habit tracking is a routine only, a routine has no due time and always repeats on a rule, a due time requires a date, either repeat requires an anchor (so an Anytime chore is structurally incapable of repeating), and the cursor mode owns its own three fields.

**Rationale**: Every clause is forced by an FR that admits exactly one storage shape. FR-317 states the one-record rule outright and the reference verifiably does the same; two tables would make FR-318's conversion a movement of the row between tables, which would have to move or orphan its resolutions — precisely what FR-332 forbids. Dropping the mode enum follows Phase 2's invariant 2 verbatim ("the presence of `events.rrule` is the single source of truth for 'does this event repeat'"): an enum beside the fields can disagree with the fields; a `num_nonnulls` CHECK cannot — and it matches FR-325's own philosophy, that kinds fall out of the fields. Enumerating the seven slot sets rather than checking containment is what makes non-emptiness (FR-333, US2-7), canonical order and — crucially — **deduplication** structural: `data.md`'s containment CHECK admits `{}` on a routine (because `array_length('{}',1)` is NULL and `NULL is distinct from 0` is true) and admits `{'morning','morning'}`, which would emit two identical occurrences, double a column's denominator, and collide on the resolution key at the second tick. `starts_on` rather than `due_date` is one column serving both a chore's due date and a routine's first eligible day; the chore's due date is not lost because it was never a column — it is the *occurrence's* date, which is what FR-358's late banner shows and what FR-353 keys a resolution by, and once `INTERVAL=N` lands the anchor is load-bearing arithmetic (R303), not merely a lower bound. `due_time` is a wall-clock `time` and never an instant (FR-326), because storing an instant would fabricate a date for an Anytime chore and re-import the drift Phase 2 avoided by keeping all-day events as plain dates; the due **instant**, needed for "is it past?" and inherited unchanged by Phase 5's reminders, is `wallToInstant(zone, date + due_time)` through the shipped `recurrence/zone.ts`, so FR-326's gap-and-fold rules come free.

**Naming**: `summary`, `description`, `emoji`, `routine`, `up_for_grabs`, `reward_points` copy the reference's verified field names, per Phase 2's discipline. Six reference fields are deliberately absent: `timer_seconds` (companion hardware is excluded from the project), `origin` (its enum is unknown), `series` and `group` (artefacts of the server-side instance expansion this project does not do), `is_future` (derived), and `position` — which becomes `task_assignees.sort_order`, because FR-311 forbids reordering chores outright and FR-310 scopes routine reordering to one section for one Profile, which is per-assignee state.

**Alternatives rejected**: Separate `chores` and `routines` tables (a conversion becomes data movement; every read becomes a union). A `kind` enum with more than two values (nothing in the spec has a third kind; Late is derived). `data.md`'s three-valued `repeat_mode` plus a nine-branch CASE (more surface, a second thing to keep true, and its `routine ⇒ repeat_mode = 'scheduled_date'` clause reads as a category error, since a routine is never offered a mode choice). `repeat_until` on both modes (two homes for one value — the drift Phase 2 refused). `BYHOUR` inside the rule for a routine's slots — refused twice over: Contradiction 7 already resolved multi-slot routines to one record, and a `BYHOUR=6,20` rule puts two occurrences on one local date **inside** the recurrence layer, destroying the invariant R204's date key and 012's uniqueness both rest on. Keeping slot multiplicity *above* the recurrence layer preserves it: the rule answers *which dates*, `times_of_day` answers *how many on each*, and the recurrence module never learns that slots exist. Three boolean slot columns (equally structural, but "the set of slots" is the spec's own vocabulary). A `task_slots` child table with per-slot ordering (full ordering fidelity, at the cost of a three-way table and a wider resolution FK; the ordering loss is already accepted and recorded in Contradiction 7).

## R307 — Assignment is a join table; Profile-only is enforced by a trigger

**Decision**: `family.task_assignees (household_id, task_id, category_id, sort_order numeric, streak_count, streak_through, created_at)`, primary key `(task_id, category_id)`, composite FKs `(task_id, household_id)` and `(category_id, household_id)` both cascading. **Zero rows means up for grabs.** `assert_task_assignee()` — a `security definer`, `search_path = ''`, revoked-from-public BEFORE trigger — refuses a `category_id` that is not a Profile (FR-323, one of only two places the spec names data-store enforcement) and refuses an assignee on an up-for-grabs task; `assert_up_for_grabs_is_unassigned()` refuses the other direction, flipping a task up-for-grabs while assignees exist. `sort_order` is the shipped fractional index (`lib/family/ordering.ts`) `[code]`, per `(task, assignee)`.

**Rationale**: FR-324 and Contradiction 6 settle the join table over the reference's row fan-out: the fan-out makes "edit this chore" mean editing N records with no way to say which were meant to move together, and makes a person's completion history the accident of which row they were given. FR-323 demands the Profile rule at the data store as well as in the picker, and a CHECK cannot read the parent row, so a trigger is this codebase's established answer (010's `assert_event_timezone`, 003's `assert_profile_account_is_member`). The 011 pattern — a redundant `unique (id, household_id, is_profile)` on `categories` plus a constant column on the join — was considered and rejected: it costs an ALTER on a shipped Phase 1 table and a denormalised column, and its extra strength (catching a category *flipped* from Profile to Label while assignments exist) buys nothing, because `is_profile` is verified immutable after creation — `validation.ts` admits it "only for creation" and the update path reuses `existing.isProfile` `[code]`.

**One residual, stated rather than hidden**: FR-322's converse — "a task that is not up for grabs has **at least one** assignee" — is an **action-tier** rule, deliberately. The spec names data-store enforcement in exactly two places (FR-323, FR-390) and this is not one of them; and a deferred constraint trigger asserting it would refuse the very first statement of a Profile deletion, whose cascade legitimately leaves an orphan for the action's next statement to clear (FR-391). So FR-391's deletion is two statements, and a crash between them leaves a task with no assignee on nobody's board — invisible and unreachable through the interface. That is *retained* data, not lost data, and re-running the cleanup repairs it, which is why it does not clear Phase 2's bar for an RPC ("a half-completed split is a *wrong calendar*"). If it ever does, the fix is one more function in 020 and a deferred constraint trigger becomes possible at the same moment.

**Alternatives rejected**: Reference-style row fan-out (above; it also loses FR-331's "an edit is an edit of the task"). A composite FK carrying `is_profile` (above). Enforcing the Profile rule only in the picker — FR-323 says "as well as", not "instead of". `sort_order` on the task row — FR-310 makes routine order per-assignee state.

## R308 — `family.task_resolutions`: one row per resolved occurrence, keyed to include the cycle

**Decision**: One table for completions **and** skips — `status in ('complete','skipped')`, absence of a row *is* "outstanding", so an endless routine stores nothing until somebody acts. The row carries:

| Column | What it is |
|---|---|
| `occurrence_date date` | the occurrence's **original** household-local date (FR-353), **nullable** for an Anytime chore's single undated occurrence (FR-328) |
| `occurrence_slot family.time_of_day` | which slot, for a routine (FR-335); NULL on a chore |
| `assignee_id uuid` | **the chain's owner** — the assignee, or **NULL for an up-for-grabs task's single household chain** |
| `category_id uuid` | the Profile **credited** — NULL only on a skip of an unclaimed up-for-grabs occurrence (FR-363, FR-368) |
| `cycle_prev uuid` | self-FK to the previous resolution, `on delete no action`; NULL at a chain head **and on every rule-mode row** (R309) |
| `resolved_on date` / `resolved_at timestamptz` | **not null on both statuses** — the day and moment it was actually ticked or skipped (FR-354) |
| `created_by uuid` | the punched-in **actor**, separately from the credited Profile (FR-350, FR-354, Assumption 3) |

and the occurrence key is

```sql
unique nulls not distinct (task_id, assignee_id, occurrence_date, occurrence_slot, cycle_prev)
```

with `check (assignee_id is null or category_id = assignee_id)` — for an assigned task the credited Profile *is* the assignee, and the actor is the one who may differ — and `check (category_id is not null or (status = 'skipped' and assignee_id is null))`, which is FR-368 plus FR-363 exactly. Rows are **append-and-delete only**: un-complete and unskip both *delete* (FR-355, FR-361), so there is no `updated_at` and no touch trigger, and their absence states the invariant. `assignee_id` and `category_id` both cascade on Profile deletion (FR-391); `created_by` is `on delete set null`, so attribution survives the actor's deletion without the credited history silently changing hands. One trigger, `assert_task_resolution()`, fired **by writes to this row only**, holds the two rules that read the parent task: skip exists on routines and repeating chores only (FR-359), and a resolution crediting nobody is possible only on an up-for-grabs task.

**Rationale**: Every part is forced. The slot is what distinguishes a routine's two daily occurrences (FR-335, `[V](skylight-api — instance_time)`); the assignee is in the key because FR-324 gives each assignee their own occurrences and their own history; the nullable date is FR-328's undated Anytime occurrence; the separate `resolved_on` is FR-354's late chore recorded on the day it was **ticked**, not the day it was due; the separate actor is Assumption 3's whole reason for existing. Storing skips beside completions is a knowing divergence from a verified reference constraint (its completions endpoint rejects `"skipped"`), taken deliberately in Contradiction 5 and entered in the divergence ledger; the observable behaviour is the reference's exactly. `resolved_on`/`resolved_at` replace `data.md`'s `completed_on`/`completed_at` because its own CHECK forces them NULL on a skip and FR-362 advances the Completed Date cycle **from the skip date** — the requirement is unmeetable under the drafted shape — and because a neutral name is the honest one for a row that stores two kinds of resolution.

The five-column key does four separate jobs, which is the argument for it:

1. **Rule mode is unchanged.** Every routine and Scheduled Date row has `cycle_prev = NULL`, so the key degenerates to `(task, assignee, date, slot)` — Phase 2's date key, inherited whole.
2. **"After → Immediately" becomes representable.** FR-343 says a chore completed on its due date with a zero delay schedules the next occurrence **that same date**; cycle *n* and cycle *n+1* differ only in `cycle_prev`. Under a four-column key the mode's headline use — a fast-turnaround chore that comes straight back — is the one thing it cannot do, and the household reads the duplicate-key error as "it says I already did it".
3. **The FR-370 double claim is refused structurally.** Two devices claiming one up-for-grabs occurrence both write `assignee_id = NULL` (the household chain) with the same date, slot and parent; they collide on this index and the loser gets `23505`. This is what discharges the client lane's contract #1 — `data.md`'s four-column key admitted two claimants crediting two different profiles, because `category_id` differed. No lock, no RPC, one INSERT.
4. **A stale client cannot fork a chain.** A device that still believes cycle *n* is open, after someone else resolved it, sends `cycle_prev = n`'s id — which cycle *n+1* already occupies. Same collision, same refusal; a phantom cycle is unrepresentable rather than merely unlikely.

**On FR-391 and an up-for-grabs claim**: cascading `category_id` means deleting a Profile also removes their past *claims* of a household chore, which retreats that chore's household chain and makes the chore due again. That is FR-391's literal wording ("that Profile's own resolutions"), it is a coherent consequence rather than data loss, and the alternative — `on delete set null` — would leave an anonymous completion, which FR-368 forbids, and would fail the credit CHECK outright rather than degrade.

**Alternatives rejected**: A separate `task_skips` table (two tables to consult for "is this occurrence resolved", two unique keys to keep in step, and the filter, count and streak rules would each have to read both). Reusing Phase 2's `event_exceptions` (same word, opposite semantics — an event skip means the occurrence *does not exist*, a task skip means it existed and was resolved; and FR-331 removes the override payload that table exists to carry). A `pending` row per occurrence, or `status` including `'pending'` (an endless routine would cost rows for ever, against the spec's own Key Entities). Keeping `completed_on`/`completed_at` and deriving a skip's date from `created_at` (an instant needing a zone conversion to become a household-local date — the exact drift Phase 2 avoided by keeping dates as dates). A four-column key plus a partial unique index on `(task_id, occurrence_date, occurrence_slot) where up_for_grabs` (it works, but the flag would have to be denormalised onto the resolution behind a composite FK, and flipping a task's up-for-grabs state would then refuse an edit **because the task carries resolutions**, which FR-332 forbids in as many words). **PG 15 note**: `unique nulls not distinct` needs PG 15+; local `config.toml` pins 17 and the hosted project is confirmed before 018 is written, with a `coalesce`-expression index as the same-guarantee fallback.

## R309 — Completed Date is a chain of resolutions, not a stored cursor

**Decision**: **Nothing about the future is stored.** `tasks.rrule` is NULL in this mode; the mode is `renew_after_amount` + `renew_after_unit` (amount 0 **is** "Immediately", which keeps the delay function total) plus the optional `renew_until`. `tasks.next_due_date` **does not ship**. The open occurrence is **derived** by a pure, total function in `lib/family/tasks/cursor.ts`:

```
openOccurrence(task, tail, chainStartedOn):
  date = tail ? addDelay(tail.resolved_on, task.renewAfter)
              : max(task.starts_on, chainStartedOn)          // the seed
  return (task.renewUntil !== null && date > task.renewUntil) ? null : { date }
```

`addDelay` is plain-date arithmetic on the shipped `plain-date.ts` helpers — `day`/`week` are `+n` / `+7n` epoch days, `month` is calendar-month addition **clamped to the last day of the target month** (31 Jan + 1 month = 28 Feb). `chainStartedOn` is `task_assignees.created_at::date` for an assigned chain, or `tasks.created_at::date` for the household chain, so adding Ben to a chore whose date was six months ago starts him **today** rather than six months late. The chain tail is published to the browser-direct read as **`family.task_cursors`**, the schema's first view, `with (security_invoker = true)` so the underlying table's RLS applies to the caller and the view needs no policy of its own: `distinct on (household_id, task_id, assignee_id)` over the resolution rows no other row references as `cycle_prev`, `order by … resolved_on desc, created_at desc` (the tie-break matters after a mode conversion, where a set of rule-mode rows all have `cycle_prev = NULL` and are therefore all unreferenced; `resolved_on` is always "today at write time" and so monotone in write order). Two indexes serve it — one on `cycle_prev` for the anti-join, one on `(household_id, task_id, assignee_id)` for the filter. `lib/family/recurrence/` is **not touched by this mode at all**.

**Rationale**: A Scheduled Date repeat is a statement about the calendar — total, and you can name every occurrence it will ever produce. A Completed Date repeat is a statement about **history**: it names exactly one future occurrence, and only once the previous one has been settled. Ask it for the occurrence after next and there is no answer — not an expensive answer, one that does not exist. So the shape is a chain, one link per cycle, and **the chain already exists in storage**: FR-353 to FR-355 require the resolutions and SC-308 renders them on past days. The next due date is therefore a fact the schema already states, and storing it again creates the only thing that can go wrong here — two records of one fact with no third record to adjudicate between them.

Deriving makes four requirements structural rather than remembered:

- **FR-344's refusal is the `cycle_prev` foreign key.** Undo is `delete from family.task_resolutions where id = $1` and nothing else: the cursor retreats because the tail retreats, the withdrawn occurrence stops existing because the row that scheduled it is gone, and the original returns to unresolved because absence *is* unresolved. Deleting a link that has a child raises `23503` — no read-then-write, no window between check and delete, and therefore no race, where a naive `select … then delete` loses to a concurrent completion of the next cycle by a hair and strands a real completion on the far side of a hole.
- **FR-370's single claim is the unique index** (R308), with no lock.
- **FR-332's "an edit never destroys a resolution"** is what derivation *does*. The open occurrence has **no stored identity** — a row keyed to it would make it resolved — so every mid-cycle edit moves it for free, with no write and nothing to strand: changing the delay from two weeks to three days re-derives the date on the next read (and correctly makes it late if that lands in the past); changing `renew_until` is one comparison; converting Scheduled Date → Completed Date makes the chain's head "the latest existing resolution", so the chore's next appearance is `lastDone + delay`, which is exactly what a parent switching modes is asking for and comes free; converting the other way leaves the rows in place with `cycle_prev` inert, kept and not surfaced where the new rule produces no such occurrence.
- **"Immediately" falls out** as `renew_after_amount = 0` with no special case anywhere.

The month-clamp is the **opposite** answer to the one the same question gets in rule mode, where `BYMONTHDAY=31` simply produces nothing in a 30-day month (R303). Both are right: a rule may legitimately be silent in a month; a cursor must always land somewhere or the chore is lost.

**The one real objection, and its answer**: the row that decides what is due today may be arbitrarily old — a chore on "after 6 months" was last resolved outside every window the board fetches — so a client-side look-back cannot see it, and PostgREST cannot express a per-group `limit 1`. That is why the tail is a view rather than a wider fetch. The honest ceiling, stated the way R206 states its own: an anti-join over the completion history of the household's completed-date chores, tens of rows per chore per year — right at one household's scale and wrong for a multi-tenant product. A materialised tail arrives additively if it ever costs anything measurable, and the chain stays the source of truth either way.

**Three consequences worth writing down before QA finds them**: (1) a completed-date chore contributes at most one occurrence to a column on any day, *except* on the day an "Immediately" cycle is completed, where it contributes two — the one just ticked and the one it scheduled, `0/1 → 1/2`; both are genuine occurrences under FR-305's denominator rule, the ratio still moves upward, and any other reading would require hiding a live, completable card. (2) A resolution changes what is on *future* days, unlike Phase 2's expansion which is pure in the stored rows — so Phase 1's bare `invalidateQueries(familyKeys.all)` is now **load-bearing** rather than merely convenient, and narrowing it later would break this mode first. (3) The **due-date field seeds only the head**: editing it after the chain has run does nothing, so the form labels it as the first due date once resolutions exist rather than silently accepting an edit with no effect.

**Alternatives rejected**: **`next_due_date` materialised on the task row** (`data.md` §B2, and the schema lane's D303) — on the wrong table under FR-324, where a chore assigned to Ana and Ben has two independent cycles and one column means Ben's Thursday moves Ana's due date: a demonstrable bug, not a preference. Relocated to the assignee row it is merely dangerous: eight write paths must maintain it (complete, undo, skip, unskip, delay edit, due-date edit, assignee added, mode conversion), a drift is **silent and undetectable** because nothing else in the schema states the same fact for it to disagree with, it has no home for the up-for-grabs chain (which has no assignee row), and undo cannot restore the previous value without reading the chain — so the chain read exists anyway and the column buys nothing on the one path it was meant to serve. It is exactly the derived-column contract R206 refused for the calendar, reintroduced for a read that is one indexed anti-join. It returns as an additive optimisation, never as the source of truth. **Materialised next-occurrence rows** (what the reference plausibly does server-side) — forbidden by the spec's Key Entities, and its distinctive failure is the worst kind: a half-applied write leaves a real-looking pending chore that no rule and no resolution accounts for and nothing can tell from a genuine one. **Rewriting `tasks.starts_on` on each completion** (the cheapest thing that could work) — destroys the seed, so nothing can answer "when was this first due" and a later conversion to Scheduled Date anchors on a mutated date; makes `updated_at`/`updated_by` lie about a change no parent made, polluting the audit trail FR-354 exists to keep honest; and undo must restore the prior value, which is only knowable from the chain. **A rule-shaped encoding** (a `renewal` FREQ, or an RRULE with a moving anchor) — no rule string expresses "a fortnight after it was last actually done", so the expander would have to take resolution history as an argument, pushing history into the stateless `recurrence/` module the fallow boundary seals for exactly this reason; the failure is architectural and permanent. **A look-back window instead of the view** — a window is a guess, an old tail sits just outside a generous one, the derivation silently falls back to the seed, and the chore **reappears as though it had never been done**: a wrong board produced by an optimisation, with no error to notice. Fetching whole chains instead is exact but unbounded — a daily "Immediately" chore is ~365 rows per assignee per year, fetched on every render, to read one of them.

> and in `data-model.md`'s "What the database enforces, and what the action does".

**Decision (superseded)**: `020_task_functions.sql` still ships `family.resolve_task_occurrence(…)` and `family.unresolve_task_occurrence(…)` — `security definer`, `search_path = ''`, revoked from `public, anon, authenticated`, `execute` to `service_role` only, following 015's discipline that **the action computes every value and the function applies them in one transaction**. But under R308 and R309 they carry far less than the schema lane drafted: **no cursor advance** (there is no cursor column; the INSERT of the resolution *is* the advance) and **no `for update` lock as the claim arbiter** (the unique index is). What remains is exactly two statements that must not half-complete:

- `resolve`: INSERT the resolution row, then UPDATE the streak checkpoint on `task_assignees` when the action supplied one. `23505` from the index is mapped to FR-393's "already resolved" and, for a claim, to FR-370's refusal — the function's exception handler reads the winner's `category_id` and returns it in the exception's `DETAIL`, so the ordinary double-tap and the contested claim share one code path and one message shape.
- `unresolve`: DELETE the row — where the `cycle_prev` FK raises `23503` for FR-344 — then UPDATE the recomputed checkpoint.

**Rationale**: The streak counter is *derived state stored beside its source* (FR-371 makes it stored, not derived — see R311). Having spent R309 refusing a derived column for the cursor on drift grounds, storing one for the streak is only safe if it moves in the **same transaction** as the resolutions it describes; FR-374's "so the badge always describes the stored resolutions" is an "always", and a resolution committed without its checkpoint leaves that false for an unbounded interval. This clears Phase 2's RPC bar by a smaller margin than 015 did, and that is said rather than glossed: a stale badge is a wrong *number*, not a wrong board. It ships as a function because the second statement is unconditional on the first, the cost is one function per verb, and the alternative asks the operator to accept "it self-heals on the next tick" for a number a child is watching.

**Two things fall out for free, and both should be stated in the plan rather than discovered**: FR-344's refusal **reads onto unskip** — FR-361 defines unskip as the same removal, a skip advances the cycle exactly as a completion does (FR-362), so the hazard is identical and the foreign key does not care which status it protects; no new code. And on a completed-date chore, a **"delete this occurrence"** — recorded as a skip under FR-364 — therefore **advances** the chore rather than removing it: a parent who deletes this fortnight's bathroom clean gets the next one in a fortnight. That is the only coherent reading (the alternative is a chore a single delete kills forever), and the confirmation copy says it. "All future" on the same chore is `renew_until = openDate − 1`, which suppresses the open occurrence and every one after it; there is no rule to truncate and no per-occurrence overrides to re-home, so the completed-date path needs **no analogue of `split_event_series`** — and because FR-331 bans per-occurrence overrides, neither does the rule-mode path.

**A residual, recorded** (and now the accepted residual of the shipped design, not of a function): the checkpoint is written from a value the action computed *before* the write, so two devices resolving **different slots of the same routine for the same person in the same second** can have the second write a value computed one slot behind — as can a crash between the resolution insert and the checkpoint update. The badge self-heals on the next resolution or undo of that routine, because FR-374 already requires a recompute there. The alternatives are worse at this scale: recomputing inside the function puts rule logic in SQL, against 015's discipline; deriving the badge at read time is R311's rejected option.

**Alternatives rejected**: **Zero functions** — one INSERT plus a best-effort streak UPDATE, self-healing on the next write; the honest runner-up, rejected on FR-374's "always". **The schema lane's locked, cursor-carrying pair** — the lock existed to arbitrate a claim the index now arbitrates, and the cursor update has no column to write. **A third function for the Profile deletion** — its half-state retains an unreachable task rather than destroying one, so it is recorded as R307's residual instead, with the function named as the fix if the plan ever disagrees. **Pushing streak recomputation into SQL** — rule logic in a function, duplicating the TypeScript rule the badge must agree with.

## R311 — Habit streaks: a stored checkpoint pair on the join row

**Decision**: `streak_count integer` + `streak_through date` on `family.task_assignees`, written by the resolve action as the **second statement** after the resolution insert (R310 as superseded — there is no RPC) from one pure rule module, `lib/family/tasks/streaks.ts` (`nextStreak(previous, dayOutcome)`). The client **reads** the counter — embedded with the task rows, so a streak change arrives on the same invalidation and needs no extra cache key — and never derives one. FR-373's rules are the module's whole content: advance by one for a day on which **every** occurrence of that routine for that person was completed, leave unchanged for a day on which any was skipped and none left unresolved, reset to zero once a day ends with an occurrence unresolved.

**Rationale**: FR-371 settles stored-vs-derived in the spec's own words ("that the count is stored rather than derived is ours") and FR-324 settles per-assignee. The date is **not optional**: FR-373 resets the streak when a day **ends** unresolved, and nobody writes anything on the day a streak breaks — a bare counter cannot know it has broken. Deriving at read time would need every day's resolutions back to the last break: an unbounded backward window, growing exactly for the households doing best, and reaching far outside the board's 28-day tail (a 300-day streak costs 300 rows the board otherwise never fetches). Storing it also makes SC-312 — thirty days containing one skipped day and one unresolved day, and an un-tick moving the count back by exactly one — a test of one pure function rather than of a query window.

**Alternatives rejected**: Client derivation over a widened resolutions window (above, and it would put a second streak implementation beside the one the badge must agree with). A counter with no date (cannot express a break caused by the passage of time). The streak on `tasks` (FR-324 gives each assignee their own). A materialised view (the recompute-on-undo path is a rule, not an aggregate).

## R312 — The seventeen Task Box templates ship in the migration, behind `family.seed_task_box()`

**Decision**: `family.task_box_items` holds **exactly** FR-377's field set — `summary`, `emoji`, `routine`, and the reserved `reward_points` — plus household, attribution and timestamps, and nothing else: no description, date, repeat or assignment. ## R314 — The read: definitions unwindowed, resolutions windowed by the anchored week, a disjoint carry tail

**Decision**: The board is **four RLS-governed cached reads**, none of them per-day, plus one lazy fifth.

| # | Query | Key | Window |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `fetchTasks` — every `family.tasks` row for the household, with `task_assignees` (and its streak pair) embedded, explicit column lists in `rows.ts` | `familyKeys.tasks(hid)` | **none** |
| 2 | `fetchTaskResolutions` — resolutions whose `occurrence_date` falls in the anchored **week** containing the displayed day, plus every `occurrence_date is null` row | `familyKeys.taskWeek(hid, weekStartISO)` | one week |
| 3 | `fetchTaskCarryForward` — resolutions in `[today − 28, weekStart(today) − 1]` (one day wider than the render bound, R316), `enabled` **only while the displayed day is today** | `familyKeys.taskCarry(hid, todayISO)` | the FR-357 tail, minus what (2) already holds |
| 4 | `fetchTaskCursors` — `family.task_cursors`, the tail of every Completed Date chain (R309) | `familyKeys.taskCursors(hid)` | **none** |
| 5 | `fetchTaskBox` — the templates, `enabled` only while the Task Box sheet is open | `familyKeys.taskBox(hid)` | none |

**The cursor tail is its own query, not an embed.** An earlier draft of this entry said "`family.task_cursors` embedded" in (1); that is not implementable. PostgREST resource embedding needs a declared foreign-key relationship, and `task_cursors` is a **view** over `task_resolutions` with no relationship to `tasks` — the embed would silently return nothing rather than erroring, and the Completed Date mode would be missing from the board with no failure to notice. It is a separate cached read under its own key, seeded as `initialData` like the other three.

Alongside these, one **narrow, id-keyed read that is not part of the board**: `fetchCategoryTaskCounts` under `familyKeys.categoryTaskCounts(hid, categoryId)`, which the Profile delete dialog fetches for FR-391's two counts (`contracts/server-actions.md`, "Amendment to a Phase 1 surface"). It sits beside Phase 2's `categoryEventCount` and is not read by the board at all.

Every key sits under the `["family"]` prefix, so Phase 1's bare `invalidateQueries({ queryKey: familyKeys.all })` — used by both `useFamilyRealtime` and `withActor`'s success path `[code]` — sweeps all five with **zero changes to either mechanism**, exactly as R207 arranged for the week. `staleTime` 30 s and default `gcTime`, mirroring the shipped hook shape. `app/family/(app)/tasks/page.tsx` becomes a server component that fetches (1), (2), (3) and (4) with the server client under RLS and seeds each as `initialData` for **its own key only**, the `calendar/page.tsx` pattern verbatim `[code]` — the cursor read included, because without it the wall tablet's first paint would be missing every Completed Date chore until the client's own fetch landed, which is the mode this whole design exists to serve. Neighbour weeks are prefetched by a `prefetchTaskWeek` shaped like the shipped `prefetchWeek`.

**Rationale**: The unit of a read is the unit of the thing read. R207's "cache the anchored week" is right for events because the event read *is* windowed by the week; the task read is not. Task **definitions** do not depend on the displayed day at all, so `data.md` B8's `familyKeys.taskDay(hid, dateISO)` — **superseded** — would refetch byte-identical rows on every Previous/Next tap and duplicate the same dozens of rows across up to 29 cache entries. And the definitions genuinely **cannot** be windowed by date: an anytime chore has none (FR-328), a completed-date chore's only occurrence is derived from a chain (FR-343, R309), a routine is a rule, and a late chore due three weeks ago must appear on today's board (FR-356). Any due-date window is *wrong*, not merely suboptimal — R206's argument for always fetching every series row, arriving at the same place for the same reason at the same scale. Resolutions are windowed because they are the one thing that grows without bound (roughly thirty rows a day at three profiles), and the week is the right quantum for three reasons: stepping day by day inside a week costs **zero** fetches; the week boundary is warmed by the same ±7 neighbour prefetch Phase 2 already ships; and it is the shape the deferred Week view (FR-385) will want, so nothing has to be re-keyed when it lands. The carry tail is a separate, **disjoint** entry rather than a widened window because FR-357 puts a carried-forward occurrence on today's board and on its own day and nowhere between (US3-3) — a pinned past or future day needs none of it — and ending it at `weekStart(today) − 1` means no resolution row is fetched twice. Keying it by `todayISO` is what makes it roll at midnight by itself (R322).

**The tasks fetch is a four-branch OR** mirroring Phase 2's week read, with each branch's predicate implying its partial index's predicate (Phase 2's note, verbatim): every rule row and every cursor row always, every anytime chore always (FR-328 puts them on every day's board), and one-off dated chores — the only kind that accretes — windowed to `[D − 28, D]` on today and to `starts_on = D` on a navigated day.

**Alternatives rejected**: `familyKeys.taskDay` holding definitions and resolutions together (above). One resolutions key covering `[today − 28, today]` for every displayed day (a 29-day window shifting one day per tap, 96 % overlap discarded each time). A widest-covering-entry lookup, reading the day out of whichever cached range contains it (cache assembly, which R207 rejected for slices and which is worse here because the ranges are heterogeneous). Server-side occurrence materialisation (forbidden by the spec's Key Entities; a horizon, a backfill and a second write path that can drift — R206's rejection, unchanged).

## R315 — `expandTaskDay`: one non-bypassable entry point, shared by renderers and actions

**Decision**: `expandTaskDay(tasks, resolutions, cursors, { displayedDate, todayDate, zone })` in **`lib/family/tasks/expand.ts`** — one name and one signature, used verbatim in `plan.md`, `data-model.md` and `contracts/server-actions.md`, because two names is how two implementations get written and the property below is what the Constitution Check rests on. There is no separate `assignees` argument: `task_assignees` (with its streak pair) rides embedded in the `fetchTasks` row shape (R314), so a task's assignees arrive with the task. It is the single entry point that turns fetched rows into `BoardOccurrence[]` — used by every renderer **and** called by the server actions to validate the occurrence a client names, so a stale client can never resolve a phantom (Phase 2's contracts step 3, applied to tasks). Framework-free, pure, and sealed behind a fallow boundary the way `calendar/expand.ts` is. Four generators, all over R304's one walk:

- **Routine** — `ruleDatesIn` × `times_of_day`, giving one separately completable occurrence per slot per matching date (FR-335), each stamped with the slot it was generated for and **never migrating between sections as the clock moves** (FR-336 — a Morning occurrence is still Morning at 22:00).
- **Chore, Scheduled Date** — the same walk anchored on `starts_on`; a chore with no repeat emits exactly its own date.
- **Chore, Anytime** — exactly one **undated** occurrence, present on every displayed day until resolved (FR-328), never late.
- **Chore, Completed Date** — `openOccurrence` from the published tail (R309), exactly one, or none past `renew_until`. Past cycles need no generator: the resolution rows themselves *are* the occurrences on their dates, and they arrive in the ordinary windowed read, so navigating back to a past day (SC-308) needs nothing extra.

One memo layer below it builds the **resolution index**, keyed exactly as the store's uniqueness is keyed — `(taskId, assigneeId ?? "", occurrenceDate ?? "", slot ?? "", cyclePrev ?? "")` — so every occurrence resolves its state in O(1) and "the client and the database agree about what an occurrence is" is a structural fact rather than a convention. Occurrence state is `"unresolved" | "complete" | "skipped"`, and **skipped occurrences stay in the list**: hiding them is the filter layer's job (FR-361) and removing them from the denominator is the counter layer's job (FR-360). Conflating those three is the single easiest way to get SC-309 wrong.

**The one asymmetry**: an **unclaimed up-for-grabs** occurrence is looked up on `(taskId, occurrenceDate, slot)` with `assignee_id` null and **ignoring the credited category**, because the credit is what the resolution *establishes*, not what identifies the occurrence. A `complete` row moves the card into the credited profile's column (FR-367) and into that column's counters (FR-305); a `skipped` row crediting nobody is household-wide (FR-363, FR-368); no row leaves it in the Up for Grabs column. There is no claimed-but-incomplete state anywhere in the spec — FR-367 makes the claim *be* the completion, and FR-369 returns it to nobody on an un-complete.

**The decomposition, stated up front rather than discovered at the gate**: `expandTaskDay` is the phase's most likely breach of `.fallowrc.json`'s absolute ceiling (`maxCyclomatic: 20`, `maxCognitive: 15`), which coverage does not excuse. It is therefore written as a **small dispatch over one generator per task shape** — `routineOccurrences`, `scheduledChoreOccurrences`, `anytimeChoreOccurrences`, `cursorChoreOccurrences`, each pure, each separately table-tested — plus `carryForwardPass` (R316) as its own exported function. The entry point itself is then a `flatMap` over the dispatch and one concatenation: cyclomatic ≤ 5, cognitive ≤ 4, with each generator in the same range. Same discipline in `visibleTaskOccurrences` (R319): **one predicate per filter**, composed by a list, so five switches plus search is a `predicates.every(...)` rather than a six-arm condition — cyclomatic ≤ 4 for the composer, ≤ 3 per predicate. R303 states its budgets the same way for `dateMatches` (3 → 6 cyclomatic, ~4 cognitive).

**Rationale**: One entry point is what stops a renderer and an action disagreeing about what an occurrence is, which at this scale (dozens of tasks, one day, sub-millisecond) costs nothing. Deriving the sub-type rather than storing it keeps FR-325 mechanical, and putting the branchiest new logic in a pure module is what the CRAP gate wants covered.

**Alternatives rejected**: A second expander per task type — three walks to keep in step. Resolving state inside the expander's inner loop with a linear scan — quadratic in a column with a month of tail. A per-task nested map for the index — more allocation, no faster at this scale, and it loses the "same tuple as the unique key" property that makes the design auditable.

## R316 — Late carry-forward: a bounded second pass, one named constant — and FR-357 vs FR-343

**Decision**: Carry-forward is a **second pass inside `expandTaskDay`**, run only when `displayedDate === todayDate`. For each **chore** task (routines never carry forward, FR-338; anytime chores have no deadline to miss, FR-328) it walks the past days through the same `ruleDatesIn`, and for each date × assignee with **no resolution** emits an occurrence placed on today, carrying its `scheduledDate` — its immutable identity and what the card displays (FR-358) — and `isLate: true`.

**The bound is a strict inequality, and this is the one number in the phase that an off-by-one would silently invert.** The predicate is stated once, here, as arithmetic rather than as a range:

```
todayEpochDay − scheduledEpochDay < CARRY_FORWARD_DAYS      // carried onto today
```

so at `CARRY_FORWARD_DAYS = 28`: an occurrence scheduled **27 days ago is carried**, one scheduled **28 days ago is not**, and one scheduled **29 days ago is not** — which is FR-357's own wording ("MUST stop carrying it forward onto today **28 days** after its scheduled date": at day 28 it has stopped). The equivalent closed range is `[today − (CARRY_FORWARD_DAYS − 1), today − 1]`; it is written as the inequality because the range form is what produced a day-28 disagreement between this entry and the quickstart's own fixture, which seeds "Hoover the stairs" at exactly `today − 28` to pin this boundary. `CARRY_FORWARD_DAYS = 28` lives once, in `lib/family/tasks/dates.ts`, and is consumed by **both** the expander's predicate and R314's tail-window derivation, so there is one number and it cannot drift. The **read** window is deliberately one day wider than the render bound — read (3) fetches resolutions over `[today − 28, weekStart(today) − 1]`, because the pass must know that the day-28 occurrence *is* resolved before it declines to carry it, and one extra day of resolution rows costs nothing. One constant, two consumers, and the read's extra day stated rather than left as a discrepancy for a reviewer to find.

**The spec composition this resolves, in the open**: FR-343 says an open Completed Date occurrence past its date is late by the ordinary rule, carries forward under FR-356/357, **and holds the cursor where it is, however late it becomes**. FR-357 says a carried-forward occurrence stops appearing on today **28 days** after its scheduled date and is thereafter visible only on its own day. Composed literally, they delete the chore from the household's life: for a scheduled-date chore the bound is harmless (the rule keeps producing fresh occurrences and the abandoned one is merely archived on its day), but for a completed-date chore the abandoned occurrence *is* the cursor — after day 28 it is on no reachable screen, no next occurrence can ever be scheduled because nothing can resolve it, and the only ways back are twenty-nine taps of **Previous** or an edit. So **this plan exempts the completed-date open occurrence from the 28-day bound**, and records it here rather than making it a silent implementation choice. Mechanically the carry pass skips the bound for an occurrence whose task has `renew_after_amount is not null` — that mode has at most one open occurrence and cannot accumulate. **The exemption must be visible everywhere the bound is**, or an implementer builds the unexempted bound and QA certifies it: it is stated in **`spec.md` itself — Contradiction resolution 11, and in the text of FR-343 and FR-357, which is where an implementer or a reviewer reads the bound** — in `contracts/server-actions.md`'s carry-forward bullet, in `data-model.md`'s "How the board is read" beside `CARRY_FORWARD_DAYS`, on `lib/family/tasks/dates.ts` in the plan's tree, and as a named check in `quickstart.md` ("a Completed Date chore neglected 40 days is still on today's board; a Scheduled Date chore at `today − 28` is not"). Assumption 7's stated reason for the bound is accumulation ("a year of forgotten chores"), and a mode with at most one open occurrence cannot accumulate — it contributes exactly one card, for ever, which is the shape FR-328 already sanctions for an anytime chore.

**Rationale**: The pass has to be pure and shared, because the server actions need the same answer: an action asked to complete "the occurrence of Take out trash scheduled 2026-09-08" on 2026-09-16 must agree that it is still on today's board and not forty days stale. Computing it in SQL would be a second occurrence engine; computing it on the client and *not* sharing it would let a stale tab resolve a phantom. Putting it in the one expander gives one answer to both.

**Alternatives rejected**: **`data.md` B2's `tasks.carry_forward_from` low-water mark** — it exists to bound an unbounded backward scan, and FR-357 bounds it for free with a number the spec states, argues for and invites argument about; the column would be a second bound behind the first, with a maintenance obligation on every resolution path including undo and skip, and `data.md`'s own §D5 concedes it "degenerates" when it is not kept up. If the schema keeps it, it is an index hint and the client will not read it. **Auto-advancing a neglected completed-date cursor at day 28** — fabricates a resolution nobody made and a `resolved_on` nobody chose. **Leaving the bound in place for completed-date chores** — a chore can then be lost by inattention, which is the failure the mode exists to prevent. **A server-maintained "open occurrences" table** — materialised occurrences, forbidden. **A per-render `today` recomputation inside components** — the same walk, uncached, in a layer with no test seam.

## R317 — The memo chain: the counters branch *above* every display filter

**Decision**: `useBoardOccurrences` is the Tasks analogue of `useWeekOccurrences` — one data path from the cached reads to drawn cards, as memo layers that invalidate independently:

```
useTasks (household key) ─┐
useTaskResolutions (week) ─┼─→ resolutionIndex          ── memo
useTaskCarry (today, enabled) ─┘        │
                                        ▼
                    expandTaskDay(...) → BoardOccurrence[]      ── memo   (R315 + R316)
                                        │
                 ┌──────────────────────┴───────────────────────┐
                 ▼                                              ▼
   columnCountersOf(occurrences)                 visibleTaskOccurrences(occurrences,
   → ring + "n of m" per column                     hiddenIds, filters, query)     ── memo
   → Up for Grabs count                                         │
   → per-routine progress          ── memo                      ▼
                                                 boardColumnsOf(visible, layout, order)
                                                 → columns × four sections          ── memo
```

**The counters branch off the unfiltered list.** FR-384 ("filters never move the counters"), FR-386's same promise for search, US4-1, US4-13 and SC-310's "none of the numbers move when any filter is toggled" then hold as a **property of the graph**, not as an `if` somebody remembers. Toggling a filter, typing in the search box or collapsing a time-of-day section re-runs one memo and cannot reach the counters at all.

**Rationale**: This is R206's lesson applied to a different shape. In the calendar the layer split bought performance (a filter toggle must not re-expand the week); here it buys **correctness of a stated requirement**, which is stronger. Four requirements plus a success criterion all say the same thing, which is exactly the sign that it should be structural. The performance property comes along free: expansion re-runs only when data, day or zone changes.

**Alternatives rejected**: Filtering first and computing counters from a second, separately-derived list — two derivations of "the day's occurrences" that can disagree, which is how a board ends up reading 3/10 beside seven visible cards. Counting inside the column components — no test seam, and the rule would then live in four places (profile column, Up for Grabs column, routine card, and the Tasks Progress display Phase 5 inherits from FR-305).

## R318 — Counters: one pure function, fixed once for three surfaces

**Decision**: `lib/family/tasks/counters.ts`, pure, three exports. `columnCountersOf(occurrences, profileId)` → `{ complete, total }`, where **total** is that Profile's occurrences on the displayed day — routines in each of their slots, chores due that day, undated anytime chores, chores carried forward late, and any up-for-grabs occurrence this Profile has claimed (which joins that column's total and completed count at the moment of the claim and leaves the Up for Grabs count) — **less every skipped occurrence** (FR-305, FR-360; US4-5's 3/10 → 3/9), and **complete** is those resolved complete. The ring fraction is `complete / total`; `0 / 0` renders the empty ring an empty column still gets (FR-316). `upForGrabsCountOf(occurrences)` counts unclaimed, unresolved occurrences only — the column carries a count and no avatar and no ring, because it belongs to nobody (FR-308). `routineProgressOf(occurrences, taskId, profileId)` is that routine's complete-of-total **on the displayed day** (FR-312, Assumption 17), same skip rule. Its unit test is written directly from SC-310's checklist, with the assertion that the numbers are identical before and after every filter is toggled.

**Rationale**: FR-305's denominator is the phase's most-referenced rule: the column ring, the count, the deferred whole-list emoji rain's "every" (Assumption 21), the calendar's Tasks Progress display and Phase 5's home screen all read it, and Assumption 8 fixes it "here once for all three". So it must be one function with one test, not a rule re-implemented per surface.

**Alternatives rejected**: Counting on the server and returning a tally — a second implementation of the denominator, which would have to re-derive occurrences anyway. Deriving the ring from the filtered list — directly contradicts FR-384.

## R319 — Five filters, two per-device stores, search as a sixth predicate

**Decision**: The rule is lib — `visibleTaskOccurrences(occurrences, hiddenIds, filters, query)` in `lib/family/tasks/visibility.ts`, the exact analogue of `calendar/visibility.ts`: pure, its own memo layer, display-only by construction, returning the input array itself when nothing is filtered so an untouched board keeps its memo identity. Storage splits in two, deliberately. **The per-Profile toggle rides the shipped store unchanged** — `useDeviceVisibility` already holds generic *category* ids under `family:hidden-categories:v1`, already prunes deleted ids, already degrades to in-memory with `persistent: false` when `localStorage` is unavailable `[code]` — so a Profile hidden on this phone is hidden on both tabs, which is the honest meaning of one device preference and one **Show all**; no key bump, no fork, existing preferences survive (R212's finding, reused). **The four task switches are a new store of the same shape**: `useTaskFilters`, module-level state + `useSyncExternalStore` + try/catch + a `persistent` flag, under `family:task-filters:v1`, holding `{ completed, late, skipped, upForGrabs }`, defaulting **completed on, late on, up-for-grabs on, skipped OFF** (FR-361 shows skipped occurrences *only* when their filter is on). **Search (FR-386) is not a store**: it is component state, threaded into the same visibility call as a sixth predicate — title or description, case-insensitive, trimmed, across every column including Up for Grabs.

**Rationale**: The existing store's value is a `Set<string>` with prune-against-known-ids semantics; four booleans are a different type with no pruning, and widening the key would orphan every shipped device's preference. Two stores, one sheet, one **Show all** that clears both is the smallest honest shape. Keeping the *rule* in lib rather than in the sheet is what let the calendar's filter be unit-tested as a truth table, and the same applies here — five switches, a search string and three occurrence states is a combination table, not a widget. It also gives FR-321's optional description a second reason to exist: it is one of the two fields the search matches.

**Alternatives rejected**: One widened store holding ids and flags (orphans shipped preferences and mixes two lifetimes — a hidden Profile is durable, a search string dies with the view). Filters in the URL (the wall tablet has no address bar, and it would make a per-device preference shareable, which FR-384 forbids). Server-persisted filters (FR-384 says the choice never leaves the device).

## R320 — Column layout: measured, wrapping on tall-and-narrow, paging on a phone

**Decision**: The board **measures itself**, reusing Phase 2's *pattern* and not its module: `useBoardGeometry` runs a `ResizeObserver` over the mounted board with hidden token probes (so the column width resolves against the live `--fam-u` rung rather than a hard-coded number) and feeds one **pure** decision function `boardLayoutOf({ viewportWidth, viewportHeight, boardWidth, referenceColumnWidth, columnCount }) → { perRow, mode: "grid" | "pager" }`, with three rules that differ from the calendar's on purpose:

- **No seven-column special case and no three-column floor.** Assumption 25 is explicit that the calendar's floor does not transfer, because a Tasks column is a *person*, not a day. The floor is **one**.
- **Wrap, don't page, when the viewport is tall and narrow** (FR-395): when fewer columns fit than exist and the viewport is portrait with room for a second row, the columns lay out as a CSS grid of `perRow` tracks over auto rows — which reproduces the reference's photographed 2×2 portrait arrangement as a *consequence* of the fit rule rather than as a separate layout.
- **One full-width column and a swipe on a phone** (FR-396), Up for Grabs first.

Each column body is its own `overflow-y: auto` region and the board is `overflow-x: hidden`, so twenty tasks are reachable by scrolling *that column* and the page never scrolls sideways (FR-394, SC-315) — `AppShell`'s `<main>` already sets `overflow-x-hidden` `[code]`. The phone pager reuses `WeekPager`'s **already-exported pure helpers** — `swipeAxisOf`, `swipeStepOf`, `travelOf` `[code]` — which move to `lib/family/swipe.ts` with their tests, with `WeekPager` and the new `ColumnPager` both binding to them. The *tiling* half is genuinely different and is not shared: tasks step one column with no overlap, no week and no cross-week continuation, so `sliceStarts` has no analogue here.

**Rationale**: FR-394 says decide by measuring, not by a fixed count, and Phase 2 already solved measurement-with-token-probes in a jsdom-safe form (`geometryOf` returns `null` when every rect is zero, and the pure `columnCountFor` is what the unit suite drives) `[code]`. Copying that seam means the branchy part is a table-tested pure function — the four viewports of the visual brief become its unit table: 1920×1080 → 4 across, 1180×820 landscape → 4 across, 820×1180 portrait → 3 across wrapped, 390×844 → 1 paged — and the DOM plumbing is verified by running the app, which is where constitution §II draws the line anyway. Moving the swipe helpers to lib is a small refactor of shipped code and the right one: a second hand-written axis-lock/threshold pair is exactly what `fallow:dupes` (minTokens 50) exists to catch, and the answer to a dupes finding is the code changing, never the gate.

**Alternatives rejected**: Forking `useGridGeometry` — its `GridMetrics`/`LayoutMetrics` are hour-grid concepts (px per minute, gutter, title line height) with no meaning here; sharing the hook would mean carrying five fields that are always zero. CSS `auto-fit`/`minmax` alone — it can pick a count but cannot express "page instead of wrapping when landscape", and it gives the pager no state to clamp. Fixed breakpoints — FR-394 forbids a fixed count in as many words. A second hand-written pager beside `WeekPager` — would trip the duplication gate.

## R321 — Reorder: a pure list reducer over the shipped fractional index

**Decision**: Column reorder (FR-309, parent-only) and routine reorder within one section for one Profile (FR-310) are **list reorders**, not canvas drags: they have no geometry, only a target index. Both go through `lib/family/tasks/reorder.ts` (`{ items, fromIndex, toIndex } → { id, sortOrder }`), which computes the new position with the shipped `ordering.ts` (`sortOrderBetween`, `needsRebalance`, `rebalance`) `[code]` — **one row written per drop**, never a renumber. The gesture is press-and-hold plus move, which is what the reference specifies for both `[V](36846381293979, 49738702477723)`; the keyboard path moves through the same reducer with `aria-live` announcements (FR-397). **Chores are not reorderable by anyone** (FR-311, Contradiction 1); their order is a fixed rule in `lib/family/tasks/layout.ts` — late first by original due date, then timed by time, then all-day, then undated anytime, ties by creation order.

**Rationale**: One write, no geometry, roughly sixty lines with the numeric part already shipped and tested. `lib/family/drag-state.ts` is **not** reused: its reducer is a distance-slop machine built for FR-253's explicit prohibition on a timed hold — the opposite gesture — and its states carry grid geometry. Widening it would put a hold timer inside the machine FR-253 forbids one in.

**Alternatives rejected**: `@dnd-kit/sortable` — a dependency surface for a one-row write with no geometry, whose sortable strategy assumes uniform rectangles the wrapped-or-paged board does not have, and R214's finding stands that the installed line is documented legacy and its successor pre-1.0. Reusing `drag-state.ts` — above. Renumbering the whole list on drop — N writes where one will do.

## R322 — The day anchor and the time-of-day window: both derived from the shipped clock

**Decision**: `useDayAnchor` holds `{ kind: "today" } | { kind: "pinned"; date }` and derives the displayed date, while `kind === "today"`, from Phase 1's shared clock store `useNow()` converted to the household zone by `localDateOf` `[code]`. Previous/Next pin an absolute date one day away; **Today** sets `kind: "today"` (FR-303). `useSectionToggles` holds the *automatic* time-of-day window as a derivation of the same clock (`wallMinutesOf` against FR-306's boundaries — midnight–noon Morning, noon–18:00 Afternoon, 18:00–midnight Evening) plus a **per-column override map**, which is **discarded whenever the derived window changes** — which is how Assumption 10's "a manual change holds until the next automatic boundary, then the automatic selection re-asserts" happens with no timer at all: the boundary is a derived value changing, not an event to schedule. Chores starts on and composes with any time of day; the four are independent switches and any combination, including none, is legal (FR-307). The override map is component state, not persisted: it expires at the next boundary by definition. **No new timer anywhere in this phase.**

**Rationale**: R210's argument transfers whole — `useNow` already publishes at minute resolution, already re-reads the clock on first subscribe after idle, already cleans up after itself, and a second `setInterval` would drift against it. The two-state anchor makes FR-315 a property of the type rather than an `if`: nothing a pinned day renders derives from `now`, so midnight cannot touch it, while the `today` state rolls the board over by derivation — the displayed date advances, the carry key (`todayISO`) changes so the tail refetches, `expandTaskDay` re-runs, yesterday's unresolved chores appear marked late with the date they were due, and every counter is the new day's (US1-10, US3-14, SC-314). A form open over the board keeps what was typed, because its state is its own component's and nothing above it unmounts. It is a **new hook, not a reuse of `useWeekAnchor`**: that hook's state is a week start plus a slice index tiled by `sliceStarts` and its arrows step seven days `[code]`; forcing tasks through it would mean passing `columns: 1` and letting a seven-slice tiling stand in for days — a lie that would then have to be maintained on both sides. What *is* shared is real: `localDateOf`, `addDays`, `diffDays`, `weekStartOf` and `wallMinutesOf` from `calendar/dates.ts`, already in the `family-calendar-core` zone components may import `[code]`.

**Alternatives rejected**: Generalising `useWeekAnchor` to an N-day anchor serving both (one hook with two tilings, two arrow semantics and two Today meanings, to save forty lines). An effect that watches the clock and imperatively advances state (the fragile shape R210 named). A `setTimeout` to the next time-of-day boundary (a third clock, and it fires wrong across a household-zone DST change the device does not have). Persisting the section overrides (they expire at a boundary they were about to lose anyway). One board-wide toggle set (contradicts FR-306's wording and makes a parent unable to expand one child's evening without expanding everyone's).

## R323 — Writes: `withActor`, pessimistic, one refusal path — and the first record-dependent permission

**Decision**: Every verb — complete, un-complete, skip, unskip, claim, create, edit, delete, reorder, template management — goes through the shipped interceptor unchanged: `withActor(() => taskAction(payload))`. It produces the punch-in **at the moment of the tap** when nobody is punched in (FR-350, US1-3), retries once on `NO_ACTOR`, extends the idle expiry on success, and invalidates `familyKeys.all` `[code]`, so all five task keys refresh with **no new machinery**. **No optimistic write and no pending overlay**: FR-393 is blunt, and unlike a dragged block there is nothing that would visibly snap back — the circle shows a busy state for one sub-second round trip and then paints from the refetch. R208's posture with even less machinery than the calendar needed. **The payload asserts nothing about identity**: the acting Profile comes from the signed punch-in cookie server-side, never from the client (FR-387); the assignee in the payload is part of the *occurrence key*, and `creditProfileId` is sent **only** for an up-for-grabs claim, where FR-368 requires one and FR-351 decides who may name whom. **One refusal path**: the control is always rendered and always tappable — FR-350 says the gate is the server, "rather than by hiding controls" — so a member tapping another's card gets `FORBIDDEN` with a per-call message naming whose task it is and that a parent may do it (FR-351); a lost race gets `CONFLICT` naming the credited Profile (FR-370); a vanished task gets `NOT_FOUND` and the details sheet closes rather than recreating it; offline gets `UNAVAILABLE` from `callAction`'s existing catch (FR-393). **`ActionError` needs no new member** — `ActionResult` already carries a per-call `message` `[code]`.

**Two contract changes to shipped Phase 1 modules, and the second is a guard.** `lib/family/guards.ts` gains `requireVerifiedActor()`, extracted from `requireParent()`. Today `requireActor()` trusts the **cookie's** claims and only `requireParent()` re-reads the profile row before deciding `[code: guards.ts:87, 124]`. FR-351 is the first rule that needs the **fresh database role** on a path that is *not* parent-only: a resolution verb must let a parent resolve anyone's occurrence and a member only their own. Built on `requireActor` alone, a parent demoted on another device would keep that power until the actor cookie expired — the shipped guarantee `requireParent` exists to give, silently dropped on this phase's one privileged non-parent-only path. So the row-reading half of `requireParent` becomes `requireVerifiedActor()` (re-reads the actor's profile, clears the cookie and throws `NO_ACTOR` if it has vanished or is no longer a profile, returns the actor carrying the **database** role), and `requireParent()` is refactored onto it, throwing `FORBIDDEN` when that role is not `parent`. No behaviour of `requireParent` changes; nothing else in Phase 1 or Phase 2 is touched. The proof is a policies-tier test: **a parent demoted to member on another device, holding a still-valid actor cookie, is refused when it resolves another Profile's occurrence** — and the same actor is still refused `createTask`, which is the shipped `requireParent` guarantee unchanged.

**The other contract change**: `lib/family/permissions.ts` is a pure binary `can(actor, op, ctx)` today, whose only non-role input is `householdHasParent` `[code]`. Phase 3 needs a **target-aware** decision — FR-351 is the first rule in this app whose answer depends on *which record* is touched (a member may resolve only occurrences assigned to themselves and claim up-for-grabs only for themselves; a parent may do any of it for anyone). `PermissionContext` gains the target, the module gains the task operations, and it stays pure and unit-tested. Assumption 3 names this as work rather than inheritance and the plan treats it that way. `can()` is used **only for affordance** — which actions the details sheet offers — never as the gate.

**Rationale**: The whole point of Phase 1's interceptor was that later phases add verbs and not plumbing; this phase adds nine verbs and no plumbing. Pessimism is not a performance choice — SC-301's budget is fifteen seconds for a punch-in plus three taps, against one sub-second action — it is FR-393's letter, and it removes the entire class of "the board says done and the database does not" that a family would act on.

**Alternatives rejected**: Optimistic `setQueryData` on the resolution index — buys nothing perceptible, and the streak counter moves server-side on the same write, so a hand-patched cache would be wrong in one more place than it was right in one. Client-side pre-refusal for snappiness — a stale role would refuse what the server would allow, and FR-350 rules out hiding controls as the mechanism. Queueing offline ticks — forbidden by FR-393.

Client-side the four tables extend `useFamilyRealtime`'s `TABLES` list **unfiltered**, and every notice funnels into the existing `invalidateQueries(familyKeys.all)`; no payload is ever rendered.

Four shipped surfaces are amended in place — each listed as work, per the spec's own Dependencies section:

- **`WeekPager`** (`app/family/(app)/calendar/components/WeekPager.tsx` — 002's task list called it `SlicePager` and it shipped as `WeekPager`; the helpers are its lines 80 / 93 / 99, with their tests in `__tests__/WeekPager.test.tsx`) — binds to `lib/family/swipe.ts` instead of owning the three pure helpers (R320).

The Tasks page registers "Add Task" with the shipped FAB registry while mounted (`useRegisterFabAction`) `[code]`; the Task Box is reached from that same create control (FR-376).

**Rationale**: R209's reasoning, unchanged and now load-bearing a second time — with default replica identity a DELETE payload carries primary keys only, never `household_id`, so a filtered subscription silently never fires on deletes, and **this phase deletes on every un-complete and every unskip** (FR-355, FR-361). Unfiltered is safe because the payload is treated purely as "something changed, re-read": content is never used, the re-read goes through the RLS-governed queries, and the household is this project's only tenant. Four more tables on one channel at one household's write rate is a handful of small queries a day; narrowing to a task-specific key prefix is a one-line change if it is ever measured to matter.

**Alternatives rejected**: Filtered subscriptions (silently miss deletes). Mapping notices to specific keys (client bookkeeping about which day contains which task, against a payload that cannot be trusted for content). Promoting replica identity to carry `household_id` (more schema to serve a filter a single-tenant project does not need).
